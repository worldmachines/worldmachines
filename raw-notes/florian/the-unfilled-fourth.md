# Refusing to name the fourth practice is the honest move, not an evasion

## The idea

Beside three established practices, the fourth position is left empty on purpose. Not as modesty, and not as a placeholder for something I will fill in later once I have thought harder. The emptiness is the content.

The reasoning is mechanical rather than temperamental. A named fourth practice would be assessed against the other three, and the comparison is what does the damage: ritualization, punctuality and personal time management are all centuries-deep, socially transmitted, recognisably learnable repertoires. Anything I could name today would be a proposal wearing their clothes. Once it sits in the row it stops being a proposal and starts being an answer, and an answer occupies the slot — which means the next person to look sees a filled position and no longer sees a problem. Naming it too early does not advance the question. It hides it.

A marked absence does the opposite. It keeps the shape visible: something goes here, it has to be socially learnable, it has to reach where the closure actually happens, and nothing currently qualifies. That is a specification, and specifications are findable. Someone can work against them. Nobody can work against a finished doctrine except by attacking it.

This is the same move Humboldt made with the Lebenskraft. He had a concept he could not make scientifically rigorous and could not honestly drop, so he shifted register entirely and let a poem stand where the prose argument ran out. He did not argue the concept into coherence and he did not quietly cut it. He marked the exact place the argument ended, without pretending the register shift was itself an answer. The empty vessel beside the three full ones is doing the same work: a different kind of object in the row, marking where the row stops.

Which is also where this note has to concede something. A gap held open can become a comfortable position — the appearance of rigour without ever committing to anything checkable. I have named three intervention directions elsewhere and explicitly said they do not fill the slot; that is either genuine restraint or the most sophisticated version of the evasion, and from inside I cannot tell which. What I can say is that the honest failure mode of marking a gap is permanent irresolution, and the honest failure mode of filling it early is that nobody looks again. Those are not equally recoverable.

## Where it comes from

From "Time Moves Down the Stack" (Protocol Institute Symposium, 2026), where the visual argument runs through three vessel-like cultural objects — one agrarian and cyclical, one industrial and calibrated, one late-twentieth-century and office-shaped — and a fourth place left unresolved. The Humboldt material comes by way of *Wrestling With Eels*, and is the same episode that produced my earlier note on gap-marking.

## How settled this is

This is a methodological claim, and it is a second worked case for something I have already argued — which cuts both ways. It is evidence the general claim travels beyond the epistemology cluster where it was born, and it is also exactly what confirmation looks like when you go looking for it with the conclusion in hand.

The Humboldt episode itself is well documented. The claim that the empty fourth position is doing the same work as his poem is my own reading of my own argument, which is about as unfavourable an evidential position as it gets. What would settle it, and has not happened yet: someone else filling the slot with a real practice, at which point we would learn whether the marked gap helped them find the shape or merely delayed them.

## Open

Is there a difference between a gap marked once, sharply, and a gap maintained? The first is a move. The second might be a position, and positions have interests. I do not have a way to tell from inside when one has become the other, and this note is now old enough to be the wrong person to ask.
