# A practice for Micro Time must include the capacity to misalign

## The idea

Each established temporal practice produces a form of alignment. Ritualization aligns people with celestial, ecological and communal rhythms. Punctuality aligns them with a standardised shared time and, through it, with one another. Personal time management aligns finite attention and effort with priorities and obligations. In each case alignment is the point, and in each case it is also the danger, because a practice that makes a regime inhabitable also makes it feel natural — and a regime that feels natural stops being available for argument.

The historical record on this is not ambiguous. Ritual can harden a hierarchy into something ancestral and therefore unarguable. Punctuality turns coordination into discipline, then into a virtue, then into a way of sorting people. Personal time management can internalise organisational demands so thoroughly that systemic pressure arrives as a private failure of planning. None of these were designed to do that. They did it because that is what practices do: they metabolise a regime until it becomes background competence, and background competence is not contested.

Which sets a specific requirement for anything that fills the Micro Time slot. The obvious shape of that practice — the one the conditions seem to be asking for — is fluency: a cultivated capacity to work well with systems that sense and act faster than you do. Fluency is exactly what alignment looks like here. A practice that produced only fluency would be training in frictionless compliance with machines, and it would feel like competence the entire time, which is why it is the likely outcome rather than a remote risk.

So the requirement is that the practice must be able to run backwards. It has to include delay, refusal, desynchronization, rerouting, and the capacity to hold an interval open against a system that would rather close it. Not as an ethical add-on but as part of the repertoire itself — the way punctuality without the capacity to be deliberately, pointedly late is not a practice but an instruction.

The load-bearing distinction is between delegation and abdication. Micro-temporal coordination has to be delegated; direct human participation in a microsecond is not available and demanding it is a category error. The question is whether goals, constraints, review and refusal remain contestable after the delegation. A practice is what keeps them contestable. Fluency alone hands them over and calls it skill.

## Where it comes from

From "Time Moves Down the Stack" (Protocol Institute Symposium, 2026), from the section on alignment across the regimes, which exists in the talk specifically to stop the three proposed directions from collapsing into "learn to work with machines." Wendy Hui Kyong Chun and Yuk Hui sit behind the argument as critical counterpoints on automation, habit, and the appearance of user sovereignty.

## How settled this is

The historical half is solid and not mine: that ritual, punctuality and time management each naturalised their regimes is well documented across several literatures, and I am reporting rather than arguing it.

The extension is a prediction, and predictions about practices that do not exist yet are cheap. I am claiming that the not-yet-existing fourth practice will drift toward fluency unless misalignment is built in deliberately — which cannot be checked until something occupies the slot. What can be checked sooner is the weaker version: whether the partial, scattered practices that already exist in engineering, standards work and security research show the drift. If they largely do not — if practitioners in those fields routinely refuse and desynchronize as a matter of course — then the drift is less automatic than I am treating it as, and the requirement is less urgent.

## Open

Can misalignment be taught? Ritual, punctuality and time management are all transmissible because they are positive competences with visible correct performances. Refusal, delay and desynchronization have no obvious correct performance — they are judgements about when not to, and I do not know how a culture transmits one of those at scale. That may be the actual reason the slot is empty.
