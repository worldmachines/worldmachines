# Frances A. Yates, *Giordano Bruno and the Hermetic Tradition* (1964)

## The question

[[frances-yates]] set out to translate Bruno's *Cena de le ceneri* as the work of a bold Copernican. Doubts about what the *Supper* debate was really about turned [[giordano-bruno]] into the centre of her studies, with "some major clue" missing. Kristeller on Ficino's *Corpus Hermeticum*, Garin on Hermetic strands in Renaissance thought and Walker on the *prisca theologia* and on spiritual and demonic magic supplied the clue, though none of them had linked Bruno to Hermetism. Her insight is that Bruno's magic belongs with his philosophy, as part of a Hermetic philosophy. She presents this as a reframing of known material ("my former Bruno studies fell into place"), not a new fact. The book "sets out to do only what its title states": it is not a monograph, and the art of memory and Lullism, which she names as the other two strands of Bruno's mind, are left for separate books ([[yates-giordano-bruno-preface]]).

## Throughlines

**A founding corpus misdated.** The Renaissance return to a golden age of magic rested on "a radical error in dating". The *Corpus Hermeticum* and *Asclepius*, written c. A.D. 100-300, were taken for the work of an Egyptian priest older than Moses and Plato. Yates dates the "reign" of Hermes Trismegistus exactly, from Ficino's translation (ordered by [[cosimo-de-medici]] in 1463, before Plato) to Isaac Casaubon's exposure in 1614. See [[casaubon-dating-yates]].

**The Renaissance Magus.** From the Egyptian Genesis's divine Man and the *Asclepius*'s "magnum miraculum", Ficino builds a cautious natural magic of spiritus and talismans. Pico caps it with practical Cabala, Agrippa systematises it, and Bruno takes it to its extreme. The outcast conjuror becomes a pious operator. See [[hermetic-magus-yates]].

**Bruno as an "Egyptian".** Yates's Bruno drops the Christian interpretation of the Hermetica on which the pious versions rested. He preaches a return of the magical religion of the *Asclepius*, and his Copernicanism is a Hermetic hieroglyph of that return, not a piece of astronomy. She rejects the legend of the martyr for science while crediting his moral stand for the dignity of man, liberty and toleration. See the Yates sections of [[giordano-bruno]].

**Magic, religion and reform.** Because the Magi claimed piety, magic was a religious issue. Its critics (Pedro Garcia, Wier, Erastus, Del Rio) were religious, and so were its irenic uses: Du Plessis Mornay, Rosseli and Patrizi turned to a Christian Hermetism without magic to rise above the wars of religion ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch09-against-magic]], [[yates-giordano-bruno-ch10-religious-hermetism]]). Bruno and the Rosicrucians pinned a "general reformation" on a monarch and saw the hope broken by events. Campanella turned in succession to Spain, the papacy and France ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch19-return-to-italy]], [[yates-giordano-bruno-ch20-campanella-3]], [[yates-giordano-bruno-ch21-after-dating-2]]).

**The will to operate.** Yates's most ambitious and most hedged thesis is that the Magus's historical function was to change the will. It became dignified and religious to operate on the world, and that change fed the seventeenth-century turn to nature even though magic's procedures were not science. See [[will-to-operate-yates]].

**Survival underground.** After Casaubon, Hermetism loses its dominant place and sinks into esoteric channels: Fludd, the Rosicrucians, Kircher's Jesuit Egyptology and, very tentatively, Freemasonry. The Cambridge Platonists keep Hermetic residues while accepting the dating ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch21-after-dating-1]], [[yates-giordano-bruno-ch21-after-dating-2]], [[yates-giordano-bruno-ch21-after-dating-3]]).

## In World Machines terms, with the notes' hedges

Yates is a historian of ideas, not a machine theorist, and every rhyme below is the notes' own reading, not hers.

- **Misdated foundation, philological correction.** A founding text-corpus carried an era's authority for about 150 years. Its correction arrived as a scholarly critique that worked slowly and was "one of the factors, and an important one", not the sole cause. This matches the shape of [[legibility-as-precondition-not-cause]] and bears on the origins of the [[modernity-machine]], though Yates frames it as intellectual history.
- **A backward-looking model of progress.** The Renaissance took progress to be "revival, rebirth, renaissance of antiquity", and Bruno's reform restores rather than advances. The Hermetic Lament and Bruno's "great year" are cyclical, returning-golden-age readings that sit loosely beside the Dawn, Day and Dusk cycle of [[world-machines]]. The differences are that the Hermetic cycle is theological and moral, with divine intervention, while WMP's cycles are infrastructural.
- **Legibility inverted.** Adocentyn, Ficino's "figure of the world", Bruno's memory systems and Campanella's painted walls make the cosmos readable and operable. The notes stress that this is magical and astral, not the administrative [[Legibility]] of [[legibility-as-machine-core]]. See [[talismanic-city-yates]].
- **Decay into survivals.** Hermetism's passage from dominant outlook to secret societies loosely echoes the [[decay-sequence]].
- **Mechanism's cost.** The magician draws the world into himself and the scientist externalises it. Yates suggests that Descartes's dualism was a reaction against Hermetic internalisation, and that this is why the problem of mind fell behind the problem of matter ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch22-fludd-controversies-2]]).
