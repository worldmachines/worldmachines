# Civilization and Capitalism, 15th–18th Century, Vol. III: The Perspective of the World (Fernand Braudel)

*The Perspective of the World* is the third and last volume of [[fernand-braudel]]'s *Civilization and Capitalism* (English edition translated by Siân Reynolds). The first two volumes (Volume I is [[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v1]]; Volume II is *The Wheels of Commerce*) built an analytic model of material life, the market economy and capitalism. Volume III is the chronological history that tests it. The Foreword calls it a gamble. Having built a model in the first two volumes, Braudel "abandons himself to the march of time" and lets chronological narrative confirm or invalidate that model, because history is unusual among the human sciences in offering itself as both explanation and means of verification ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-foreword]]). The subject narrows to the economic history of the world between the fifteenth and eighteenth centuries, though Braudel insists that "economic" can never be cut away from politics, culture and society: "in history, everything is connected" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-foreword]]).

## World time and the world-economy

The volume's first concept is **world time**, the exceptional scale on which certain regions and activities are drawn into a shared, world-spanning rhythm while other populated areas stay outside it. Braudel's illustration is India. Draw four lines along its coasts and inland trade routes, and only the edges of that quadrilateral kept pace with global rhythms. The interior, like other "blank spaces" (even parts of Britain during industrialization), stayed a "zone of silence and undisturbed ignorance." World time is a superstructure, a "crowning achievement" fed by forces beneath it, and it never accounts for the whole of human existence ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-foreword]]).

The volume's central spatial category is the **world-economy** (*économie-monde*). A world-economy is not "the world economy" of all trading humanity. It is "a fragment of the world, an economically autonomous section of the planet able to provide for most of its own needs." Every world-economy obeys three rules: its frontiers shift only slowly, it has a single dominant city at its centre and cities take turns in that role, and it is organized into a hierarchy of core, intermediate zone and periphery ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-world-economies-ground-rules]]). Braudel names Immanuel Wallerstein's *The Modern World-System* as built on the same hierarchy and gives it a qualified yes. His two main revisions are that the European world-economy was born in thirteenth-century Italy, not the sixteenth century, and that the economic frame must not crowd out the political and cultural orders ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-an-order-among-other-orders]]). See [[braudel-world-economy]].

## Method

- **Layered time.** Braudel inventories the economic cycles named by the 1970s: Kitchin (three or four years), Juglar (six to eight), Labrousse's intercycle, Kuznets (about twenty) and Kondratieff (half a century or more). Above them sits the secular trend, "the longest cycle of all" and "very little studied." He rejects Keynes's "in the long run we shall all be dead" as banal: "we all live in both the long-term and the short-term" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-divisions-of-time]]). Four secular turning-points (1350, 1650, 1817 and 1973–4) serve as vantage points for narrating which centre dominated ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-divisions-of-time]]). In the industrial-revolution chapter he maps Kuznets's split between what makes growth possible and how it actually happens onto his own longue durée and conjoncture ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch06-growth-and-division-of-labour]]).
- **Prices as evidence of a system.** Grain prices rising and falling almost in unison from Muscovy to Spanish America, and in Macao and India with a twenty-year lag, are "the most convincing evidence of the coherence of a world-economy penetrated by monetary exchange" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-divisions-of-time]]).
- **Orders of magnitude.** Braudel defends retrospective national accounting as "orders of magnitude," accepting margins of error of 10–20% for modern aggregates and nearer 30% for historical ones. He tests Bairoch's wage multiplier against Venice, Orleans and Vauban's France, and uses ratios (debt to GNP, budget to GNP, patrimony to income) where direct figures fail ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-weights-and-measures]]). He uses the same approach for American treasure ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-spanish-portuguese-america]]). He also warns that statistics are "a contribution but not a solution," as the 1976 Mathias–O'Brien comparison of England and France shows ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-london-great-britain-national-debt]]).
- **Against a priori tests.** Toll abolition and price convergence both fail as tests of a national market. Carrière's figures put the Rhône tolls at barely 0.35% of traffic value ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-elements-and-compounds]]).
- **Structure over great men.** Charles V's imperial project could not decide the fate of a world-economy already present in Antwerp, Lisbon, Seville and Genoa ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-an-order-among-other-orders]]). No single man, Thomas Mun included, saved the pound ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-england-island-and-pound]]). England's economy "was not moving towards any goal, rather it encountered one" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch06-long-distance-trade-inland-transport]]).
- **A confessed Eurocentrism.** Chapter 5 admits it judges the rest of the world "from a misleadingly privileged position," and asks whether Europe's industrial revolution was possible "without this constant assistance" from elsewhere ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-americas-spaces-and-slavery]]).

## The chapter arc

**Foreword.** The gamble on chronology, world time and its zones of silence, and a case against economism ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-foreword]]).

**1. Divisions of space and time.** The three rules of a world-economy and the marble-block image of inert economic mass moved by a few well-placed levers ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-world-economies-ground-rules]]). The world-economy as one order among the political, social and cultural orders, and the qualified endorsement of Wallerstein ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-an-order-among-other-orders]]). The taxonomy of cycles and the four secular vantage points ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-divisions-of-time]]).

**2. City-centred economies: Venice and before.** Europe's first world-economy forms in the eleventh to thirteenth centuries as a bipolar system: an industrial North culminating in Bruges, a mercantile South in Italy, and the Hansa organizing the Baltic ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-first-world-economy-bruges-hansa]]). The Italian maritime cities rise, the Champagne fairs act as a hinge between the two poles, and France ends up encircled by a "circuit of capitalism and of modernity" that passes around her territory ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-italian-cities-champagne-fairs]]). Venice wins against Genoa at Chioggia (1378–81) and builds a deliberately captive world-economy ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-venice]]). Portugal opens the pepper route but stays peripheral, and Antwerp, a city that never sought the role, inherits Venice's crown ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-portugal-and-antwerp]]). The "age of the Genoese" (1557–1627) is financial supremacy without a fleet ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-age-of-the-genoese]]).

**3. City-centred economies: Amsterdam.** Dutch primacy begins at home in agriculture, urbanization, toleration, fisheries and the cheap fluyt ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-united-provinces-at-home]]). The same monopolistic method is applied first to Europe's Baltic and Iberian trades, then to Asia through the V.O.C. ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-traders-to-europe-and-the-world]]). The V.O.C. succeeds in Asia and the West India Company fails in Brazil ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-voc-asia-and-america]]). A formally weak state turns out to be an effective one, with regressive taxes and commerce acting as raison d'état ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-dutch-state-taxes-business]]). The entrepôt generates a credit machine ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-amsterdam-entrepot-and-credit]]). Subordinate economies from the Baltic to the East Indies are exploited by attraction rather than administration ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-away-from-amsterdam]]). Amsterdam withdraws in the eighteenth century through three credit crises and the Batavian revolution ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-decline-of-amsterdam]]).

**4. National markets.** The national market is a nested hierarchy of isolate, market town, *pays* and province, and the city-centred economy is set against the territorial one ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-elements-and-compounds]]). A methodological apologia for national accounting follows ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-weights-and-measures]]). France is Europe's first territorial state, yet her size keeps her from economic primacy and leaves her split between Paris and Lyon ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-france-paris-and-lyon]]). Her margins are rich, her interior poor, and the margins colonize the interior ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-france-differential-history]]). England becomes an island by accident and holds the pound stable for three centuries ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-england-island-and-pound]]). London builds the national market and an unequal Great Britain, and the national debt is her "artificial wealth" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-london-great-britain-national-debt]]).

**5. For and against Europe: the rest of the world.** The Americas are Europe's "doing," built on a succession of bonded-labour regimes ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-americas-spaces-and-slavery]]). Spanish and Portuguese America is marginalized, exploited more by smugglers than by Spain, and neither feudal nor capitalist ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-spanish-portuguese-america]]). The English colonies turn against Europe, and contemporaries already see the United States displacing England as England had displaced Holland ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-colonies-against-europe]]). Black Africa is "collaborator as well as victim" in the slave trade ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-black-africa]]). Muscovy is a world-economy apart, with a state-built serfdom and a lively peasant market ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-russia-state-serfdom-market]]). Russia conquers Siberia while becoming a raw-material periphery of Europe ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-russia-siberia-european-intrusion]]). The Ottoman Empire is a rival world-economy whose decline was deferred until the nineteenth century ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-turkish-empire]]). The Far East is three world-economies pivoting on India, invaded by a fourth that conquered largely with Asian crews, brokers and sepoys ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-far-east-european-assault]]). India is treated through its villages, its artisans and the Mogul "astonishing machine, rigid yet fragile" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-india-villages-artisans-mogul]]). The chapter closes with India's decline, the Malacca–Batavia–Calcutta succession and Bairoch's finding of near-parity with Europe around 1800 ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-india-decline-malacca-new-centres]]).

**6. The industrial revolution and growth.** The revolution is a dialectic of long accumulation and short explosion, tested against the Third World and against earlier "revolutions that came to nothing" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch06-relevant-comparisons]]). Braudel works sector by sector while saying the method falsifies a synchronic change ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch06-britain-agriculture-demography-technology-cotton]]). The commercial revolution turns England toward a distant periphery, and inland transport precedes demand ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch06-long-distance-trade-inland-transport]]). Growth predates the industrial revolution, and the division of labour produces the specialist industrialist ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch06-growth-and-division-of-labour]]). Finance capitalism is a recurring late stage, and progress was bought against real wages ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch06-finance-conjuncture-living-standards]]).

**Conclusion.** The model is "launched" onto the twentieth century to see whether it is still seaworthy. Its essential message is capitalism as distinct from the market economy ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-conclusion-past-and-present]]).

## Throughlines

- **Centres take turns.** "Dominant cities did not dominate for ever; they replaced each other" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-world-economies-ground-rules]]). The sequence Venice, Antwerp, Genoa, Amsterdam, London (with New York forecast) organizes Chapters 2 to 4. Each centre's turn toward finance is read as a "sign of autumn" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-amsterdam-entrepot-and-credit]]). See [[braudel-succession-of-dominant-centres]].
- **The same hierarchy at every scale.** The province is "a national economy, indeed a world-economy, in miniature" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-elements-and-compounds]]). France splits into rich margins and a poor interior that the margins colonize ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-france-differential-history]]). Ireland is a periphery inside the state that is becoming a world-economy's core ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-london-great-britain-national-debt]]). Industrial England inverts its own inherited core and periphery ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch06-growth-and-division-of-labour]]). Siberia is a periphery within the periphery ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-russia-siberia-european-intrusion]]).
- **Capitalism sits on coexisting modes of production.** Wage labour, sharecropping, serfdom and slavery coexist across zones rather than following one another in time, and capitalism enters peripheries through colluding local "entry points" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-an-order-among-other-orders]]). Following Eric Williams and Marx, slavery is constitutive of European capitalism, and Braudel uses the word genocide for what happened to American Indians and enslaved Africans ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-americas-spaces-and-slavery]]).
- **City versus territorial state.** Bücher's *Stadtwirtschaft* and *Territorialwirtschaft* explain why city-states led early and territorial states won later ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-elements-and-compounds]]). Amsterdam is "the last city-empire," and after it the role passes to London "with the backing of the entire English economy" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-united-provinces-at-home]]).
- **Credit is the real machinery.** The Genoese asientos turned Philip II's irregular revenue into regular income ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-age-of-the-genoese]]). Bills of exchange in eighteenth-century Amsterdam reached four to fifteen times the specie in circulation ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-amsterdam-entrepot-and-credit]]). The pound's fixed value was the precondition of "easy credit" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-england-island-and-pound]]). The punctually serviced national debt was "the major reason for the British victory" over France ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-london-great-britain-national-debt]]).
- **Silent builds, belated recognition.** The Genoese hegemony, the "Dutch miracle," the Hansa and the industrial revolution each assembled quietly and were named late ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-age-of-the-genoese]], [[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-united-provinces-at-home]], [[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-first-world-economy-bruges-hansa]], [[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch06-long-distance-trade-inland-transport]]). See [[braudel-visible-autumn-hidden-dawn]].
- **Progress at the expense of the many.** Real wages fell furthest when elite indicators looked healthiest ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-divisions-of-time]]). "Under the conditions of the ancien regime, progress was always at the expense of the living standards of the workers," and "two generations were sacrificed to the creation of an industrial base" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch06-finance-conjuncture-living-standards]]). Bairoch's estimates put Europe and the rest of the world near parity around 1800, and Braudel calls mechanization "a weapon of domination" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-india-decline-malacca-new-centres]]).
- **No technology in itself.** "A new device merely opens a door; it does not compel anyone to enter" (Lynn White) ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch06-relevant-comparisons]]). Coke smelting waited forty years on relative prices ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch06-britain-agriculture-demography-technology-cotton]]). This continues Volume I's argument (see [[limits-of-the-possible]]).

## Where it meets the World Machines frame

The notes flag many resonances. Each is partial, and the hedges belong to the claim.

- **[[world-machines]].** The succession of centres rhymes with dominant infrastructures succeeding one another. It rhymes "in shape (centre succession, real turbulence at the hinge) rather than in mechanism or scale": Braudel's centres change hands over decades within one continuous European world-economy, not across a thousand-year Dawn–Day–Dusk arc ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-world-economies-ground-rules]]). The Ottoman, Russian and Far Eastern world-economies coexisting beside Europe's faintly echo machines in friction, but they are geographically parallel trade circuits, not temporal regimes ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-turkish-empire]], [[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-russia-state-serfdom-market]]). The Conclusion is a counter-rhyme. Braudel's capitalism "explicitly resists any fixed decay curve," reorganizing rather than dying on schedule ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-conclusion-past-and-present]]).
- **[[gramsci-gap]].** Braudel defines a secular crisis as one coherent system completing its decline while another "is being born amid much hesitation and delay." His register is economic-structural rather than a crisis of legitimacy ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-divisions-of-time]]). The Batavian revolution of 1780–7 is a small-scale legitimacy vacuum resolved by outside force ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-decline-of-amsterdam]]), and 1968 is an old legitimacy cracking on a decade-long timescale ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-conclusion-past-and-present]]).
- **[[history-machine]].** The pattern is quiet, distributed assembly followed by belated naming. It fits the Hansa, the Genoese age, the Dutch "miracle," the pound's survival, the industrial revolution and the "calculators" whose scattered efforts became national accounting ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-first-world-economy-bruges-hansa]], [[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-age-of-the-genoese]], [[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-united-provinces-at-home]], [[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-england-island-and-pound]], [[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch06-relevant-comparisons]], [[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-weights-and-measures]]). The notes mark that the concept was built for paradigms, not financial or commercial hegemonies.
- **[[legibility-as-machine-core]].** The valence is mixed. A single stable unit of account did work that elsewhere needed layers of institutions ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-england-island-and-pound]]). The bill of exchange is "a private, mercantile legibility built by bankers for profit" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-amsterdam-entrepot-and-credit]]). The V.O.C.'s spice monopoly made a resource base legible by force ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-traders-to-europe-and-the-world]]). But Spain's licensing apparatus dissolved into smuggling ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-spanish-portuguese-america]]), the ancien-régime surveys of France went unpublished or unfinished ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-france-differential-history]]), and planned, orderly Batavia lost to disorderly Calcutta ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-india-decline-malacca-new-centres]]). Price reconstruction in Senegambia shows legibility demonstrating African economic agency rather than erasing it ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-black-africa]]). This bears on [[legibility-as-precondition-not-cause]].
- **[[decay-sequence]].** Amsterdam stays "one of the high altars of world capitalism" after it has lost functional leadership ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-decline-of-amsterdam]]). The V.O.C. kept growing in visible apparatus after its profits reached zero around 1724 ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-voc-asia-and-america]]). The Mogul nobles kept up twice-daily visits to confirm the emperor still lived ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-india-villages-artisans-mogul]]). Each case is far smaller than the civilizational infrastructure the concept describes.
- **[[capture-resistance]], [[Conatus]] and [[The Thicket]].** Relevant cases include the Genoese withdrawal of capital from a failing Spain ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-age-of-the-genoese]]), the maroon republic of Palmares ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-americas-spaces-and-slavery]]), colonial smuggling ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-colonies-against-europe]]), the serf-merchant Gratchev hiding his fortune, and Russia's protective distances ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-russia-state-serfdom-market]]). Each note qualifies these as economic, pre-modern or geographic analogues, not the autonomy those concepts were coined for.
- **[[Configurancy]].** Antwerp "woke up one morning to find itself there" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-portugal-and-antwerp]]), and England "encountered" its industrial revolution rather than pursuing it ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch06-long-distance-trade-inland-transport]]). The frame is economic-structural rather than Rao's open-ended agents-and-worlds ontology.
- **[[psychohistory-and-world-machines]].** Writing from inside the 1972–4 crisis, Braudel asks whether it is an ordinary Kondratieff downswing or a reversal of the secular trend, while admitting the cycles' mechanism is a mystery ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch06-finance-conjuncture-living-standards]]). The Conclusion disclaims any strong prediction: "I make no claim to explain the present in the light of history" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-conclusion-past-and-present]]).
