# Revolutionary Spring (Clark)

Christopher Clark's *Revolutionary Spring: Fighting for a New World, 1848–1849* is a Europe-wide history of the revolutions of 1848–49 (see [[christopher-clark]]). Its opening claim is that 1848 was the only truly European revolution. Unlike 1789, 1830, 1870 or 1905/1917, it produced parallel tumults from Portugal to Wallachia and from Norway to Palermo, with a global fringe that reached the French Caribbean, the British imperial periphery, Latin America and Sydney ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-introduction]]). Contemporaries lived it as one European event. Later historians nationalized it into separate stories of failure, and Clark reads that verdict as an artefact of nation-state framing. His counter-case is that the revolutions were a 'particle collision chamber' in which socialism, radicalism, liberalism, nationalism, corporatism and conservatism were tested and changed, and a 'revolution in government' whose effects outlasted the defeat ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-introduction]], [[clark-revolutionary-spring-conclusion]]).

## The argument in brief

- **A cascade, not a set of national stories.** The 'single most striking feature' of 1848 is that revolutions broke out across the continent at once. Clark rejects both the domino picture and the idea of independent events: the revolutions were cognate, rooted in one economic space and kindred political cultures, and synchronized by news ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch04-nouvelles-diverses-february]]). See [[revolutionary-cascade-clark]].
- **Against 'failure'.** Revolutions are 'the sum of many potentially dissonant or even contradictory intentions', so success and failure are the wrong categories. Clark measures effects instead: every political camp learned the techniques of modern politics, and 'failure' was later reminted as success ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-conclusion]]).
- **The social question tore the revolutions apart.** Pauperism, the working poor and the right to work divided the spring coalition. Conservatives, liberals and radicals gave three different diagnoses of inequality ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-introduction]]). How the era saw and tabulated distress mattered as much as the distress itself ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch01-politics-of-description]]). See [[politics-of-description-clark]].
- **Entropy.** The unity of spring gave way within weeks to fissures between liberals and radicals, political and social revolution, town and country, and rival nations ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-vagabond-sovereignty]], [[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-revolution-shuts-itself-down]]). See [[entropy-of-revolution-clark]].
- **Counter-revolution as executor.** The line between revolution and counter-revolution was blurred. Counter-revolutionaries were 'as often as not – in their own eyes – the executors, rather than the gravediggers, of the revolution', and the 1850s order was a reconstruction, not a restoration ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-introduction]], [[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch09-constellations-circulation]]). See [[post-revolutionary-synthesis-clark]].
- **Fear and emotion.** Euphoria, disappointment and above all fear of the lower orders shaped events. Fear was 'part of the revolution rather than an exogenous factor' ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch01-galicia-1846-conclusions]]).

## Structure

**Introduction.** The claim of uniqueness, the nationalization of memory, three phases (spring euphoria; a divisive summer culminating in the June Days; an autumn of counter-revolution plus a second radical wave ending in summer 1849), and the method: assemblies versus clubs, old powers transformed, fear, garrulousness and historical self-awareness ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-introduction]]).

**1. Social questions.** Distress as backdrop, not cause. The 'politics of description' in the statistical and literary enquiries of the 1830s–40s ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch01-politics-of-description]]); pauperism, contested commons and the compound crisis of 1845–7, with divergent state responses from Jerez to Ireland ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch01-precarity-and-crisis]]); the textile revolts of Lyons, Brno, Prague and Silesia ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch01-weavers]]); and the Galician double uprising of 1846, where 'the estate-owner's national dream might turn out to be the peasant's feudal nightmare' ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch01-galicia-1846-conclusions]]). Clark concludes that there was no direct line from hunger to revolution, but social conflict was politically consequential.

**2. Conjectures of order.** A political field of the 1830s–40s that had not yet sorted into parties: radical women's attack on the power of the father ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch02-its-a-mans-world]]); liberalism as a middle path and 'metapolitics', and the divided radicals ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch02-partisans-of-liberty-radicals]]); conservatism as opposition and religion 'unstuck' from ecclesiastical authority ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch02-conservatives-religion]]); nationalism as feeling and elite cultural work ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch02-patriots-and-nations]]); slavery as a root metaphor that avoided the thing itself, and history felt as an irreversible current ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch02-free-and-unfree-places-in-history]]).

**3. Confrontation.** The July Revolution of 1830 as a legal revolution and a 'European media event' ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch03-glorious-days-liberal-revolution]]); the disillusioned social left after 1830 ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch03-unfinished-business-social-revolutionaries]]); conspirators and Mazzini ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch03-clandestinity-national-insurrection]]); Vormärz Germany and the making of [[robert-blum]] ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch03-political-ferment-in-germany]]); Swiss culture wars and Hungarian radicalization ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch03-swiss-culture-wars-hungary]]); the July Monarchy's hollowing and Italian moderatism ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch03-bourgeois-monarchy-moderates-italy]]); Metternich's order and the cracks of 1846–47 ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch03-rock-of-order-cracks-avalanche]]).

**4. Detonations.** Palermo in January ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch04-i-predict-a-riot]]); the wave critique and February in Paris ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch04-nouvelles-diverses-february]]); Vienna and Pest ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch04-we-are-dead-shall-we-be-slaves]]); Berlin and Milan ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch04-soldiers-out-five-days-of-milan]]); and the revolutions that did not happen, whose absence Clark treats as a result of pre-emption and policing ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch04-dogs-that-didnt-bark-end-of-beginning]]).

**5. Regime change.** Streets, cafés and funerals as the medium and ritual of a new order ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch05-revolutionary-space-honour-your-dead]]); provisional governments as a 'hiatus, not a transition' ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch05-establish-a-government]]); parliaments that proved moderate or conservative ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch05-elect-a-parliament]]); constitutions as versatile technologies used to pre-empt, stabilize and bury revolution ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch05-draft-a-constitution]]).

**6. Emancipations.** Abolition in the French empire and 'Black 1848' ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch06-abolitionist-black-1848]]); women present at the barricades and absent from the assemblies ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch06-waving-from-windows]]); Jewish emancipation and anti-Jewish violence ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch06-liberty-and-risk]]); Roma emancipation in Wallachia and the four emancipations compared ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch06-roma-slaves-time-of-emancipation]]). See [[four-emancipations-clark]].

**7. Entropy.** Vagabond sovereignty in Paris, Berlin and Vienna ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-vagabond-sovereignty]]); Hecker's radical breakaway in Baden ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-radical-breakaway]]); the rural 'black box' ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-town-and-country]]); national questions, from Italy's collapsed coalition to Croatia, Posen and Schleswig-Holstein ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-national-questions-1]], [[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-national-questions-2]]); the June Days and the thermodynamic metaphor ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-revolution-shuts-itself-down]]).

**8. Counter-revolution.** Naples as the compact arc from revolution to counter-revolution ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch08-naples-in-summer]]); the Habsburg counter-stroke in Prague, Lombardy and Vienna ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch08-empire-strikes-back-1]], [[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch08-empire-strikes-back-2]]); France and Prussia close down, and Britain's small counter-revolution on Cephalonia ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch08-iron-net-very-small-place]]); the second wave ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch08-the-second-wave]]); geopolitics inside the revolutions ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch08-geopolitics]]); the birth of realism and the dead ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch08-realism-and-the-dead]]).

**9. After 1848.** Exile and dispersal ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch09-present-is-a-foreign-country]]); global reception ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch09-global-1848]]); new political constellations and the age of circulation ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch09-constellations-circulation]]); material progress as legitimacy and the remodelled city ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch09-material-progress-city]]); from censorship to public relations ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch09-censorship-to-public-relations]]).

**Conclusion.** Mimicry of 1789 as 'felt historicity', not emptiness; success and failure rejected; the collision chamber; the post-revolutionary synthesis; liberals and radicals who failed to listen to each other and both missed the countryside; and the present as a rhyme ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-conclusion]]).

## Throughlines

1. **Improvised authority.** 'The revolutionaries of 1848 were not the authors of revolution, but people who had been precipitated into positions of responsibility by the sudden collapse of authority' ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch04-dogs-that-didnt-bark-end-of-beginning]]). 'It was the revolution that made revolutionaries, not the other way around' ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch04-soldiers-out-five-days-of-milan]]). Newspaper offices drew up ministries, and authority was assembled from acclamation, seizure and paper ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch05-establish-a-government]]). Sovereignty then 'flowed unpredictably' through the mobilized population ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-vagabond-sovereignty]]). Wallachia is the counter-type, where a compact elite planned the seizure in advance.
2. **Old regimes that lost confidence first.** In Palermo 'the collapse of the regime's confidence in itself preceded and facilitated the revolution raised against it' ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch04-i-predict-a-riot]]). Guizot's chamber debated a bank law while barricades rose, a 'kind of autopilot' of regimes near extinction ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch04-nouvelles-diverses-february]]). Security states policed conspiracies that did not cause the revolution and were unprepared for mass demonstrations ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch03-rock-of-order-cracks-avalanche]]).
3. **Two revolutions in one.** For liberals the revolution was an accomplished event to stabilize; for radicals a process of social transformation just begun ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-vagabond-sovereignty]]). In Vienna the political revolt of burghers and students and the social revolt of suburban workers ran side by side, and the newly armed guards suppressed the second ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch04-we-are-dead-shall-we-be-slaves]]).
4. **Nation as the most contagious and most divisive force.** Nationalism was 'above all a feeling' before 1848 ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch02-patriots-and-nations]]), transnational in its borrowings ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-national-questions-1]]), and in 1848 a 'phantom' that raised solidarity within nations while corroding it between them ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-national-questions-2]]).
5. **Theatre and felt history.** Costume, staged speech and stage fright drove events ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch04-we-are-dead-shall-we-be-slaves]]); funerals made insurgents into martyrs of a state that did not yet exist ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch05-revolutionary-space-honour-your-dead]]); actors read 1848 against the template of 1789 ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-conclusion]]).
6. **Geopolitics inside the revolution.** The Vienna settlement wove constitutions into the international order, so that the domestic/international distinction 'made very little sense'. Interventions and non-interventions shaped both the opening and the closure ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch08-geopolitics]]).

## World Machines resonances (hedged)

Clark is a historian, not a machine theorist, and the notes carry their resonances as analogies. The closest fit is the pattern of an order breaking down and being reassembled in new form. Several notes reach for [[gramsci-gap]], and each hedges: Clark's hiatus lasts days and weeks, and it closes in a consolidation that strengthens the state rather than in an epochal interregnum ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch05-establish-a-government]], [[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch09-constellations-circulation]]). The social question's statistics and the 1850s turn to administration touch [[legibility-as-machine-core]] and [[legibility-hardens-hierarchy-bayly]]. Clark's statisticians were often reformers, though, and the legibility reading is the notes', not his ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch09-material-progress-city]]). The cascade across many nodes echoes [[history-machine]] only loosely, since Clark insists there was 'no central nervous system' and treats personnel and contingency closely ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch09-global-1848]]). His closing claim that what 'we used to call modernity' is 'now in flux' rhymes with [[modernity-machine]] in Dusk, but it is a historian's impressionistic sketch ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-conclusion]]). Comparable global-synchrony arguments are in [[converging-world-crises-bayly]] and [[bayly-birth-of-the-modern-world]].
