# "The Mass" as Fiction

**Primary source**: John Carey, *The Intellectuals and the Masses* (1992) — see [[carey-intellectuals-and-masses]]

## The Claim

"The 'mass' is, of course, a fiction." Carey's central analytic object is not a population but a word — a term whose function is to strip the majority of people of human status, or at least of whatever qualities the term's users claim as their own distinguishing superiority ([[carey-intellectuals-and-masses-preface]]).

The chapter-length version of the argument is that "the masses do not exist": the mass is a metaphysical abstraction, "the crowd in its metaphysical aspect," onto which intellectuals project attributes precisely *because* it cannot be individually known ([[carey-intellectuals-and-masses-ch1-revolt-of-the-masses]]). The projection is not incidental to the concept; it is the concept.

## The Genealogy

**Augustine.** The word descends from *massa damnata* / *massa perditionis* — the condemned mass of humanity minus the elect few God inexplicably chooses to save. Carey draws the line from this doctrine of the excess, disposable human remainder through to the crematoria, naming "this well-established Christian precedent for disposing of the surplus 'mass' by combustion" ([[carey-intellectuals-and-masses-preface]]).

**Statistical alarm.** Ortega y Gasset's figures — Europe rising from 180 to 460 million between 1800 and 1914 — supply the demographic premise. The mass is first rendered legible as a threatening aggregate, and only then denied any interior life ([[carey-intellectuals-and-masses-ch1-revolt-of-the-masses]]).

**Naming as domination.** Hardy's trajectory is Carey's paradigm case: inarticulate night terror at London's population ("a monster whose body had four million heads and eight million eyes") converting into contemptuous ease once he can fix "these people" in language at the British Museum. Rewriting the mass lets intellectuals "acquire the control over the mass that language gives" ([[carey-intellectuals-and-masses-ch2-rewriting-the-masses]]).

## The Repertoire of Images

Carey's evidence for the category's fictional status is its **instability** — the images are mutually incompatible yet interchangeable in use:

- **Animal / herd**: Nietzsche's rabble; Yeats's swarming birds and fish; Trafford's Oxford Street crowd as "shoaling mackerel on a sunlit afternoon" ([[carey-intellectuals-and-masses-ch7-wells-against-wells]])
- **Disease**: Le Bon's crowd fused with Pasteur's bacterial model of society; Hitler's "Jewish virus"
- **Sub-material**: jelly and slime (Hitler on the Jew); Woolf's imagined lavatory women as "slugs" ([[carey-intellectuals-and-masses-postscript]])
- **Code**: Mass Observation reducing individuals to entries like "M 45 D"
- **The clerk / the suburban**: a datable social fact — the white-collar employment boom and the building boom — converted into a moral and aesthetic verdict on a class ([[carey-intellectuals-and-masses-ch3-suburbs-and-clerks]])
- **Cosmetic forms**: the peasant, the pastoral innocent, the noble prole, the divinized punkah-wallah — flattering images that perform the identical erasure
- **Consumption tokens**: tinned food as a class-marker of soullessness, recurring from Forster, Hamsun, Eliot, Betjeman, Greene, Wells and Orwell through to Warhol's 1962 soup tins

Hitler cycles through the same incompatible set — feminine and irrational, childlike, a nation to be welded by propaganda, noble workers against a despised bourgeoisie, a healthy peasantry, and finally the Jew as sub-material. Carey treats the instability as diagnostic: the concept is a flexible device that intellectuals and demagogues alike use to justify withholding personhood from whomever they need to exclude ([[carey-intellectuals-and-masses-ch9b-hitlers-intellectual-programme]]).

## The "Not Fully Alive" Move

The most consequential operation the category performs is the denial that the mass is alive: Hardy's division of "souls and machines," Eliot's Dante-inflected crowds on London Bridge, Lawrence ranking himself above "the Mexican who drives the wagon for me." If the masses are already dead, their elimination is imaginatively costless. Carey follows this to its terminus: industrialized extermination became thinkable once it was accepted that Jews were a mass, "not fully alive people," making transportation, incineration and the manufacture of fertilizer from ashes seem merely proportionate.

In WMP vocabulary this is the suppression of [[Conatus]] by classification — the individuating striving of actual persons averaged into a type, which is exactly what [[capture-resistance]] names as the thing liveness must refuse. Shan Bullock's line about his clerk is the counter-statement Carey keeps returning to: dissolved into "thousands like him... Twopenny clerks," and yet "He has a soul, this figure that I see in the crowd."

## Why It Is Not Merely a Literary Problem

The category does administrative work. Aggregation is what makes a population manageable *and* eliminable: Carey's postscript follows the logic from demographers' proposed "solutions" (war, plague, famine) and the impulse to blame "irresponsibly procreative Indians and Chinese," through to Bogotá's death squads hunting street children — the point at which the statistic becomes an administrative act. This is where the mass-concept meets [[legibility-as-machine-core]] and [[necropolitics]].

## Ortega's Counter-Definition, and the Images It Still Uses

*The Revolt of the Masses* ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses]]) is the strongest text in the wiki that directly contradicts this page, and the contradiction is kept, not reconciled.

**The counter-claim.** Ortega defines the mass not as a population but as a psychological type present in every class: the man who “feels himself ‘just like everybody’” and is content with it, against the man who “demands more of himself than the rest” ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch01-the-reality-of-the-masses]]). “Mass” applies “not so much because of his multitude as because of his inertia” ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch07-noble-life-and-vulgar-life]]); at its barest, the mass-man is one “satisfied with thinking the first thing he finds in his head” (n. 16, [[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-endnotes]]); and his purest instance is the prestigious specialist scientist ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch12-the-barbarism-of-specialization]]). On this account the category is a standard anyone can fail, not a caste. See [[mass-man]].

**What Carey's instability test would still catch.** The same book supplies its own repertoire of shifting images, which reads as additions to the list above: the “buoys adrift” ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch01-the-reality-of-the-masses]]); the “spoilt child” ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch06-the-dissection-of-the-mass-man-begins]]); the primitive or *Naturmensch* who has “slipped through the wings” onto civilization's stage ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch09-primitivism-and-the-technical]]); Rathenau's “vertical invasion of the barbarians” and “heap after heap of human beings” dumped onto history ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch05-a-statistical-datum]]); the deaf who “will want to listen” ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch07-noble-life-and-vulgar-life]]); schoolchildren gone wild, extended to whole “mass-peoples” ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch14a-who-commands-in-the-world-1-3]]); the parasite, “a mere negation” ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch15-arriving-at-the-real-issue]]). The text also slides between definition and headcount — technicism engendered the mass-man “quantitatively” before “qualitatively” ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch12-the-barbarism-of-specialization]]) — and carries a period-racial aside on “inferior races” ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch11-the-age-of-the-self-satisfied-dandy]]).

**What the two readings share.** Neither Ortega nor Carey treats the mass as a neutral sociological count. For Carey the category is a device for withholding personhood; for Ortega it names a failure of self-demand that any person, of any class, can commit. The disagreement is over whether a psychological definition can be kept clean of the aggregate it was drawn from — and Ortega's own text supplies evidence on both sides.
