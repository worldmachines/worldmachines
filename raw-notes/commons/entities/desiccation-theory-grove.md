# Desiccation Theory (Grove)

**Source**: [[grove-green-imperialism]]. The bare name is broader than Grove's usage. Here it means the claim, as he tracks it from antiquity to the 1860s, that clearing forests reduces rainfall, dries springs and changes a local or regional climate. The Conclusion names it one of the three pillars of early conservationism, alongside islands and gardens ([[grove-green-imperialism-conclusion]]).

## A discontinuous ancestry

- **Theophrastus** linked deforestation to declining rainfall in Greece and Crete. Neither Greece nor Rome acted on it, and it became consequential only after its Renaissance republication in 1483 ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch01-s1-early-conservation]]).
- **Columbus**, via his son Ferdinand, knew “from experience” that clearing the Canaries, Madeira and the Azores had reduced rain and mist. Grove calls this “the first documented post-classical instance in a colonial setting” of the connection ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch01-s2-island-environments-1400-1660]]).
- **Scattered sightings.** Mandelslo's 1647 aside on a cloud-capped wooded mountain supplying fresh water is an observation, not a policy argument ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch01-s4-st-helena-mauritius-insular]]). Evelyn noted that Barbados “every year it becomes more torrid,” and Montserrat's 1702 ghaut ordinance protected streamside vegetation ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch01-s6-ecological-pressures]]). St Helena observers tied tree loss to failing streams and the slaves' yam crop before 1700 ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch03-s1-st-helena-2]]). Deodati's 1696 description of Mauritius's water-retaining “wet forest” is a pre-theoretical noticing, and Grove deflates its originality ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch03-s5-mauritius-importance]]).
- **Against the grain.** Forests were read as “disease-ridden and dangerous” well into the seventeenth century. Woodward's 1708 claim, the first clear published forest–rainfall claim, argued that clearing *improved* the air ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch04-s1-hales-newtonian]]).

## The mechanism: Hales

Woodward had a correlation without a mechanism. Stephen Hales, applying Newtonian ideas of motion and conservation of energy to plant–atmosphere relations in *Vegetable Staticks* (1727), supplied one and reversed the old assumption that forests were unhealthy ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch04-s1-hales-newtonian]]). The theory crossed to France faster than it spread at home, through Buffon's translation and Duhamel du Monceau. By the 1750s theorists proposed that man might control the constitution of the atmosphere by controlling forest cover ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch04-s2-climate-theories-france]]). Grove is explicit that this science “did not lead by itself to an environmental critique.” It needed a tropical administrator fluent in it.

## From theory to law

- **Mauritius.** Poivre's 1763 Lyons speech warned of deforestation-driven rainfall change in tropical colonies ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch04-s2-climate-theories-france]]). Praslin's 1766 instructions made rainfall anxiety an object of French colonial policy, and the 1769 Réglement économique followed ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch05-s4-poivre-origins]], [[grove-green-imperialism-ch05-s5-poivre-programmes]]). Grove stresses that desiccation carried higher stakes in the seasonally arid tropics, where rainfall decline threatened agriculture directly, than in temperate Europe and America, where clearing was widely seen as a good ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch05-s4-poivre-origins]]). By 1781 a Ministère de Marine memoir asked the governor to protect woods “to prevent the drying up of rivers and streams.” Desiccation anxiety had become institutionally self-sustaining ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch05-s10-bernardin]]). In 1805 Matthew Flinders, a captive on the island, recorded that the rains had decreased “owing as it is thought to the hills being stripped of their covering” ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch05-s11-revolution-global-context]]).
- **The Caribbean.** The 1764 Tobago proclamation and 1765 Barbados ordinance reserved forests “to preserve the seasons,” even though Dr George Young, who sited the reserves, privately held the opposite, miasmatic view ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch06-s1-caribbean-forests]]). Soame Jenyns carried Hales's and Duhamel's theory into the Ceded Islands settlement ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch06-s2-soame-jenyns]]). St Vincent's King's Hill Act of 1791 reserved a hill “for the purpose of attracting the clouds and rain” ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch06-s4-anderson-advocacy]]), and Anderson argued that “no crop depends so much” on rain as sugar cane ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch06-s5-anderson-critique]]).
- **St Helena.** In 1794, under pressure from Banks and the Mauritius and St Vincent precedents, the East India Company's directors wrote that “trees have an attractive power on the clouds” and ordered replanting ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch03-s1-st-helena-3]], [[grove-green-imperialism-ch07-s4-famine-calcutta-garden]]). Beatson, reading Humboldt, built a prize scheme, rainfall records and irrigation works ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch07-s5-burchell-beatson]]).

## Islands proved it locally; Humboldt generalised it

Forster's comparison of moist St Helena with barren Ascension in 1775 assembled a vegetation–moisture–soil feedback loop, although he never stated the reverse inference ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch07-s3-forster-st-helena]]). Grove says island-derived theory still lacked “an effectively global dimension” and “rigour and scientific principle” until after the 1820s. Humboldt's Lake Valencia account supplied a continental model, which Grove treats as unsuperseded. Because the East India Company refused Humboldt entry to India, Boussingault's 1837 paper carried the “law” into empire, through translation, colonial learned societies and the newspaper ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch07-s6-humboldtian-diffusion]]).

## India: from timber to climate

The surgeons picked up Boussingault through the *Edinburgh New Philosophical Journal*. Gibson withheld the climatic argument for years out of scientific caution and used it as a “psychological weapon” only in 1846, after Konkani and Parsi testimony gave him regional evidence ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch08-s3-lobbying]]). Despatch No. 21 of 1847 accepted Indian deforestation as “part of a global phenomenon” ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch08-s4-bombay-department]]). Balfour built an explicit chain from tree cover to rainfall to food supply ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch08-s5-balfour]]). By the 1860s the debate was international: Livingstone rebutted it as a purely geophysical matter, and J. Spotswood Wilson's 1858 paper pushed it toward an early, non-canonical anticipation of greenhouse thinking ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch08-s7-wider-perspective]]).

## Always contested

The theory never went unchallenged inside the colonial establishment: Young's miasma view, James Johnson's 1818 warning that wooded mountains breed fevers ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch08-s2-roxburgh-1]]), Cullen and the Dewan of Cochin in the Madras debate ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch08-s5-balfour]]). Grove's point is less that it was true than that it worked. It was the argument that moved governments, and he notes that it also served fiscal and political control over forest-dependent people ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch08-s7-wider-perspective]]); see [[conservation-as-control-grove]] and [[diagnosis-waits-for-crisis-grove]].

## World Machines resonances, with their hedges

One note reads desiccationism as an early model of a natural process becoming something a state might act on, at “the fragile edge” of [[legibility-as-machine-core]] ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch04-s2-climate-theories-france]]). Another reads Woodward's standardized travel instructions as a protocol for making the world's climates comparable, while calling the resonance partial ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch04-s1-hales-newtonian]]). The [[isaac-newton]] link is to Newtonianism's uptake by naturalists, not to Newton's own work. Grove's own synthesis is that Newtonian science and Rousseauist language were allies here: Hales let risk be put in measurable, state-usable terms ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch05-s11-revolution-global-context]]). On the Humboldt question the canon holds two readings: [[wulf-invention-of-nature]] credits [[alexander-von-humboldt]] with the first theory of human-caused climate change, and Grove places him as the universaliser of an older colonial tradition ([[grove-green-imperialism-introduction]]).
