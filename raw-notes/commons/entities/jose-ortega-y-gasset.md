# José Ortega y Gasset (1883–1955)

**Key work in this wiki**: *The Revolt of the Masses* (1930) — see [[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses]]

## Life and Works

The biographical sketch available in this corpus comes from the translator Marciano Guerrero's introduction, and is his account ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-essays-introduction]]): a doctorate from the University of Madrid; training in Germany from 1905 (Leipzig, Berlin, Marburg); a Madrid professorship from 1910; the founding of *Revista de Occidente* in 1923; and exile from 1936 to 1945 during the Spanish Civil War. Guerrero presents him as Spain's preeminent twentieth-century philosopher and lists *Meditations on Quixote*, *Invertebrate Spain*, *The Theme of Our Time*, *The Dehumanization of Art*, *Kant*, *The Revolt of the Masses*, and *The Mission of the University* among his major works.

Ortega's own endnotes to *Revolt* repeatedly cite his earlier essays — *España Invertebrada*, *El Espectador*, “El origen deportivo del Estado,” *Meditaciones del Quijote*, *Las Atlántidas*, *The Dehumanization of Art* — which makes the book look less like a single new work than a synthesis point for two decades of prior writing ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-endnotes]]).

## Style (per Guerrero)

Guerrero praises the prose as transparent rather than “mystical and hermetic,” built from aphorism and metaphor used to “bring to light difficult ideas,” and anchored in Ortega's maxim “In philosophy, clarity is courtesy.” He traces Ortega's thought to three currents — Schopenhauer's pessimism, Nietzsche's superior individual, and Mill's liberalism — a genealogy that is Guerrero's, not Ortega's ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-essays-introduction]]).

## Characteristic Moves

- **History read through a dominant human type.** “What actually decides is our character,” and collectively “the type of men dominant” in a society ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch05-a-statistical-datum]]). The [[mass-man]] is his central instance.
- **“Aristocratic” as ontology, not program.** Society *is* aristocratic by essence — organized around a functioning minority — which he distinguishes from both fashionable “Society” and the State ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch02-the-rising-of-the-historic-level]]).
- **The historian as reverse prophet.** Hegel, Comte, and Nietzsche read the coming mass ascendancy from the “general structure” of their age; only structure, never detail, is foreseeable ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch06-the-dissection-of-the-mass-man-begins]]).
- **Non-determinism.** History is metaphysically open — “reality hesitates, walks up and down” — so the present may be a new organization of humanity or a catastrophe ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch09-primitivism-and-the-technical]]).
- **Idealist theory of rule.** Command rests on public opinion, “the universal law of gravitation in political history”; force only fills the vacuum when opinion is absent ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch14a-who-commands-in-the-world-1-3]]).
- **Anti-essentialism about nations and States.** The State is “the will to do something in common” ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch14c-who-commands-in-the-world-7]]); the nation, reading Renan's “daily plebiscite” forward, is a program willed toward the future ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch14d-who-commands-in-the-world-8-9]]).
- **History as a technique.** Historical knowledge “prevents us committing the ingenuous mistakes of other times”; the past is a “revenant” that can only be digested, not fought ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch10-primitivism-and-history]]).
- **A good thing's success reversing on it.** The move is repeated across science, the State, liberal democracy, and the nation-state — see [[civilization-undone-by-its-own-success]].

## Politics

Ortega writes as a liberal: liberalism is “the right which the majority concedes to minorities” and “the noblest cry that has ever resounded in this planet” ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch08-why-the-masses-intervene-in-everything]]). He condemns Fascism by its form, naming Mussolini's “All for the State” as Statism's contemporary face ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch13-the-greatest-danger-the-state]]), treats Bolshevism and Fascism alike as “false dawns” ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch10-primitivism-and-history]]), sides with an England that sets “limits” to the State ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch13-the-greatest-danger-the-state]]), and ends *Revolt* calling for a united Europe ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch14d-who-commands-in-the-world-8-9]]). Guerrero reads his elitism as meritocratic — an “aristocracy of the mind” — and so compatible with Millian liberalism ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-essays-introduction]]). That reading is contested in this wiki by [[carey-intellectuals-and-masses]] and [[the-mass-as-fiction]].

## Read Against Carey's *Intellectuals and the Masses*

John Carey's *The Intellectuals and the Masses* ([[carey-intellectuals-and-masses]]) reads Ortega as a founding text of intellectual mass-phobia; *The Revolt of the Masses* itself ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses]]) lets that reading be checked. The two readings contradict each other and both stand; what the source adds is detail on where they meet.

- **The statistic is Sombart's, and Ortega uses it as a claim about formation.** The 180-to-460-million figures that Carey treats as the demographic premise of mass-phobia are borrowed from Werner Sombart; Ortega's argument from them is that the population flood outran traditional culture's capacity to *educate* — the newcomers were “instructed” in technique but not formed in its spirit ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch05-a-statistical-datum]]).
- **Ortega explicitly denies the class reading.** Mass and select minority are psychological types found in every class ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch01-the-reality-of-the-masses]]); “mass” is a matter of “inertia,” not “multitude,” and “nobility” etymologically means earned fame, not blood ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch07-noble-life-and-vulgar-life]]); the most prestigious figure of the age, the man of science, is his “prototype of the mass-man” ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch12-the-barbarism-of-specialization]]). Guerrero's introduction presses the same line — an “aristocracy of the mind,” meritocratic rather than inherited ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-essays-introduction]]) — though that is Guerrero's gloss.
- **But headcount and psychology are fused in the text.** Ortega himself says technicism engendered the mass-man first “quantitatively,” through the population leap, and then “qualitatively” ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch12-the-barbarism-of-specialization]]) — which is the slide from aggregate to type that Carey's analysis targets.
- **Politically, Ortega sits nearer the liberal pole than Carey's company of eugenicists.** He calls liberalism “the noblest cry that has ever resounded in this planet” ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch08-why-the-masses-intervene-in-everything]]) and indicts Mussolini's “All for the State” as a movement of mass-men ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch13-the-greatest-danger-the-state]]). That does not cancel Carey's case — the same book contains an aside on “inferior races — the pygmies, for example” that reflects period racial-hierarchy science ([[ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch11-the-age-of-the-self-satisfied-dandy]]) — but it means the Ortega of *Revolt* is an anti-Fascist liberal elitist, not a proto-Fascist one.

See [[mass-man]] for the concept as Ortega builds it, and [[the-mass-as-fiction]] for Carey's side.
