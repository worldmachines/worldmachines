# Entropy of revolution (Clark)

'Entropy' is Clark's title metaphor for the loss of cohesion that followed the unity of spring 1848: the speed with which an 'oceanic immersion in a collective self' gave way to 'anxiety and distrust' ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-vagabond-sovereignty]]). He uses it as an image, with hedges, and pairs it with a warning that the downward arc was not simply a loss of order ([[clark-revolutionary-spring]]).

## The image and its limits

Clark announces the metaphor through images of wasps dispersing and dust motes in a sunbeam, and argues that the apparently random dispersal still shows a pattern ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-vagabond-sovereignty]]). He borrows the vocabulary of Carnot and Clausius (heat, dissipation, entropy) and of Helmholtz's nerve science, while warning that it 'would be vulgar' to treat thermodynamics as a mirror of politics. He prefers a circulatory metaphor because it makes room for blockages and unevenness ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-revolution-shuts-itself-down]]). His closing caution is that waning cohesion was not only 'an entropy intrinsic to the process' but the result of 'new and competing forms of order-making' (liberalism, socialism, nationalism and others) whose futures were both brilliant and terrible.

## The fault lines

1. **Event against process.** For liberals, under the tricolour, the revolution was an accomplished event needing stabilization; for radicals, under the red flag, it was a process of social transformation only just begun ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-vagabond-sovereignty]]). The same split recurs in Vienna, where workers treated the revolution as 'a process, and not as an event' ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch08-empire-strikes-back-1]]), in Naples ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch08-naples-in-summer]]), and in Görgey's and Kossuth's disagreement over Hungary ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch08-geopolitics]]).
2. **Chamber against street.** The parliaments elected in 1848 proved moderate or conservative and treated themselves as the return to order that made insurrection illegitimate. For the left this was a 'blocked artery' ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch05-elect-a-parliament]]). The Frankfurt Assembly's acceptance of the Malmø armistice exposed 'the tension between the street and the chamber' ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-national-questions-2]]).
3. **Republicanism against the social question.** Blanc's Ministry of Progress was shouted down, the National Workshops were closed, and the June Days followed. Clark reads this as the moment when 'republicanism and the commitment to social causes were different and separable things' ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-revolution-shuts-itself-down]]). In Vienna the moderates' wage cuts for public-works labourers ended in a 'muted echo' of the June Days ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch08-empire-strikes-back-1]]).
4. **'After' against 'while'.** In Baden the dispute was over how radically a new administration had to break with the past to count as new: the radicals demanded a purge as a precondition, the liberals kept the old Confederal Assembly as a working holdover. Even the radicals split, as [[robert-blum]] refused Hecker's walkout on procedural grounds ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-radical-breakaway]]).
5. **Nation against nation.** Nationalism was the revolutions' 'phantom', raising elation and solidarity within nations while corroding solidarity between revolutionary movements ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-national-questions-2]]). It also pushed social demands 'down the hierarchy of needs' ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-national-questions-1]]).
6. **Town against country.** Urban leaders lacked knowledge of, and time for, the rural majority, and their fiscal choices (the 45 per cent surtax in France) opened the way for the counter-revolution ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-town-and-country]]).
7. **The middle erodes.** In the second wave, liberals, a 'saturated power' frightened by the June Days and the clubs, drifted toward the forces of order, and elections showed the middle withering ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch08-the-second-wave]]).

## Seeds before 1848

Clark shows the fissure already forming: in the contested thresholds of every liberal commitment (franchise, chambers, free trade) ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch02-partisans-of-liberty-radicals]]); in liberals who saw urban unrest 'both as an opportunity and as a threat' ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch03-political-ferment-in-germany]]); in the Swiss war, where victory was a poor teacher ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch03-rock-of-order-cracks-avalanche]]); and in the stack of unresolved problems hidden by early euphoria ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch04-dogs-that-didnt-bark-end-of-beginning]]). Fear was the accelerant: fear of the lower orders let authorities divide liberals from radicals ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-introduction]]).

## The verdict

In the Conclusion, the mutual denunciation of liberals and radicals is a central tragedy of 1848. Radicals saw who was being left behind; liberals valued the discursive mediation of interests; neither made sense of rural society ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-conclusion]]).

## World Machines resonances (hedged)

Clark's pairing of entropy with competing order-making reads, in the notes, as closer to friction between machines than to decay into nothing ([[clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-revolution-shuts-itself-down]]). That differs from [[entropy-and-assemblage-maintenance]], where entropy is the drift a machine must work against. Clark's is a historian's image for a coalition, not a thermodynamics of institutions. The vagabond sovereignty of spring loosely rhymes with the [[gramsci-gap]], but Clark's interregnum is short and ends in a violent settlement.
