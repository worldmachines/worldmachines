# Pangloss

Dr. Pangloss is Candide's tutor in the castle of Thunder-ten-tronckh, "the oracle of the family", and the voice of the doctrine that [[voltaire-candide]] satirizes. This page follows his career as the reading notes record it. His words are his own, delivered in the narrator's ironic register; the notes read the doctrine's target as Leibniz-style optimism by interpretation, apart from the one line in which he names Leibniz. For the doctrine's logic and how it fares across the book, see [[panglossian-optimism-voltaire]].

## The teacher

He teaches "metaphysico-theologo-cosmolonigology": "things cannot be otherwise than they are", and not merely right but best, so noses exist for spectacles and the baron's castle is "the most magnificent of all castles". The note on these chapters calls the doctrine "a social ratifier": the existing hierarchy is declared the best end. His "experimental philosophy" with a chambermaid, watched from the bushes, leads Cunégonde to think she might be "a sufficing reason for young Candide", which the note reads as a bawdy parody of the principle of sufficient reason. The sage who ran the experiment suffers nothing there; the pupil is exiled ([[voltaire-candide-ch01-02-castle-and-bulgarians]]).

## A ruined body, an extended system

He reappears as a beggar covered with syphilitic sores, traces the disease through a cordelier, a countess, a page and a Jesuit to a companion of Columbus, and defends it as a necessary ingredient of the best of worlds: without it "we should have had neither chocolate nor cochineal". Against the Anabaptist James's account of people who "were not born wolves" yet make cannon, he answers that "private misfortunes are public benefits". The note's point is that he does not revise the system; he extends it ([[voltaire-candide-ch03-04-war-and-pangloss-found]]).

At Lisbon he stops Candide from saving the drowning James by proving the coast was made for that drowning; finds the earthquake's "sufficient reason" in a sulphur train from Lima; lectures a looting sailor on "universal reason"; and, while Candide lies wounded asking for wine and oil, argues probability against demonstration before fetching water. A familiar of the Inquisition catches him in his own system (original sin, free will), and the auto-da-fé hangs him for speaking his mind ([[voltaire-candide-ch05-06-lisbon-earthquake-auto-da-fe]]).

## The absent authority

Believed dead, he goes on governing Candide's speech. Candide wishes for his "most excellent advice", quotes his proof that "the goods of this world are common to all men" against a thieving friar, sets his teaching that all people are by nature equal against the Baron's quarterings, recalls his lecture on centaurs and satyrs when monkeys turn out to be lovers, and at the cauldron asks what Pangloss would say ([[voltaire-candide-ch09-10-inquisitor-and-cadiz]], [[voltaire-candide-ch15-16-baron-killed-and-oreillons]]). "O Pangloss! Pangloss! what joy would this have given you if you had not been hanged" ([[voltaire-candide-ch13-14-buenos-aires-and-paraguay]]). Others judge him: Cunégonde says he "deceived me most cruelly" ([[voltaire-candide-ch07-08-cunegonde-found]]); Martin tells Candide his hanged sage "laughed at you" ([[voltaire-candide-ch22-paris-2]]). Candide also remembers that he admired Homer ([[voltaire-candide-ch25-pococurante]]).

## Resurrection without retraction

He turns up chained to a Turkish galley oar, "the most profound metaphysician in Germany" priced by the captain as rank-bearing property ([[voltaire-candide-ch27-voyage-to-constantinople]]). His story: rain defeated the burning, a novice executioner botched the hanging, a surgeon who began to dissect him fled thinking him a devil, and a cadi later sent him to the galleys for putting a fallen bouquet back in a woman's bosom in a mosque. Asked whether he still thinks all is for the best, he answers: "I am a philosopher, and it would not become me to retract my sentiments, especially since Leibniz could not be wrong", adding pre-established harmony, the plenum and materia subtilis. The note's reading: loyalty to a system, not experience; a comic figure, "not a policy analyst" ([[voltaire-candide-ch28-29-reunion]]).

## On the farm

He cannot get a university post, writes a treatise on the Baron's lack of rights, and brings a dervish his questions on causes and effects, the origin of evil and pre-established harmony, only to be told "Be quiet". He absorbs Candide's "we must cultivate our garden" into his system (Adam was put in Eden to work it) and still recites the chain of catastrophes that led to "preserved citrons and pistachio-nuts". The narrator's verdict on him is the book's last word on the doctrine: he "maintained it, and at the same time didn't believe it all", a system persisting as habit after conviction has gone ([[voltaire-candide-ch30-conclusion-garden]]).
