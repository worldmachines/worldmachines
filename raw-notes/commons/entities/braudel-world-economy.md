# Braudel's World-Economy (économie-monde)

**Source**: [[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3]], where it is the central spatial category of [[fernand-braudel]]'s third volume. It is Braudel's rendering of the German *Weltwirtschaft*.

## Definition

Braudel separates two expressions. "The world economy" is Sismondi's "market of the universe," all of trading humanity. "A world-economy" "only concerns a fragment of the world, an economically autonomous section of the planet able to provide for most of its own needs." He anchors the idea in his own earlier study of the sixteenth-century Mediterranean. There, economic unity imposed from above by Venice and the other Italian cities cut across the Christian, Muslim and Greek frontiers that seemed decisive everywhere else. A world-economy is "a sum of individualized areas ... which it brings together." It is typically the largest coherent economic zone of its period, larger than a state, empire or civilization ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-world-economies-ground-rules]]). Braudel credits Rörig as "the first historian to give the meaning 'world-economy' to the German word Weltwirtschaft" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-first-world-economy-bruges-hansa]]).

## The three rules

1. **Frontiers change only slowly.** World-economies border one another along zones "only worth crossing, economically speaking, in exceptional circumstances," "thick shells, hard to penetrate." These usually follow real barriers: the Sahara, the Atlantic before its crossing, and the roadless, swamp-choked approaches that kept Muscovy outside the European world-economy into the seventeenth century ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-world-economies-ground-rules]]).
2. **One dominant city, and cities take turns.** Each world-economy has an urban "logistic heart" ringed by an archipelago of subordinate towns. "There is only room for one centre at a time in a world-economy," and each hand-off is "a massive historical shift of forces" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-world-economies-ground-rules]]). See [[braudel-succession-of-dominant-centres]].
3. **A hierarchy of zones.** Every world-economy polarizes into "a narrow core, a fairly developed middle zone and a vast periphery." Braudel tests the schema against von Thünen's *Der isolierte Staat* and faults it for leaving out "the very important concept of inequality," which he insists has an actual origin and history. His practical marker of an intermediate zone is a resident colony of foreign merchants: Genoese bankers in Madrid, Dutch merchants in Leipzig, English merchants in Lisbon. The periphery is marked by serfdom or slavery, a barely monetized economy and very low prices. Backward "black holes outside world time" (Breton hinterlands, Alpine valleys, the undrained Fens) also persist inside the core itself ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-world-economies-ground-rules]]).

## World time and the conjuncture

A world-economy's reach is never total. The Foreword's "world time" links certain axes of activity into a shared rhythm and leaves "zones of silence and undisturbed ignorance" elsewhere. Its unreached zones mark its boundary ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-foreword]]). Near-synchronous price movements across huge distances are Braudel's main evidence that such a system exists. He goes further and suggests the world-economy "manufactures" the conjuncture as a "vibrating surface," rather than merely passing on shocks ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-divisions-of-time]]). A shared climate is not a shared experience, though. A conjunctural crisis "does not and cannot have the same impact on the strong and the weak," and usually ends up "making the strong stronger and the weak weaker" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch06-finance-conjuncture-living-standards]]).

## How the hierarchy works

- **Attraction, not administration.** A dominant economy can "exploit subordinate economies, while not soiling its own hands with the less profitable activities." Europe's ring of second-rank powers stayed attached to Amsterdam through capital and credit alone. The "true" periphery (Ceylon, Java, Spanish America, English India) could be held only by force. Braudel even asks whether China failed to become a magnetic world-economy because it lacked "a semi-periphery sufficiently strong to provide energy for the core" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-away-from-amsterdam]]).
- **Inherited inequality.** Braudel rejects Ricardo's comparative advantage as a "pseudo-theorem." The international division of labour was never "a concerted agreement made between equal parties" but "a chain of subordinations." He prefers the "deliberately simple-minded" tautologies "a poor country is poor because it is poor" and "growth breeds growth" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-an-order-among-other-orders]]).
- **Labour regimes by zone.** Wage labour, sharecropping, serfdom and slavery coexist across zones, so moving between zones means moving between labour regimes, not historical periods. Capitalism enters the periphery through colluding local "entry points" such as the Polish landowner selling to Gdańsk or the Brazilian engenho owner tied to Lisbon ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-an-order-among-other-orders]]). Braudel offers a hedged inference from the Baltic cases: grain exported at scale tends toward "refeudalization," while mining or forestry "might predispose to a degree of liberty" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-away-from-amsterdam]]).
- **Marginalization.** Braudel prefers this word to "exploitation" for Latin America: a zone being told what to do by an international division of labour it does not control. Independence changed the master from Iberia to London, not the position ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-spanish-portuguese-america]]). For eighteenth-century England, the periphery becomes "a reserve to be drawn on by the world-economy she dominated" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch06-long-distance-trade-inland-transport]]).
- **Leverage over inert mass.** Braudel's own image for how such thin means (a little silver, credit, high prices at the end of a trading chain, habit) could steer so much is a huge marble block moved with a few levers. Because ordinary economic activity is "trapped, imprisoned, unable to move from the ground," it can be governed from above ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-world-economies-ground-rules]]).

## The same structure at every scale

The pattern repeats inside nations and provinces. The province is "a national economy, indeed a world-economy, in miniature" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-elements-and-compounds]]). France resolves into rich Atlantic ports and eastern border towns flanking a poor interior, which the margins "satellize," as with Toulouse. Braudel deliberately withholds the word "periphery" for France's rich margins because in his vocabulary periphery means backwardness, and the distinction holds only at a given scale ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-france-differential-history]]). Ireland is a periphery within the British Isles, moved through export "cycles" like colonial Brazil ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-london-great-britain-national-debt]]). Industrialization inverts England's old south-east core and north-west periphery, a rare case of periphery overtaking core ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch06-growth-and-division-of-labour]]). Amsterdam stood to the towns of the United Provinces "in the same position as did Venice to those of the Terraferma" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-united-provinces-at-home]]). The V.O.C.'s Asia and Europe are "two unequally laden trays on a scale" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-voc-asia-and-america]]).

## Many world-economies

Europe's was not the only one.

- **Muscovy** is a world-economy in itself, with Moscow as its centre, the Volga as its "capitalist backbone," and Siberia and the steppe as peripheries. Distance is its structural key ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-russia-state-serfdom-market]]). After Peter the Great it also became a raw-material periphery of Europe, closer to semi-periphery than colony ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-russia-siberia-european-intrusion]]).
- **The Ottoman Empire** was "unquestionably from the very beginning a world-economy," with a centre that migrated from Bursa to Aleppo and Alexandria, then Smyrna, then Istanbul ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-turkish-empire]]).
- **The Far East** had three world-economies (Islam, India and China) that could fuse into something close to one, pivoting on India, before Europe forced its way in as a fourth ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-far-east-european-assault]]). This zone was "structured enough to be penetrated with relative ease, but not sufficiently structured to defend itself" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-india-decline-malacca-new-centres]]).
- **East Africa** was a peripheral route of the Indian world-economy long before 1498 ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-black-africa]]).

## Braudel and Wallerstein

Braudel credits the core, middle zone and periphery schema as the basis of Wallerstein's *The Modern World-System* and calls the model "certainly a valid one." It explains why central regions keep surpassing themselves and why "development is the reverse side of underdevelopment" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-world-economies-ground-rules]], [[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-an-order-among-other-orders]]). He presses two revisions. The European world-economy was born in thirteenth-century Italy, not in the sixteenth century with the Habsburg collapse of 1557. And the economic order is one order among the political, social and cultural ones, which "constantly interpenetrate and never permanently subordinate one another" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-an-order-among-other-orders]]). He accepts Wallerstein's placement of Muscovy outside "European Europe" before Peter ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-russia-state-serfdom-market]]). He does not rest on Wallerstein's flat inclusion of America in the sixteenth-century European world-economy and reads America instead as Europe's own "doing" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-americas-spaces-and-slavery]]).

## Where the frame stops

- **Culture keeps its own map.** Florence, not Venice or Genoa, set Renaissance Italy's cultural tone, and Paris rather than London was eighteenth-century Europe's cultural capital. The bill of exchange stayed almost entirely inside Christendom, "circulating among friends so to speak." Civilization is "the grandfather, the patriarch of world history" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-an-order-among-other-orders]]).
- **State and war vary by zone.** Government is necessarily strong at the core. Near the core, mercantilism is "above all a means of self-defence," and at the edges the state is hollow. War at the core is rule-bound and fought by siege. At the periphery it becomes *guerra volante* ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-an-order-among-other-orders]]).
- **Position decides development.** England industrialized because she was "in fact the centre of the world," while the modern Third World is "firmly on the periphery." Braudel asks: "is an industrial revolution possible only at the heart of an open world-economy?" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch06-relevant-comparisons]]).

## World Machines resonances

Each rhyme is partial, and the notes' hedges belong to it.

- **[[world-machines]].** A bounded order with a dominant centre, dependent zones and a finite run echoes the world-machine idea. But Braudel's unit is "organized around trade geography and capital accumulation over centuries, not the fuller institutions-technologies-assumptions bundle a world machine comprises" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-first-world-economy-bruges-hansa]]). Several world-economies coexisting loosely recalls machines in friction, but they are "economic-geographic trade circuits with a core and periphery, not temporal-infrastructural regimes running a Dawn-Day-Dusk arc" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-turkish-empire]]). The Foreword's zones of silence are simply places exchange never reached, not rival infrastructures ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-foreword]]).
- **[[Configurancy]].** Braudel describes the orders assembling from countless uncoordinated encroachments on one another's frontiers. The note qualifies this: his frame is "a fixed small set of historical 'orders,' not Rao's open-ended agents-and-worlds ontology" ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-an-order-among-other-orders]]).
- **[[history-machine]].** Braudel subordinates Charles V's personal project to structural forces already assembled in Antwerp, Lisbon, Seville and Genoa ([[braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-an-order-among-other-orders]]).
