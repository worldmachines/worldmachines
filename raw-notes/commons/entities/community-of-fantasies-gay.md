# Community of Fantasies (Gay)

In the fiction chapter of *The Tender Passion* ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v2]]), [[peter-gay]] argues that love was the nineteenth-century novel's inescapable business and that fiction can be read as historical evidence of a class's desires and defenses. The phrase "community of fantasies" names his theory of how that works.

## Fantasy, disciplined and shared

Following Freud on the writer and daydreaming, fantasies are "unsatisfied wishes" corrected in imagination, and they "change with every variation in the human situation." A group with a strong hold on its members, such as a sect or a social class, imposes styles of feeling, channels appetites and so "organizes communities of fantasies." The individual supplies drive and anxiety, culture the materials, and aesthetic form the architecture. Fiction is fantasy "disciplined, organized, embellished," and that is why fantasies are "rich historical material" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch3-1-community-fantasies]]).

## The cracked mirror

Gay grants that the "mirror traveling along a highway" became a truism, but calls the mirror "curiously devised, badly discolored, sometimes cracked": the writer's needs and a "professional superego" matter as much as milieu, and period labels are "more useful for combat than they are for diagnosis." The novel's value as a witness lies in its capacity for "analyzing, representing, and in significant ways distorting" erotic experience ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch3-1-community-fantasies]]). Later he calls it "that undependable mirror," which reflects politics and medicine but goes its own way ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch3-3-beyond-good-evil-part-2]]). The hedge is part of the concept: fiction is evidence of what a class could bear to see, not a transparent record of practice.

## What fiction taught, and how it thrilled

Respectable fiction was a morality of postponed gratification. Its good women are "experts in serene waiting," and the undisciplined heart is punished ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch3-2-hearts-undisciplined-others]]). It also hinted at adultery or incest and then showed the suspicion unjustified, giving readers a double profit of venturesomeness and relief, a safari that turns out to be a trip to the zoo ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch3-2-hearts-undisciplined-others]]). Most middle-class readers wanted happy endings and "just a nice story". The palatable fare therefore tells the historian "what squeamish and anxious readers wanted to avoid," while a minority of demanding masters "forced access to the very conflicts ... that their culture was doing its best to drive from the stage of awareness" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch3-1-community-fantasies]]). The psychological novel, from Constant's *Adolphe* on, made lovers' irrational fantasies "the common coin of literature" and love an experience "beyond good and evil." Serious novelists came to punish the failure to love rather than loving unwisely ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch3-3-beyond-good-evil-part-1]]).

## Reading as work

Against censors such as Louis Proal, who held that reading invites imitation and novels cause crime and suicide, Gay offers a Freudian model. Reading is playful regression that teases the id, flatters the ego and soothes the superego. It is "economic" because it rehearses forbidden pleasures at little risk, and it can substitute for action as well as inhibit it. A work is a work because it "works in its readers" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch3-2-hearts-undisciplined-others]]).

## The oedipal core and "innocent, ignorant knowledge"

Incest is a perennial theme proving "love begins at home." Victorian literature mostly entered it only obliquely, as unconscious disclosure the public absorbed unconsciously ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch3-3-beyond-good-evil-part-1]]). The amorous triangle's delayed resolution mirrors the rhythm of erotic gratification. Gay reads *David Copperfield*, Turgenev's *First Love*, *Henry Esmond* and *Pepita Jiménez* as oedipal texts whose authors found things "without knowing what they saw" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch3-3-beyond-good-evil-part-2]]). Reviewers' misreadings of *Henry Esmond* (Eliot's inaccurate summary, Forster's disbelief, Oliphant's outrage) serve as evidence of collective psychic defense ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch3-3-beyond-good-evil-part-2]]).

## An anxious, fractured public

Reception evidence shows no smooth passage from reticence to candor. Pressures for restraint intensified around mid-century, just as the middle classes consolidated power. National stereotypes of French permissiveness are unreliable, and censorship continued after the 1880s ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch3-3-beyond-good-evil-part-2]]). The *Saturday Review*'s wavering on French novels, Whitman and obscenity law is "visible confusion issuing from invisible conflict" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch3-2-hearts-undisciplined-others]]). Gay's conclusion is that the bourgeois reader was anxious, not simply hypocritical or hard-hearted, and that the "community of bourgeois fantasies" had fractured into segments of reading publics. He concedes that real prudes and real prosecutions existed ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch3-3-beyond-good-evil-part-2]]; compare [[the-philistine-bourgeois-as-fiction]], [[bourgeoisophobia]]).

## Hedges

- The oedipal readings are Gay's own, applied with confidence to authors who had no such vocabulary ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch3-3-beyond-good-evil-part-2]]).
- His evidence for love as the novel's constant is argument by accumulation, not a measured survey ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch3-1-community-fantasies]]).
- The rhyme with World Machines is modest. Print, lending libraries, serial magazines, editors and reform societies act as institutions routing intimate life into approved channels, but Gay's single-class, Euro-American frame offers no machine theory ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch3-1-community-fantasies]], [[gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch3-2-hearts-undisciplined-others]]).
