# The Hermetic Magus (Yates)

In [[yates-giordano-bruno]], the Renaissance Magus is a new kind of man who operates on the world by knowing its hidden chains of influence. Yates traces him from a text to a type: from the divine Man of the Hermetic Genesis, through Ficino, Pico and Agrippa, to Bruno at the extreme and Campanella as the survivor.

## The textual charter

Yates reads Corpus Hermeticum I, the "Egyptian Genesis", against Moses. There, Man is created divine, wishes to create like the star-Governors, is not punished for it, and falls into Nature by an act of his own power. Her summary is "Man as Magus". She joins it to the *Asclepius* passage on man as *magnum miraculum*, which Pico later put at the head of his Oration ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch02-pimander-asclepius-1]]). The *Asclepius*'s god-making statues supply the doctrinal charter for sympathetic magic. The optimist gnosis of Corpus Hermeticum XI and XII, in which nothing is dead and even the earth moves, is "the ideal philosophy for Man as Magus" ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch02-pimander-asclepius-2]]). Gnosis and magic are one system: the operator knows the descending chains and builds ascending links ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch03-hermes-and-magic]]).

## Ficino: a natural magic, nervously

Ficino's *De vita coelitus comparanda* (1489) wraps talismanic magic inside ordinary medicine for melancholy scholars under Saturn. Yates argues that its commentary on Plotinus is "really a commentary on the *Asclepius*". Its mechanism is the *spiritus mundi*, for which the *Picatrix* is a probable source, though she hedges this ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch04-ficino-natural-magic-1]]). He prefers planet images to decan "demons" so that he can believe he practises a natural, not demonic, magic. Orphic singing is the aural twin of the talisman. Yates insists on continuity with medieval magic and calls Ficino's practice "a revival of the religion of the world" ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch04-ficino-natural-magic-2]]). She coins the term "astral magic", as opposed to deterministic astrology: it is a way of gaining power over the stars and escaping fate ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch03-hermes-and-magic]]).

## Pico: Magia and Cabala

Pico embraces natural magic more openly and caps it with practical Cabala, which reaches beyond the stars to angels, the Sephiroth and God through Hebrew. Yates makes him the founder of the Hermetic-Cabalist tradition and calls the Oration "the great charter of Renaissance Magic" ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch05-pico-cabalist-magic-1]]). Natural magic works through intermediate causes, while Cabala goes to the first cause, which makes it higher and more dangerous ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch05-pico-cabalist-magic-2]]). The social status of the magician changes: the outcast conjuror becomes the pious Magus, "a divine man" parallel to the divine Raphael or Leonardo. Pico is the first to formulate man as Magus acting on the world "to control his destiny by science". After his condemnation, Alexander VI vindicates him, and Yates reads the Borgia frescoes as a coded statement of that vindication ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch05-pico-cabalist-magic-3]]). Pseudo-Dionysius's angelic hierarchies supply the Christian frame, and practical Cabala gives Pico, unlike Ficino, a way to operate with angels ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch06-pseudo-dionysius]]).

## Agrippa: the survey that says it plainly

Agrippa's *De occulta philosophia* is derivative and popular, but it is the first clear synoptic map: elemental, celestial and intellectual worlds, and natural, mathematical and ceremonial magic. It goes beyond Ficino toward the demonic magic of the *Asclepius*. Yates concludes that the innocent Ficinian magic and the pious Picoan Cabala carried, through the *prisca theologia*, the power implications Agrippa makes explicit. The Magus becomes a priest working religious miracles, close to the Egyptian theocracy of the *Asclepius* ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch07-agrippa-survey]]).

## Opposition

Because the Magi claimed piety, the objection was theological: when is an angel not a demon? Pedro Garcia rejected all magic as diabolical ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch05-pico-cabalist-magic-3]]). Protestants such as Wier and Erastus wanted religion cleared of magic and read the *prisca theologia* as Egyptian idolatry. The Jesuit Del Rio rejected both Ficino's talismans and Pico's Cabala. Separately, the Latin humanist tradition, with its correct chronology and historical realism, was uncongenial to magic: Erasmus's religious humanism is anti-Magus ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch09-against-magic]]). A parallel religious Hermetism kept a Christian Hermes and disowned the magic of the *Asclepius* altogether ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch10-religious-hermetism]]).

## Bruno: the Magus without the Christian scaffolding

[[giordano-bruno]] is, for Yates, the direct logical result of the Hermetic glorification of man as divine miracle, taken where Pico never intended ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch14-bruno-cabala]]). He abandons the Christian interpretation of the Hermetica and makes the magic of the *Asclepius* "the core of Hermetism" ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch12-hermetic-reform-3]]). He subordinates Cabala to Egyptianism and drops the Sephiroth and angel orders that the Christian Magus had built to guard natural magic against demons. His magic works through "links", above all the magically excited imagination, and his memory systems exist to form a Magus personality ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch11-bruno-first-paris]], [[yates-giordano-bruno-ch14-bruno-cabala]], [[yates-giordano-bruno-ch17-bruno-germany]]). Yates traces the root theological reason to his refusal to read the Hermetic *Filius Dei* as the Second Person of the Trinity ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch19-return-to-italy]]).

## Campanella and the end of dominance

Campanella is a Magus with a mission who is "luckier and more cunning" than Bruno. He practised Ficinian astral magic for Urban VIII in 1628 and built a Catholic theology in which Grace is a kind of *Magia Divina* ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch20-campanella-1]], [[yates-giordano-bruno-ch20-campanella-2]]). After Casaubon, Fludd is the reactionary survival of Ficino and Pico, and Mersenne's attack treats image-magic as meaningless rather than frightening ("he has crossed the watershed") ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch21-after-dating-1]], [[yates-giordano-bruno-ch22-fludd-controversies-1]]).

## World Machines readings, with their hedges

The notes read the Magus as a theological, pre-scientific ancestor of the [[modernity-machine]]'s secular drive to act on the world. They qualify this each time: the operating power is divine or sympathetic, not mechanical, and Yates makes the link to science only in her Chapter VIII and conclusion (see [[will-to-operate-yates]]). Agrippa's graded, lever-bearing cosmos rhymes with the [[world-machines]] idea of deep infrastructure that organises a worldview, but it is a cosmology of occult correspondences, not an institutional machine ([[yates-giordano-bruno-ch07-agrippa-survey]]).
