# Critics as Legislators of Taste

**Source**: [[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5]], Chapter Three (*Acknowledged Legislators*), with the museum directors of Chapter Five.

## The Formula and Its Deflation

Victorian reviewers adapted Shelley's "unacknowledged legislators" (his phrase for poets) and called themselves "the acknowledged legislators of taste." The same critics also confessed their work left "no permanent mark on anyone." Gautier: "a newspaper article is a shrub that loses its leaves every evening and never bears any fruit." Gay reads the swing as a symptom of "an age of vertiginous movement." The evidence of real influence belies it: Monet subscribed to clipping services, and Bruckner dreaded Hanslick's reviews ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch03-s1-guides-to-pleasure]]).

## Two Readings of the Critic's Rise

- **The public as docile follower.** Hazlitt complained in 1814 that reputation is "stamped ... by authority, not by popular consent." In 1889 the *Musical Times* sneered at readers "of so invertebrate an order" that they could not judge for themselves ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch03-s1-guides-to-pleasure]]).
- **The public as freed.** Gautier and Fontane wrote in an intimate first person, "throwing ... bridges between critic and reader." Critics also contradicted each other over the *Eroica* and Constable's *Salisbury Cathedral*, and that taught readers expert consensus was unreliable. Gay says this paid "unexpected dividends for middle-class publics," who could "go their own way" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch03-s1-guides-to-pleasure]]).
- **Freud underneath both.** Taste is never "wholly free": "a man's home was often his parents' castle." Because habit is easier than the "mental effort" originality demands, aesthetic pleasure is conservative. That is why the avant-garde met "furious opposition" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch03-s1-guides-to-pleasure]]).

## Pedagogues, Combatants, Craftsmen

- **Pedagogues.** Shaw's "that great booby, the public" and Pissarro's complaint that an exhibition "reeks powerfully of bourgeois" come from critics who defined themselves against their own readers. Yet they kept trying to educate those readers. "The Londoner is not indifferent to fine art: he hates it" is engagement, not secession ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch03-s1-guides-to-pleasure]]).
- **Combatants.** Hanslick's campaign against Wagner mixed several things: conviction (his partisanship for Brahms), grievance (Wagner's Beckmesser caricature of him) and family history (his Jewish mother against Wagner's antisemitism). Critics' "bellicose style" and "military metaphors" cast them as defenders of culture "against barbarians on the march" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch03-s1-guides-to-pleasure]]).
- **Craftsmen.**
  - Sainte-Beuve called himself "a naturalist of minds" practicing "literary natural history." His 1857 review held *Madame Bovary* "accountable to criticism alone." In Gay's framing he was also "a good bourgeois in his own way," and the Goncourts called him "a hopeless bourgeois" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch03-s2-master-craftsmen]]).
  - Thoré was a democrat who was imprisoned and exiled. He wanted "ART FOR MAN," yet held that "the subject hardly matters." His decades-long campaign for Vermeer, "a Sphinx," shows criticism manufacturing a canon and a market together. Even Fromentin walked past the "splendid Vermeers" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch03-s2-master-craftsmen]]).
  - In Paris, Thoré also diagnosed the "governmental logic" of French art administration ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch02-s2-majestic-dispensations]]).

## Incoherence as the Norm

- **Judgments were chronically inconsistent.** Baudelaire preferred Constantin Guys to his friend Manet. Shaw found Schubert "brainless." Henry James ignored the Impressionists and preferred Sargent. Flaubert reversed his verdict on Stendhal within seven years ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch03-s3-from-zola-to-wilde]]).
- **Manet's defenders still classed him as bourgeois.** Ravenel hailed *Olympia* as "an armed insurrection in the camp of the bourgeois." Zola rescued it as pure form, yet still "recognized Manet to be a bourgeois, if a rebellious one." Gay calls both defenses "both satisfactory and unsatisfactory" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch03-s3-from-zola-to-wilde]]).
- **Wilde put criticism above creation.** In "The Critic as Artist" criticism "demands infinitely more cultivation than creation" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch03-s3-from-zola-to-wilde]]).
- **Baudelaire the pluralist.** He wrote "as many sorts of beauty as there are habitual ways of seeking happiness" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch01-s1-battles-of-the-budgets]]), and in his 1846 Salon he addressed the bourgeoisie directly as the founder of "collections, museums, galleries" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-bexp-s3-confusing-confrontations]]).
- **Canons were flexible.** The official Salons eventually absorbed Corot and the Barbizon painters. Bénédite in 1897 could define the Impressionists only by negatives ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-bexp-s3-confusing-confrontations]]).

## Beyond the Press

Taste had other legislators besides critics.

- **Museum directors.** Lichtwark in Hamburg, Tschudi in Berlin and Pauli in Bremen "acted as important collectors in their own right—with other people's money." For Lichtwark taste was "a telltale sign of character" and almost "a matter of national health" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch05-s3-the-perspiring-philistine-on-trial]]).
- **Advisers.** Walters's adviser Lucas was content with "a bland menu" of minor academics ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch04-s3-the-victorian-maecenas]]). The chapter also opens on Mary Cassatt as dealer-adviser and on the conformist Mrs. Bates, who says of Corot "we let him go ahead" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch03-s1-guides-to-pleasure]]).

## Resonances (hedged)

- **[[kulturkritik]].** Critics' anxiety about their own legitimacy is "criticism's kulturkritik turned reflexively on itself" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch03-s1-guides-to-pleasure]]). Sainte-Beuve fits "lightly," since his mode is scholarly and institutional, not a totalizing civilizational diagnosis ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch03-s2-master-craftsmen]]). Wilde makes the act of critique itself the site of value ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch03-s3-from-zola-to-wilde]]).
- **[[carey-intellectuals-and-masses]].** Sainte-Beuve's guardianship of standards sits closer to Carey's defensive intelligentsia. Thoré's democratism is "the contrary case within the very same profession" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch03-s2-master-craftsmen]]). Gay's Victorian critics are a "narrower, less totalizing cousin" of Carey's modernists, still educating their public rather than seceding from it ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch03-s1-guides-to-pleasure]]).
- **[[western-canon]].** On Gay's evidence canons were made by advocacy and were flexible rather than fixed (Vermeer, the Barbizon painters) ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch03-s2-master-craftsmen]], [[gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-bexp-s3-confusing-confrontations]]).
