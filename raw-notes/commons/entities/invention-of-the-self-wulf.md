# The invention of the self (Wulf)

The subtitle of [[wulf-magnificent-rebels]] names its thesis. Wulf claims that in the 1790s the Jena Set, 'intoxicated by the French Revolution, placed the self at the centre stage of their thinking' ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-prologue-1]]), and that 'We are this Ich' ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-prologue-2]]). The notes treat the claim of lasting influence as asserted at the head of the book rather than demonstrated ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-prologue-1]]). What the chapters do show is a genealogy in two registers, doctrine and life.

## The doctrinal line

- **Prehistory.** *Werther*'s 'I withdraw into myself and find a world there' marks the Sturm und Drang elevation of feeling that Goethe had left behind by 1794 ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch01-happy-event-1]]; see [[sturm-und-drang]]).
- **Kant.** In Wulf's gloss, time, space and causality become categories of the mind, making the self a lawgiver of nature rather than a cog in a mechanistic universe ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch02-priest-of-truth-1]]).
- **Fichte.** The Ich posits itself and the non-Ich; freedom and self-determination are its ground ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch02-priest-of-truth-1]], [[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch02-priest-of-truth-2]]; see [[johann-gottlieb-fichte]]).
- **Novalis.** The Ich is circular; 'Philosophy is originally a feeling'; love turns the non-Ich into a 'You' ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch05-novalis-in-love]]). He also took the free Ich to its limit in a plan to die by willpower ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch08-handful-of-darkness]]).
- **Schelling.** Self and nature are identical ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch11-naturphilosophie]]), and later an Absolute precedes both ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch18-starving-rats-1]]); see [[naturphilosophie-wulf]].
- **Goethe's Faust.** Wulf reads 'In the beginning was the Act' as the original act of the Ich positing itself ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch17-black-fog-2]]).
- **Hegel.** Wulf's gloss on the *Phenomenology*: mind moves from consciousness to self-consciousness to 'absolute knowing', realising that reality is its own creation ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch20-french-in-town-1]]).

## The lived line

Wulf repeatedly places lives beside doctrine. While Fichte and Schelling wrote about the self-determining Ich, Dorothea Veit, divorcing at great risk, 'belonged to herself' ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch13-divorce-women-sex]]). Caroline 'lived and breathed the new philosophical ideas' as 'the empowered free self' ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch17-black-fog-1]]). The Humboldts' consensual open marriage is documented through their own letters ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch06-splendid-circle-1]]). Novalis, Friedrich, Caroline and Dorothea believed 'their personal experiences were reflections of a wider world', and turned life into writing ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch14-schlegel-clique]]); Novalis's diary is, in Wulf's words, 'an uncompromising investigation and meditation on the self' ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch08-handful-of-darkness]]).

## Afterlife

Wulf argues that Wilhelm von Humboldt's Berlin university was shaped by 'the concept of a self-determined Ich', as education for self-emancipation ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-epilogue-after-jena-2]]), and that the centrality of self-consciousness helped pave the way for psychology and psychoanalysis, while stopping short of saying Freud derived his theory from Jena ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-epilogue-influence-selfish]]).

## Against Gay's inwardness

The notes set this repeatedly beside Peter Gay's account ([[infrastructure-of-bourgeois-inwardness]], [[self-construction-through-writing-gay]]) and mark the difference each time. Wulf's self is philosophical and volitional rather than domestic or epistolary ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch02-priest-of-truth-2]]); she treats it as a discovery, liberation and legacy 'we must use wisely', not an infrastructural construct ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-prologue-2]]); her actors are self-consciously experimental where Gay's bourgeois describe an inward habit ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch14-schlegel-clique]]). The parallel with [[self-construction-through-writing]] holds best for Novalis's diary and the Romantic novels and loosest for Caroline, whose self-making the note locates in conduct rather than writing ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch17-black-fog-1]]). The moral tension this self carries is on [[art-of-being-selfish-wulf]].
