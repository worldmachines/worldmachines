# The Homogenocene (Mann)

A term that, Mann says, some biologists now use for the era in which once-distinct ecologies blend into a uniform mix. He ties it to the [[columbian-exchange-mann]] and uses it as [[mann-1493]]'s name for the world Colón 'almost accidentally created'; 'the world has become one' ([[mann-1493-ch01-to-the-lighthouse]]). He admits that a new epoch brought on by a world-spanning economy sounds grandiose ([[mann-1493-ch01-reversals-of-fortune]]).

## Where Mann finds it

- **In miniature.** Hispaniola's hitchhiking earthworms, mosquitoes, honeybees and rats, pouring 'like eager tourists' into new lands, set up the concept ([[mann-1493-ch01-seams-of-pangaea]]). Tsenacomoco, becoming 'ever more like Europe' through tobacco, livestock and honeybees, shows 'the hallmark of the nascent Homogenocene' ([[mann-1493-ch02-english-flies]]); the Jamestown colonists were 'bringing the Homogenocene to North America' ([[mann-1493-ch02-lowly-creatures-strange-land]]).
- **In Asia.** The galleon trade opened the Homogenocene in Asia ([[mann-1493-ch01-shiploads-of-silver]]). Ming decrees and silver hunger turned eastern China toward mulberry monoculture, a homogenization Mann does not name there ([[mann-1493-ch04-wooden-noses]]).
- **For people.** Polyglot, unequal Mexico City was the Homogenocene 'for Homo sapiens' and 'the first twenty-first-century city', with Mann's qualification that modernity is 'a patchy thing' ([[mann-1493-ch08-shook-up-city]]). The worldwide population boom is 'a sign and effect of the onset of the Homogenocene' ([[mann-1493-ch06-potato-wars-sea-of-genes]]). Even comparing malaria across the Atlantic is 'a mark of the era we live in' ([[mann-1493-ch03-no-distempers-villa-plasmodia]]).
- **As pest infrastructure.** Mann's own synthesis: uniform fields of few varieties, joined by railroads, steamships and refrigeration, make each farm an identical food pool that a pest need adapt to only once. L. O. Howard's 1898 count, thirty-seven of the seventy worst US insect pests being recent imports, is his evidence ([[mann-1493-ch06-war-upon-the-beetles]]).
- **As lived landscape.** A Yunnan rubber plantation of one species and one age, silent of birds and insects, where a driver says 'There's nothing left' and the farmers are grateful for the income. Mann leaves both verdicts standing ([[mann-1493-ch07-no-birds-grease-chemistry]]).
- **As forecast.** Leaf blight reaching Asia's clonal rubber would make Asia and the Americas 'increasingly alike' through a shared crop and a shared pathogen. This is Mann's projection, not a finding ([[mann-1493-ch07-wickham-end-of-world]]).
- **As a campaign target.** Manila conservationists fight invasive exotics in a country becoming a 'vest-pocket version of the Homogenocene', while botanists point out that every plant in the folk song 'Bahay Kubo' is introduced ([[mann-1493-ch10-fractured-cerebration-staircases]]).

## Sameness and its counter-current

Mann does not treat homogenization as the whole story. Ortiz's transculturation and 'the crash and chaos of constant connection' frame the book ([[mann-1493-prologue]]). In the Coda the Homogenocene produces both identical landscapes and fragmentation into local identities: people want the world's goods and also want to 'be aggressively themselves'. Mann leaves open which wins, or whether the conflict is inevitable ([[mann-1493-ch10-on-the-boat]]). He implicates himself, 'simultaneously promoting and denouncing the Columbian Exchange' at a nursery counter, and calls this 'fractured cerebration'. Supposedly age-old heritage such as the Ifugao terraces turns out to be a product of the same exchange now threatening it ([[mann-1493-ch10-fractured-cerebration-staircases]]).

## World Machines resonances, with their hedges

- **[[divergence-machine]].** The Homogenocene is convergence of ecosystems, so it rhymes with the Divergence Machine only inversely; Mann's concern is biological rather than institutional ([[mann-1493-ch02-english-flies]], [[mann-1493-ch01-to-the-lighthouse]]). The beetle's genetic diversity beating the farmers' uniform response is a loose analogy for variety defeating standardization ([[mann-1493-ch06-war-upon-the-beetles]]).
- **[[modernity-machine]] and [[Legibility]].** Uniform, readable land answered by illegible, uncontrolled pest explosions is the notes' reading. The note says plainly that Scott-style legibility is the note-writer's gloss, not Mann's ([[mann-1493-ch06-war-upon-the-beetles]]).
- **[[contested-uniformity-bayly]].** The notes connect the Coda's unity-versus-division question to Bayly's paradox of convergence breeding difference ([[mann-1493-ch10-on-the-boat]]). Mann's version starts from ecology and stays an open question.
