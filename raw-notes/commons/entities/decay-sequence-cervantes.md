# Decay Sequence: Chivalry's Ladder in Don Quixote

Extends [[decay-sequence]] with material from the [[cervantes-don-quixote]] reading notes.

## Candidate instance: chivalry's ladder in Don Quixote

Like the Adams cases on [[decay-sequence]], these are **candidates**: Cervantes never states a sequence, and each rung is a reader's reading.

Chivalry is unusual among the corpus's candidate instances because its *Functional* stage is already over before the text begins. Ormsby's prefatory essay dates the death to the fall of Granada and Ferdinand's dismantling of "the free institutions of mediaeval Spain," and is explicit that what the novel attacked was the costume afterlife rather than the institution ([[cervantes-don-quixote-front-essay-don-quixote]]); the biography sets the Cervantes family's own real knights-errant, castle-holding and Moor-fighting, in the generations before the author's own poverty ([[cervantes-don-quixote-front-cervantes-life-1]]). The novel therefore reads as a comedy conducted entirely on the lower rungs.

| Stage | Case in the novel |
|---|---|
| Functional | offstage — the ancestral knights-errant of the Cervantes pedigree, and chivalry as it stood before Granada |
| Performative | the innkeeper's dubbing: an account-book for a missal, prayers muttered over a kneeling man, serving girls girding the sword while suppressing laughter, performed cynically by the host and sincerely by its subject ([[cervantes-don-quixote-p1-01-05]]) |
| Explicitly Allegorical | the ducal pageants — a costumed Merlin decreeing 3,300 lashes from a triumphal car ([[cervantes-don-quixote-p2-33-36]]), the Trifaldi's procession and the wooden Pegasus rigged with fireworks ([[cervantes-don-quixote-p2-37-41]]) — the form staged as a statement about itself, by people who need it only to be diverting |
| Ornamental | warrior-saints carved for a village retablo and carried past on labourers' shoulders, an older order of sacralised violence now literally freight ([[cervantes-don-quixote-p2-57-59]]); and the mock epitaph-sonnets of the "Academicians of Argamasilla" that close Part I ([[cervantes-don-quixote-p1-52]]) |
| Invisible / Absent | not staged in the novel, but recorded in its reception: Pacheco's testimony that afterwards any man in the old drapery was mocked as a Don Quixote himself ([[cervantes-don-quixote-front-essay-don-quixote]]) |

Three things this case contributes beyond another set of rungs:

- **The discourse can be fully intact when the referent is gone.** Don Diego de Miranda judges that the entire law and ordinance of knight-errantry could be reconstructed out of Don Quixote's breast alone, in the same breath as calling him "a man of brains gone mad" ([[cervantes-don-quixote-p2-17-19]]). Ornament here is not simplification but elaboration — the system is *more* articulate on the way down, which cuts against reading late-stage decoration as mere residue.
- **The diagnostic gets performed in-fiction, by a partisan.** The curate and barber's scrutiny of the library sorts a whole genre volume by volume — spared for style and usefulness, kept as a friend's ornament, or flung out of the window as combustible husk, with *Amadis* weighed for condemnation as "the founder of so vile a sect" ([[cervantes-don-quixote-p1-06-09]]). It is exactly the connoisseurship [[decay-sequence]] recommends, run by someone with an interest in the verdict, which is a useful caution about who holds the instrument.
- **The afterlife corollary is unusually well documented.** The dead institution's ridiculed stage out-produced its functional one by a margin no other case in the corpus matches: "quixotic" entered the languages, and Ormsby's introduction can already treat two and a half centuries of English readership as part of what the book *is* ([[cervantes-don-quixote-front-introduction]]).

The obvious objection is the one [[decay-sequence]] already carries about over-reading ornament: Cervantes is writing comedy, and comedy selects for absurd survivals. A dead form that persists *quietly and usefully* would be invisible to this novel by construction.
