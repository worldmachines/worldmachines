## Afterlife: the island Utopia put to administrative work (Grove)

Richard Grove's *Green Imperialism* ([[grove-green-imperialism]]) traces what happened to the island-commonwealth form once Europeans held real islands. More appears in Grove's Utopian lineage as inheritance, named directly beside Dante, Columbus, Shakespeare and Marvell ([[grove-green-imperialism-conclusion]]).

**A template being revised.** Grove places *The Tempest* a century after *Utopia* and reads it as moving away from More's “redemption of European political economy” toward an empirical register ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch01-s3-discourse-of-islands]]). The robinsonnade tradition runs from More through Godwin, Neville's *Isle of Pines*, Vairasse's *Severambians*, Leguat and Defoe, and Grove argues that it and physiocracy together made “a fully fledged” environmentalism possible ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch05-s7-defoe-rousseau-commerson]]). A Huguenot proposal for an “Isle of Eden” sufficient “to contain easily a long descent of generations” echoes the bounded island-society, with the difference that Rodriguez was being colonised in fact ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch01-s4-st-helena-mauritius-insular]]).

**Renamed onto Tahiti.** Bougainville had called Tahiti “La Nouvelle Cythère.” Commerson substituted “Utopia,” explicitly crediting More's republic, which turned a timeless erotic myth into an arguable model polity ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch05-s9-commerson-utopian-science]]).

**From description to ordinance.** Grove draws a direct line from Commerson's praise of Tahitian fruit trees planted at “judicious intervals” to the tree-planting provisions of Mauritius's first conservation ordinance, promulgated about a year later ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch05-s9-commerson-utopian-science]]). Poivre told the colonists they lived in a “physical and social paradise” and backed the vision with a Land Court, felling caps and reserves ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch05-s5-poivre-programmes]]). The note on the 1769 ordinance reads Commerson's Baconian inventory of the island as the kind of total survey [[the-legible-commonwealth]] describes, now serving a real colonial state, though Grove himself frames it as scientific patronage and moral vision ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch05-s6-poivre-commerson]]).

**The new condition.** For Commerson and Bernardin de Saint-Pierre, “for an island to be a Utopia” the laws of nature had to be obeyed and artifice minimised, so environmentalism became a condition of the ideal society ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch05-s7-defoe-rousseau-commerson]]). Grove's own counterweight is that Mauritius was a slave colony, “far from being a Utopia” ([[grove-green-imperialism-ch05-s9-commerson-utopian-science]]). See [[tropical-island-eden-grove]].
