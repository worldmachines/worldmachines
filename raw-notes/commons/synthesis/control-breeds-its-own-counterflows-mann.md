# Control That Bred Its Own Counterflows (Mann)

**Kind**: synthesis across the [[mann-1493]] notes. Mann never states this pattern as a thesis. It appears only when his separate case studies of sea bans, cartels, racial taxonomies, labor imports and planting quotas are read side by side.

## The cases

- **The Ming sea ban.** Meant to monopolize trade's profits, the ban cut the navy because trade was illegal and produced 'a delirium of smuggling'. Wokou crews were mostly Chinese traders working with Yuegang merchant families, and local elites and even naval officers were entangled, so enforcement was hollowed from within: 'Merchants were pirates, pirates were merchants' (Lin Renchuan). The attempt to increase state power ended by weakening it ([[mann-1493-ch04-extra-effort-merchants-pirates]]).
- **Ming money.** A state that banned coin after coin ended by adopting the merchants' privately certified silver through tax edicts, then bought that silver from the wokou it was fighting, 'at war with its own money supply' ([[mann-1493-ch04-out-of-money]]).
- **Spain's Manila cartel.** Cartels, taxes, ship quotas and textile-import limits were all evaded by smuggling on both sides ([[mann-1493-ch04-wooden-noses]]). Manila's Spaniards needed Chinese labor and silk while fearing the Chinese, and cycled through restriction, massacre and repopulation into the nineteenth century ([[mann-1493-ch04-magic-mountain]]).
- **The casta system.** As mixing grew, authorities tried harder to separate people just as separation became impossible; people shopped for identities and officials sold them ([[mann-1493-ch08-family-values]]).
- **The Panama road.** Spain imported enslaved Africans to run it and so created the maroon population that most threatened it. A principal-agent split between short-term Seville merchants and a distant crown blocked any campaign ([[mann-1493-ch09-in-the-isthmus]]). On Hispaniola, officials kept asking for thousands more slaves while Lemba's sixteen-year war shut most sugar mills ([[mann-1493-ch09-africans-in-charge]]).
- **Qing highlands.** Zhejiang officials banned maize in 1802 and 1824 and nothing happened. Landowners kept tax-free upland rents and spread the flood risk across the region, 'absorbing all of the gain and only a fraction of the pain' ([[mann-1493-ch05-malthusian-interlude-mountains]]).
- **Tree quotas.** On the Loess Plateau, plan targets reward trees planted rather than trees alive, and farmers with no stake let them die: 'Every year we plant trees, but no trees survive' ([[mann-1493-ch05-unlearning-from-dazhai]]).
- **Yuan militias.** The Yuan authorized private militias against the peasant rebellions of the 1350s; they escaped control and tore Zaytun apart ([[mann-1493-appendix-b-globalization-in-beta]]).

## The connection

Three things recur that no single note says.

1. **The controller depended on the controlled.** In each case the instrument of control relied on the same people who defeated it: the Ming on Fujianese merchants for silver, Manila on the Parián for silk and labor, the Panama road on enslaved Africans, New Spain on the mixed people its taxonomy tried to sort, Zhejiang's reformers on the landowners who profited from upland rents, the Yuan on the militias it licensed. The counterflow was not an outside force but the dependency turned around.
2. **Failure often ended in ratification.** The Ming rescinded the ban in 1567 and made silver its tax money ([[mann-1493-ch04-extra-effort-merchants-pirates]], [[mann-1493-ch04-out-of-money]]). Spain tolerated the Parián under its own gobernadorcillo ([[mann-1493-ch04-wooden-noses]]). Officials sold casta identities and promoted mestizo heirs to Spaniards ([[mann-1493-ch08-family-values]]). The crown signed 'capitulations' with maroons it could not defeat ([[mann-1493-ch09-capitulations]]). At Mazagão, maroons and the Portuguese kept up a mutually convenient fiction ([[mann-1493-ch09-shake-it-ox-dona-rosario]]). The state wrote into law, or into pretence, what it could not suppress.
3. **The measure detached from the goal.** Where control survived as record-keeping it tracked the wrong thing: tree counts instead of living trees ([[mann-1493-ch05-unlearning-from-dazhai]]), stamped and numbered silver bars while Basque officials evaded the crown's fifth ([[mann-1493-ch04-treasure-of-the-world]]), and flood records that let the Qing state see a disaster without acting on it ([[mann-1493-ch05-malthusian-interlude-mountains]]).

## What this reading must hold

- **Mann does not conclude this.** His explanations are local: Economics 101 cartel logic in Manila ([[mann-1493-ch04-wooden-noses]]), a principal-agent problem in Panama ([[mann-1493-ch09-in-the-isthmus]]), Anne Osborne's collective-action problem in the Qing ([[mann-1493-ch05-malthusian-interlude-mountains]]), and incentives on the Loess Plateau ([[mann-1493-ch05-unlearning-from-dazhai]]).
- **Control sometimes worked.** Potosí's silver kept flowing through the Basque-Vicuña war ([[mann-1493-ch04-treasure-of-the-world]]), and Portugal did destroy Palmares, though with a hybrid force that had more in common with the maroons than with the metropole ([[mann-1493-ch09-africans-in-charge]]). The pattern is recurrent, not universal.
- **Being made readable can protect.** In the Amazon, legal recognition became the maroons' shield ([[mann-1493-ch09-shake-it-ox-dona-rosario]]), so the pattern is not a general case against legibility.
- **Period sources.** The picture of a Ming state 'utterly' losing control comes partly from gazetteers and chroniclers whose formulations Mann himself flags as face-saving or idiomatic ([[mann-1493-ch04-extra-effort-merchants-pirates]]).

## World Machines resonances

This is the failure side of [[legibility-as-machine-core]] and [[Legibility]]: projects to make trade, money, people or land readable and channelled that generated the informal networks they aimed to suppress. The notes flag each rhyme as loose and none of the vocabulary as Mann's ([[mann-1493-ch04-extra-effort-merchants-pirates]], [[mann-1493-ch04-out-of-money]], [[mann-1493-ch08-family-values]]). The ratification point meets [[decentralization-produces-chokepoints]] from the other side: there, escapes from a centre produce new chokepoints; here, chokepoints that could not hold produced negotiated escapes. That comparison is this page's, not the notes'.
