# Poeticising the mechanical world (Wulf)

The reading notes on [[wulf-magnificent-rebels]] keep meeting the same image in different mouths, and keep adding the same hedge. Set side by side, they show a consistent pattern that no single note states.

## The recurring image

- **Wulf's scene-setting.** An eighteenth century of instruments, steam engines, roads, maps and pendulum clocks, in which humans 'remained cogs in a seemingly divinely ordained machine'; Novalis's 'monotonous clatter of a gigantic millwheel'; Hegel's 'Everything is useful' ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-prologue-2]]).
- **Schiller.** 'Utility is the great idol of our time' ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch03-finest-minds-1]]).
- **Humboldt, in Wulf's retrospect.** A nature squeezed into a 'corset of classification' or run as divine clockwork ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch07-little-academy-1]]).
- **Novalis.** The 'age of classification' of Linnaeus's two-word names and a metre defined by the planet, and a Prussia run like a factory by 'mechanical administration' ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch08-handful-of-darkness]]); light reduced to 'mathematical obedience' ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch11-naturphilosophie]]); and in *Europe*, the world as 'a materialistic machine, a gigantic millwheel' ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch15-new-confederation]]).
- **Schelling.** The English value mathematics only for 'Spinning Jennies & Stocking-weaving machines' ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch18-starving-rats-2]]).
- **Institutions too.** A student's name for the Tübinger Stift is 'a miserable cog in a slave machine' ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch10-symphilosophy]]).

## The remedy is synthesis, not refusal

The remedy each time is a new mode of knowing inside science and art. Novalis says 'The sciences must all be poeticised', and Wulf stresses he was not against reason but for synthesis ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch08-handful-of-darkness]]). The Tübingen resolution wanted to poeticise the sciences ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch10-symphilosophy]]). Schelling kept experiment ('a question posed to nature') while making art the key ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch14-schlegel-clique]]). Humboldt used forty-two instruments alongside feeling and imagination ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch19-current-exodus]]). Wulf presents the *Athenaeum* group as extending science and philosophy rather than rejecting them ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch09-sublime-impertinence-2]]), and says they wanted imagination alongside reason ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-prologue-2]]). Schiller's critique comes from within the Enlightenment, through Kant ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch03-finest-minds-1]]).

## The connection

Each note flags its own passage locally. Read together, they show a pattern that holds across the Set's members and across a decade: the machine is named as the thing to answer, and the answer is almost always a change of epistemic mode (poeticised science, art as knowledge, feeling joined to measurement) rather than an institutional counter-structure or a return to a pre-mechanical past. The one clear exception proves the rule. Novalis's *Europe* answers the millwheel with an idealised medieval Christendom, and it is the text the friends themselves suspected of 'going backwards' and which Goethe advised them not to publish ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch15-new-confederation]]). The targets also differ, and the notes keep them apart: a mechanised cosmos and utility for Novalis and Schelling ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch15-new-confederation]], [[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch18-starving-rats-2]]), taxonomy for Humboldt ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch07-little-academy-1]]), and only in the Linnaeus-metre-administration passage something close to [[Legibility]] in the state-and-standards sense ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch08-handful-of-darkness]]).

## World Machines hedges

- The image rhymes with [[modernity-machine]] and its drive for [[Legibility]], but Wulf's machine is a metaphor, not a machine-theory of eras ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-prologue-2]]). The note on Novalis says a Divergence-versus-Modernity reading ([[divergence-machine]], [[world-machines]]) is the notes' mapping, not Wulf's ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch08-handful-of-darkness]]); Schelling's jibe is aesthetic, not a critique of infrastructure ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch18-starving-rats-2]]).
- The synthesis-seeking remedy sits uneasily with the anti-rationalists of [[isaiah-berlin]]'s [[counter-enlightenment]] ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch08-handful-of-darkness]]), and one note calls Wulf's portrait of the Enlightenment 'somewhat one-sided' ([[wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch11-naturphilosophie]]).
- If the pattern holds, it suggests the Jena Set belongs less to a rejection of the modernity machine than to an attempt to change what counts as knowing within it. That is this page's reading of the notes, offered as an imperfect rhyme. For the doctrine behind it see [[naturphilosophie-wulf]].
