# Making a people one without legibility (Douglass's Rousseau)

A cross-cutting reading that ties several local arguments in [[douglass-rousseau-and-hobbes]] to the World Machines frame. Almost every reading note from the book flags the same hedged rhyme: [[jean-jacques-rousseau]]'s general will, legislator and civil religion are "a design for making a people one", comparable at a distance to the legibility programmes of the [[modernity-machine]] and [[the-legible-commonwealth]]. No single note says what the rhyme amounts to when the notes are put together. This page states it. [[robin-douglass]] is an intellectual historian and makes none of these claims; the connection is the reader's.

## The claim

Read across the notes, Douglass's Rousseau is a unification design built explicitly **against** the legibility route to order. Three features, taken from different notes, together mark it as a counter-design and not a variant:

1. **Its medium is passion, not information.** The Mirabeau letter rejects the Physiocrats' rational "natural order" because it calculates the despot's interest and ignores "the play of the passions". It is a system for "the people of Utopia", not "the children of Adam" ([[douglass-rousseau-and-hobbes-intro-mirabeau-letter]]). Rousseau faults the Abbé de Saint-Pierre's "perfected reason" and a political language grown "more precise and less passionate" ([[douglass-rousseau-and-hobbes-ch4-reason-and-the-passions]]). The republic binds citizens through love of fatherland, festivals, public esteem and pride aimed at civic glory ([[douglass-rousseau-and-hobbes-ch4-love-of-fatherland]]). Where [[legibility-as-machine-core]] makes a population readable from a centre, Rousseau's design makes the population feel itself one. The notes' own qualifier recurs throughout: "affective, not informational" ([[douglass-rousseau-and-hobbes-ch4-of-love-and-fear]]).
2. **It forbids the representative centre.** Legibility needs a reading centre. Rousseau's sovereignty cannot be alienated, represented or divided. It exists only in the assembled citizens willing general laws, and any representative sovereignty is an illegitimate alienation ([[douglass-rousseau-and-hobbes-ch3-sovereignty-inverted]], [[douglass-rousseau-and-hobbes-conclusion-oppositions-and-affinities]]). Freedom is dependence on law, not on any man's will ([[douglass-rousseau-and-hobbes-ch3-freedom-preserved]]). The legislator who "denatures" citizens has no legislative right and can only persuade "without convincing" ([[douglass-rousseau-and-hobbes-ch3-law-nature-denaturing]]). See [[sovereignty-inverted-douglass]].
3. **It does not scale, by design.** The republic requires a people with a common bond, self-sufficiency and uncorrupted passions. It prefers agriculture to commerce, applies to small republics at low levels of economic development, and aims at preventing corruption, not remedying it ([[douglass-rousseau-and-hobbes-conclusion-republics-naturally-good]]). Once luxury arrives the passions turn individualistic, and by the logic of the argument "fatherland and citizen" should be "effaced from modern languages". The legibility machine spreads through exactly the commerce and scale that dissolve Rousseau's precondition.

The rhyme with [[the-legible-commonwealth]] is therefore partly a mirror image. More's Utopia answers dispossession by *universalizing* legibility toward communal ends. Douglass's Rousseau answers corruption by refusing the administrative route altogether, and his own jibe at the Physiocrats uses the word "Utopia" for the rationalist design he rejects ([[douglass-rousseau-and-hobbes-intro-mirabeau-letter]]). Both aim at a people made one. They differ on the instrument.

## Where Hobbes sits

Hobbes also designed for unity by ordering the passions. He redirected fear of death toward the state of nature and taught *Leviathan* in the universities ([[douglass-rousseau-and-hobbes-ch4-hobbes-and-fear]]). His sovereign is the representative centre Rousseau forbids. Douglass's contrast of fear and love ([[douglass-rousseau-and-hobbes-ch4-of-love-and-fear]]; see [[love-and-fear-douglass]]) can be read as two answers to one problem of making a people governable. Hobbes's answer, a single representative will held together by fear, sits nearer the centralizing logic the WMP associates with the [[modernity-machine]]. Rousseau's answer, an assembled will held together by love, sits further from it. This is an extrapolation. The notes compare Hobbes's engineered fear to legibility projects only as "family resemblance" ([[douglass-rousseau-and-hobbes-ch4-hobbes-and-fear]]).

## The dark edge, and Douglass's limit on it

Unification by cultivated passion has its own costs. Public education makes the citizen feel "no longer one but a part of the unity" ([[douglass-rousseau-and-hobbes-ch4-love-of-fatherland]]), and critics have read civil religion and the total-unity reading as threats to liberty (see [[isaiah-berlin]] and [[counter-enlightenment]]). Douglass sets aside the totalitarianism debate over civil religion ([[douglass-rousseau-and-hobbes-ch3-unity-civil-religion]]). He argues that will-formation does not destroy freedom in Rousseau's own thin, non-Kantian sense ([[douglass-rousseau-and-hobbes-ch4-free-will-and-virtue]]), and he takes a middle position between pluralism and total unity ([[douglass-rousseau-and-hobbes-ch3-freedom-preserved]]). This page records that disagreement and does not settle it.

## Limits of the rhyme

- **Normative, not infrastructural.** Rousseau's republic is a normative, republican design for small states. It is not the deep infrastructure of an era, and it was never built at scale. Douglass calls the vision obsolete for modern commercial states even as its critique of commerce stayed live enough to demand [[adam-smith]]'s answer ([[douglass-rousseau-and-hobbes-conclusion-republics-naturally-good]]).
- **Not simply anti-modern.** Rousseau reaches his conclusions with modern natural-law tools (sovereignty, contract, will) ([[douglass-rousseau-and-hobbes-ch3-sovereignty-inverted]]). He cannot be placed cleanly outside the machine, which fits the [[kulturkritik]] pattern of critiquing modernity with modernity's instruments. Douglass does not draw that link himself.
