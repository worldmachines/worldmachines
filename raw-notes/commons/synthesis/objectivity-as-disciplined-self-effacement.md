# Objectivity as Disciplined Self-Effacement

*The Naked Heart* treats history-writing as one of the bourgeoisie's instruments of self-knowledge, "the autobiography of their culture" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v4-ch3-price-of-professionalism]]). Read beside the chapters on the novel and autobiography, the century's cult of objectivity looks less like an escape from the volume's reveal/conceal problem than one more form of it. In history and fiction alike, the author's withdrawal was a technique for getting at other minds, and also a way of concealing one's own. Gay reads it, as he reads the diarist's reserve, as a defended boundary.

## Four versions of the vanishing author

- **Ranke's *Selbstauslöschung*.** Ranke's historicism ("every epoch is immediate to God"; say only "how it had really been") depended on an empathetic self-obliteration, entering the past "humbly, hat in hand." Acton praised him for repressing "the poet, the patriot, the religious or political partisan" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v4-ch3-ranke]]).
- **The prose of the discipline.** Macaulay opened his *History* in 1848 with "I propose to write." Henry Adams opened his forty years later with a census figure and no "I" anywhere. Gay calls this a move "from emotional self-display to clinical self-concealment," the diarist's managed boundary enacted in a discipline's style ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v4-ch3-price-of-professionalism]]).
- **"Discipline" itself.** Historians' preferred word "suggestively hints at a commitment to self-control, that prized nineteenth-century bourgeois trait." Gay pushes this toward the historian as a cultural psychoanalyst surfacing "the sins of the fathers" against a "conspiracy of silence" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v4-ch3-biographic-appetite]]).
- **Chekhov's impartial witness.** Chekhov insisted that a story's speaker is not its author and that the writer should not "solve such problems as God, pessimism," only report and leave conclusions to the reader. Gay's paradox is that this self-effacement, not self-exposure, "will secure him the access to the naked heart he craves" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v4-ch4-immediacy]]).

A fifth, cautionary instance comes from autobiography. Darwin wrote his memoir "as if I were a dead man in another world looking back at my own life," a disclaimer of self-interest that Gay treats as itself a pose ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v4-ch2-between-probes-and-poses]]).

## The counter-pole: identification

Self-effacement had a twin that pulled the opposite way. Gay defines identification as ranging from ordinary empathy to "total merger with the other": Fustel's "it is history that is speaking through me," Michelet dissolving into "the Spirit of the Revolution." It delivered "prized insights" and also let Motley disguise his hatred of Philip II as scholarship, serving "aggression no less than insight" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v4-ch3-price-of-professionalism]]). Ranke's self-obliteration was itself a form of identification, absorbing "the feelings, the values, the beliefs of distant ages" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v4-ch3-ranke]]).

## The connection

Neither pole escaped the self. Ranke's objectivity was "an achieved public image, not a private fact." His letters reveal a man at home with kings, anxious about "the socialist menace," blind to religious minds outside his own tradition, making love to archives ("I am longing for the moment when I gain admittance to her"), and writing a historicism that was "Lutheranism by other means" and that patriots used "as an agent for German self-satisfaction" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v4-ch3-ranke]]). The profession's professed disinterestedness coexisted with Acton's "cloven hoof of party preference." Historians risked becoming "flattering mirrors of their time rather than unclouded telescopes," and the simplified patriotic history of the classroom, not the "demanding Rankean ideal," primed Europe for August 1914 ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v4-ch3-agendas-high-and-low]]).

Gay's own resolution is disciplined standpoint, not the elimination of standpoint. Committed historians "broken through to new perceptions with their gladiatorial posture": Eduard Bernstein's Marxism made the overlooked Diggers visible to him where Firth and Gardiner had missed them. The danger ("tunnel vision") is real, and the corrective is self-discipline ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v4-ch3-agendas-high-and-low]]). In this reading, objectivity and identification are two settings of one relation between a writing self and the other minds it writes about. It is the volume's central problem of revealing and concealing, moved from the diarist's own self to the historian's and novelist's relation to others. Victorian biography's "homemade commonsensical psychology," which rushed through childhood "with a virtually audible sigh of relief," marks how little theory the century had for managing either pole ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v4-ch3-heroes-and-antiheroes]]).

## Hedges carried from the notes

- Ranke's method "closely rhymes" with [[einfuhlung]], but Ranke framed it as a religious calling, a "secular priest['s]" duty, rather than Herder's more secular hermeneutics. [[justus-moser]]'s *Zeitstil* pluralism is the same principle in miniature ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v4-ch3-ranke]]).
- The archive as an instrument of "pure perception" resonates with [[legibility-as-machine-core]], with qualification: it applies the modernity machine's faith in documentation to render the *past* transparent ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v4-ch3-ranke]]). The move to footnoted, impersonal prose is a local instance of [[Legibility]], but what is made legible is a discipline's own authority claim, not a population ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v4-ch3-price-of-professionalism]]).
- The assembling chairs, journals and congresses read like a [[history-machine]], a paradigm arriving "by accretion rather than argument" ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v4-ch3-price-of-professionalism]]).
- The nationalist classroom instances [[imagined-communities]], though Gay's evidence is curricular where [[benedict-anderson]] stresses print capitalism ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v4-ch3-agendas-high-and-low]]).
- Identification rhymes with [[self-construction-through-writing]], but the self built (and at the limit unmade) is a professional persona, not the diarist's personal self ([[gay-bourgeois-experience-v4-ch3-price-of-professionalism]]).
