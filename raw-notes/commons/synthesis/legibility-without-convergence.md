# Legibility Without Convergence

[[legibility-as-machine-core]] treats making the world readable, measurable, and orderable as the [[modernity-machine]]'s central optimization — and treats convergence as what legibility is *for*: uniform method, standard units, one path, everyone eventually in the same place. Manuel's prophets ([[manuel-prophets-of-paris]]) supply an unusually clean natural experiment against that coupling, because four of them build the apparatus in convergence's service and the fifth builds the same apparatus and points it the other way.

## Four counting machines aimed at one endpoint

The convergent versions are familiar and, taken together, remarkably complete:

- **Condorcet** dissolves genius from a rare endowment into a statistical proportion of an educated population, so that instruction mechanically multiplies "productive scientists" ([[manuel-prophets-of-paris-condorcet-01]]); then proposes a world organization of dispersed observation posts on uniform method with decennial reporting, and a *mathematique sociale* under which the state could run "by social mathematics... without debates" ([[manuel-prophets-of-paris-condorcet-02]]).
- **Saint-Simon** grounds three mutually exclusive social classes in Bichat's physiology, so that a person's station is readable off their natural endowment ([[manuel-prophets-of-paris-saint-simon-01]]).
- **The Saint-Simonians** make that reading an administrative procedure: aptitude "clearly definable by a hierarchy of experts" who assign each person an "optimum spot," a "cubbyhole in the perfect system," with a single directing bank weighing the credit needs of every branch of industry against a central budget of the totality of annual products ([[manuel-prophets-of-paris-children-02]]).
- **Comte** extends classification to the whole of knowledge and time — the law of three states correlated with three modes of activity and three ages of humanity, a hierarchy of sciences, a calendar assigning a figure to every day — with even colonized peoples required to pass through identical stages, merely at different rates ([[manuel-prophets-of-paris-comte-02]]).

In every case the counting is justified by the destination. The population is measured *because* it is going somewhere, and the measurement proves it is on schedule.

## Fourier counts just as hard, and refuses the destination

Fourier's psychology is at least as classificatory as anything above. Twelve passions arranged as a tree with unityism at the trunk and luxurious, group, and serial branches; beyond them innumerable "manias" pinned to ratios as fine as 1 in 810 and 1 in 100,000; 810 fundamental character types; a phalansterian card catalogue matching travelers to partners; horoscopes forecasting a child's future manias precisely enough to catch a future monster before he reaches a throne ([[manuel-prophets-of-paris-fourier-02]]). That is total actuarial transparency of the interior — a legibility project aimed at desire itself, and more invasive than anything Condorcet proposed.

What it is *for* is the opposite of convergence. Fourier holds the passions constant across every century, country, and legislator — "nothing can vary in my theory" — and holds that repression never destroys a passion but deforms it into a "counterpassion... as malignant as the natural passion would have been benign." The catalogue therefore exists to guarantee that no type goes ungratified: the bloodthirsty man is routed to the hunt, the future Nero to a butcher's trade, the sadist's cruelty into passionate friendship. Where the Saint-Simonians sort people into an optimum spot in order to *correct* them, Fourier sorts them in order to make correction unnecessary — and Manuel makes the doctrinal fork explicit in Fourier's polemics against them: a moral revolution remaking human nature toward obedience, versus a social combinatorics rich enough to absorb every deviation without correction. His arithmetic of abundance points the same way: thirty-seven million future Homers, Newtons, and Molières on a fully populated globe, scarcity of genius being an artifact of repression rather than of nature ([[manuel-prophets-of-paris-fourier-03]]). The ball-ritual maxim generalizes it into an epistemology — "the complex is always just and true, the simple always false."

## The claim

No single note argues this, but placed side by side the units make it hard to avoid: **measurement and convergence are separable, and in Paris between 1790 and 1840 they came apart.** The exhaustive catalogue, the statistical population, the expert assignment of persons to slots — these are neutral instruments with respect to the telos they serve. Condorcet and the Saint-Simonians and Comte use them to compress human variety toward one path; Fourier uses them to guarantee that variety is never compressed at all. What differs is not the technique but the answer to *what the counting is evidence for*.

The corollary matters for the frame. If legibility is the [[modernity-machine]]'s core optimization and variety-with-proliferation is the [[divergence-machine]]'s, then Fourier is a case of one machine's signature technique already running in service of the other's signature goal, decades before the second machine is usually dated as switched on. Two readings are available and the notes do not settle between them:

1. **Legibility is substrate, not telos.** Classification is infrastructure that either machine can build on, and the Modernity Machine's association with convergence is historical rather than necessary. This would suggest [[legibility-as-machine-core]] is naming a *capability* that the Modernity Machine happened to monopolize, not a value it owns.
2. **Fourier's variety is a convergence in disguise.** His 810 types are a closed enumeration, his harmony a single cosmic destination, and his phalanstery admits deviation only in forms his catalogue already anticipated. On this reading he is not divergent at all but a modernist whose universal path happens to be wide.

The second reading has real support in the material: as the reading note on Fourier's last years puts it ([[manuel-prophets-of-paris-fourier-03]]), his aim was cosmic harmony rather than resistance to standardization as such. But the first has the sharper diagnostic payoff, because it predicts something checkable elsewhere in the corpus: that Dawn-phase [[divergence-machine]] activity should be recognizable less by an absence of measurement than by measurement pointed at proliferation.

## A note on where each lands on the striving self

The fork shows up again at the level of the individual. Comte makes "Personality" an "odious word" that must "merge in the totality of existence" rather than flower ([[manuel-prophets-of-paris-comte-02]]) — about as explicit a program for suppressing [[Conatus]] as a nineteenth-century system offers, and a useful negative definition for [[capture-resistance]]. Fourier's premise is the inverse: passions are drives that must be given absolute free expression, and "as soon as we wish to repress a single passion we are engaged in an act of insurrection against God" ([[manuel-prophets-of-paris-fourier-02]]). Two systems built out of the same classificatory materials, arriving at opposite verdicts on whether the striving individual should be averaged into the whole.
