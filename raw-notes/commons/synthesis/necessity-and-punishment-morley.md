# Necessity and punishment (Morley)

This synthesis draws on Volume II of [[john-morley]]'s [[diderot-and-the-encyclopaedists]] ([[morley-diderot-v2]]). No single note states its claim; it connects arguments made in separate chapters.

## The local arguments

- **Holbach's treatise.** In the System of Nature, praise, blame, reward and punishment are tied to the effect of conduct on common welfare: forces acting on motives, as one dams or diverts a stream. Morley rates the demolition of free will one of the book's most effective parts and adds his own qualification, that capital punishment aims at other people's minds, not the offender's. Necessarian theory should make us act against bad training and institutions rather than against the individual. He credits the humane treatment of insanity and certain crimes to men like Pinel, trained in eighteenth-century materialism. Yet he warns that Holbach has emptied praise and blame of their old content without noticing, and he calls for a readjustment of ethical vocabulary to man as an object of science ([[morley-diderot-v2-ch06-holbach-system-of-nature-2]]). He also faults Holbach for treating conscience as a personal calculation instead of something the past imposes, which he thinks inconsistent with Holbach's own doctrine of necessity ([[morley-diderot-v2-ch06-holbach-system-of-nature-3]]).
- **Helvétius's self-love.** Morley concedes that deterrent penal law presupposes Helvétius's doctrine of self-love, and quotes Charles Comte on graded resentment and gratitude as needed for self-preservation, while insisting that sympathy deserves a place ([[morley-diderot-v2-ch05-helvetius-2]]).
- **D'Alembert's Dream.** In Diderot's 1769 dialogues, Bordeu (Diderot's mouthpiece) recasts virtue as beneficence and vice as maleficence, remorse as a product of ignorance or vanity, and rewards and punishments as “means of correcting the modifiable being”. Morley does not endorse the view and is sharply censorious of the form (“the most ugly and disgusting piece in the literature of philosophy”), while granting its sincerity and dialectical force ([[morley-diderot-v2-ch09-conclusion-2]]).
- **Rameau's Nephew.** He explains his moral deafness by fibre, bad company and “the paternal molecule”: education merely crosses inclination. The materialist idiom is the circle's, but it is placed in a cynic's mouth, and I does not accept his conclusions about morals ([[morley-diderot-v2-appendix-rameaus-nephew-5]]; see [[rameaus-nephew]]).

## The connection

Set side by side, these four places show the same premise (conduct is necessitated by organisation, training and circumstance) arriving at the same practical conclusion: sanctions are instruments for modifying motives, not retribution for free acts. Holbach states it as doctrine, Helvétius's self-love supplies its psychology, Bordeu states it as dialogue, and the nephew turns its physiology into an alibi.

Morley's response divides along a line the notes make visible only together. Where the premise points toward institutions (reform training and laws, treat the insane humanely, aim punishment at deterrence), he approves, in Holbach's case warmly. Where it is voiced as a frank dissolution of moral desert in dialogue, he either recoils (the Dream) or, in the nephew's case, the dialogue itself withholds assent. His one consistent reservation runs across both: the doctrine leaves the old vocabulary of praise, blame and conscience standing without its old content, and needs either a readjusted ethical language or a historical account of how conscience is inherited ([[morley-diderot-v2-ch06-holbach-system-of-nature-2]], [[morley-diderot-v2-ch06-holbach-system-of-nature-3]]). The notes do not say whether Morley's distaste for the Dream is about its doctrine or only its form, so this synthesis does not decide that either.

## World Machines resonances, with the notes' hedges

Holbach's claim that every agent in a revolution is as necessary as each molecule in a storm bears on determinist readings of history, with the notes' caveat that it is a metaphysical claim, not a forecasting method ([[morley-diderot-v2-ch06-holbach-system-of-nature-2]]). Necessitarian matter belongs to the [[enlightenment-monism]] strand, with [[baruch-spinoza]] as an older lineage, though the notes call both fits partial ([[morley-diderot-v2-ch06-holbach-system-of-nature-2]], [[morley-diderot-v2-ch09-conclusion-2]]).
