# La Selva Oscura (part 4 of 5)

The dark forest has carried three senses in this essay — a psychological condition, a cosmic predicament, and now a cosmology — and the work in each case is the same: don’t retreat, grow. The dark forest is currently our society’s most dominant cosmology.17 Read pessimistically — if your main value is not changing — it is a counsel of despair: the universe is dangerous, the comfortable mediations have failed, hide. But the same cosmology, read as provocation, says something else. The work is not to retreat into a smaller cosmology but to grow into the larger one. Even the aspiration to look beyond what is comfortable and at hand is itself a form of cosmic self-assertion — a refusal of the door-slamming, a small step toward the wider consciousness the dark forest is asking for. Liu wrote at the end of Death’s End: the ultimate fate of all intelligent beings has always been to become as grand as their thoughts.

Coda: A Note for Contraptions Book Club Readers

This essay’s framework fits within the World Machines thesis (which grew out of the Contraptions Book Club). The framework runs on a Dawn / Day / Dusk lifecycle, with three machines coexisting at any given moment. The Modernity Machine (Dawn c. 1200–1600, Day 1600–2000, now Dusk) was the cookie-cutter map at civilizational scale — legibility, convergence, canonicity, the straight path to the Enlightenment future. The Gramsci Gap — the “time of monsters,” the interregnum between the rules and rulers of an old world and a new one — is the selva oscura at civilizational scale, the period when the question persists and the answer does not. The Divergence Machine (Dawn 1600–2000 on the margins of the MM, Day 2000–c. 2400 on the ~400-year Day estimate) is the wider consciousness at civilizational scale — pluralism, coexistence without closure, the trackless territory walked rather than crossed. (A still-Dawning Liveness Machine is the one after that; out of scope here.)

Read this way, the personal midlife of the writer (me, awakening in COVID) and the species-scale midlife at the end of the Modernity Machine (all of us, around now) are the same developmental crisis at different scales of magnification. Liu’s developmental sequence (cradle → toddler → flight) and the world machine sequence (MM Day → Dusk while DM Dawn → Day) are doing similar work. The discipline the Divergence Machine asks of us is the discipline vanaprastha asks for: presence over flight, bearable proximity, work on myth, opening the door a second time.
