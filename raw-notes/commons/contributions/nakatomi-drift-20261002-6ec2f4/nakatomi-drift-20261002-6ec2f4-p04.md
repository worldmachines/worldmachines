# Nakatomi Drift (part 4 of 5)

Tuan’s statement that Place is produced through a pause in movement is especially important here. Without pausing to name, sketch, write, or model what you encounter, you do not create a new Place. You merely wander. So productive getting lost requires alternating drift and arrest. Too much drift and nothing consolidates. Too much arrest and the thicket becomes an encyclopedia, or perhaps an overbearing LLM-wiki.

In retrospect, this is why essays became a critical part of the Protocol Institute Psychohistory special interest group (SIGPSY). An essay is not simply a report from the journey. It is the temporary Place made by stopping long enough to discover where you have arrived.

Why do this? #

Because AI is very good at navigating the official architecture. It can take the elevator to the right floor, summarize what is there, and propose reasonable connections to the neighbouring rooms. That is useful. It is also exactly why the human advantage may lie elsewhere: in choosing strange entrances, remaining exposed to the wrong material, and noticing affordances that no itinerary asked for.

The point is not to refuse maps. It is to stop mistaking the map for the limits of movement.

A working vocabulary #

Place – A stabilized centre of meaning and value that offers a particular set of intellectual actions.

Space – The agent-relative field of moves currently perceived as possible.

Affordance – A move made possible by the encounter between a particular thinker and a particular Place.

Portal – A configuration of Places that changes the thinker’s reachable Space.

Thicket – A portal composed of densely entangled Places whose interactions generate unintended routes.

Dérive – A mode of traversal that suspends predetermined destinations and follows local intellectual gradients.

Nakatomi Drift – Traversal through an unintended affordance: using the infrastructure of an idea rather than its official entrance.

Feyerabendian move – A sub-concept of the Nakatomi Drift: the deliberate violation of a dominant navigational rule to disclose terrain that the rule renders inaccessible.

Prediction – The projection of a future from possibilities that can already be represented in the present.

Anticipation – Attentive movement into a future whose path and relevant possibilities are still taking form.

Not-knowing – Openness to a world in formation, rather than the absence of an answer presumed to be complete elsewhere.

New idea – A newly stabilized Place produced by traversing a route not represented in the previous map.

The deepest consequence is that originality is not necessarily a property of an idea considered alone. It may be a property of the route by which Places were made adjacent.

A thicket does not manufacture originality by adding more content. It constructs conditions in which the thinker can move through content in ways that existing maps do not anticipate.

The purpose of the thicket is not to contain the unknown. It is to make unanticipated movement possible.

Coda #

The Space and Place vocabulary, and the formula “Place is security, Space is freedom,” come from Yi-Fu Tuan’s Space and Place: The Perspective of Experience (1977). This essay is a direct continuation of The Birth of Thickets, which described the thicket as an emergent niche and a portal of curated Places opening onto an interior that remains Space. The move here is from the ontology of the thicket to the practice of traversing it.

Psychogeography and the dérive come from Guy Debord and the Situationist International. Debord’s method belongs to the city; using it as a practice for moving through literatures, theories, and intellectual ambiances is my extension.

The methodological permission comes from Paul Feyerabend’s Against Method. “Anything goes” here does not mean that every belief or method is equally true. It means that no method should be allowed to legislate in advance every route by which knowledge may arrive. The separation between permissive route generation and later testing, selection, and cultivation is mine.

“Nakatomi Space” is Geoff Manaugh’s, from his 2010 BLDGBLOG essay of that name: architecture traversed through ventilation shafts, maintenance systems, unfinished cavities, and other routes excluded from its official plan. The Nakatomi Drift – and the translation of those infrastructural passages into the latent topology of a body of ideas – is mine.

The distinction between prediction and anticipation, intention and attention, ignorance and not-knowing, as well as the paired movements of exposure and attunement, come from Tim Ingold’s “On not knowing and paying attention: How to walk in a possible world”. His walker falls forward into exposure and recovers through attunement. I have treated that alternation as the temporal engine of the thicket.
