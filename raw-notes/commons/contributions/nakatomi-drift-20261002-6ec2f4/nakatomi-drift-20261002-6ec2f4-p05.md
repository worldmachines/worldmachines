# Nakatomi Drift (part 5 of 5)

The unknown as something more than missing information comes from Rebecca Solnit’s A Field Guide to Getting Lost. The formulation that getting lost is the unfamiliar appearing – the world becoming larger than your knowledge of it – is hers, encountered again through Maria Popova’s essay on the book. Solnit gives the thicket its terra incognita: not an empty region awaiting completion, but a condition in which discovery remains possible.

A more immediate ancestor is Venkatesh Rao’s “How to Take Your Brain Off-Road”. Rao’s disorderly reader becomes lost by no longer knowing how to read, navigates the intertwingled territory of ideas by gut feel, and resists the maps that accumulated knowledge makes too easy to follow. The Nakatomi Drift is my attempt to give that off-road reader a spatial vocabulary, an architecture, and a method of movement.
