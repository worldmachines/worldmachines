# The Birth of Thickets (part 2 of 3)

The Dark Forest Theory of the internet suggests something similar, but I propose that the condition is a counterfeit, and easy to mistake for the real thing. The dark forest is a Place dressed as Space. I have described that retreat before as a return to the village, but a village built inside a bunker: hidden, bounded, unfree, with someone always at the door counting who goes in. A member-owned working group and an influencer’s private channel present identical surfaces, both opaque to outsiders, both thick with in-jokes, both perfectly clear about who belongs. What separates them is the direction the energy flows. Who pays the energy cost, and who captures the value? In the working group the energy stays with the people spending it. In the channel it is harvested out to the one person who owns the door, and what he hands back is neither freedom nor security. The platform runs that operation at scale, a container that promises you a world of your own and returns a legible slice of everyone’s, which is what the Dusk apparatus produces when it gets hold of divergence.

The Day Machine cut a new niche out of that same excess, the way the machine before it was undone by its own success at counting. While averaging is partial, illegibility is a luxury and a mild handicap: you give up reach to keep your shape. Once averaging goes total, illegibility becomes the only ground on which a shape can still stand, and people have started living on it. Liveness is the machine at Dawn. Nobody builds a Dawn machine; it turns on wherever something has begun running that the older logic cannot get hold of, and that is happening inside thickets. Whatever the next era turns out to be, its first working parts are running there now.

Thing is though, you cannot start a thicket, because founding is the operation. Write a charter, draw a boundary, keep a roster, and each of those acts converts Space into Place, so a thicket founded on purpose resolves quickly in favour of the plain. Decline to convert and what remains is a thicket. Allow the fence to rot, and what grows in the clearing is not something you planted.

So it arrives as a calling. The niche opens, some fascination you cannot put down turns out to fit it, and what hears the call is The Octotypic Mind: the distinct terroir of minds. Nobody convened the people who could not leave the dying web alone. The web began dying, which opened a niche for a kind of attention that had nowhere else to go, and the people already shaped for it walked in without ever deciding to.

Interregnums are usually described in time. The old machine is dying, the new one cannot yet be born, and what breeds in the interval is morbid: rulers who have stopped making sense, rulebooks growing faster than anyone can read them, institutions elaborating themselves well past the point of use. Monsters, in the oldest sense of the word, which meant an omen that something had gone wrong. But an interregnum in time can be swapped for an interregnum in space. You do not have to wait out the gap if you can go and stand where it has already closed, and that is what the habitat offers over the calendar.

A second kind of monster is waiting there. In the folkloric sense a monster is not evil, only reality reworking itself, the unfinished business of a world that was never fully articulated. It shows up where a form holds more possibility than it can contain and the form gives way. What opens is a crack, and a crack is not a gap or a lack or a hole wanting to be filled. It is surplus.

So the same excess sorts two ways. Read back into an index by the old apparatus, it turns morbid. Left unread, it splits the form and what comes through reworks whatever it touches. You cannot live inside a crack, since nothing keeps its shape at that range. You can only build alongside one, and thicketing is exactly that: staying near enough to be reworked, sheltered enough to survive it, and declining every invitation to say what the opening is for. The people who manage this tend to seem slightly monstrous and roaring with Liveness.

As with life, Liveness breaks rules. A Day is supposed to last four centuries, and machine lifespans have been shortening for as long as there have been machines to measure. Liveness is switching on inside thickets faster than the framework’s clock allows for, which would make the era only now beginning a short one, and would bring the Divergence Machine to its own Dusk well before the four centuries are up.

A thicket cannot be your target. Thicketing is the act of noticing, dwelling, and thriving in an emergent niche. But you don’t build a thicket; you give attention to the arrangement of the universe that has opened up a Space.
