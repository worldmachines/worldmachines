# Chapter Twenty: The Yellow-Robe Demon of Casserole Mountain

With Sun Wukong banished back to Flower-Fruit Mountain, Tripitaka presses west with only Pigsy and Sandy. Pigsy's hunger and a stolen nap leave Tripitaka alone in a forest, where he wanders off course and is captured by an indigo-faced demon, Yellow-Robe, who intends to eat him along with his disciples. Tripitaka is saved not by combat but by the demon's wife, Princess Baihuaxiu of Precious Image — herself a captive of thirteen years — who fabricates a divine dream-vow to talk her husband into freeing him, then sends a letter with him to her royal parents. At the city of Precious Image, the king stamps Tripitaka's travel papers, is undone by his daughter's letter, and drafts Pigsy and Sandy to rescue her. Their first assault fails (saved only by Guanyin's hidden guardian spirits), and when they return for a second round without that protection, Pigsy's bragging curdles into cowardice: he ducks into a bush mid-fight, and Yellow-Robe captures Sandy, closing the unit on a cliffhanger.

## Themes

**Monkey's absence exposes the party's real fragility.** Without Wukong, competence collapses into ordinary human failure: Pigsy falls asleep on an errand, loses track of time, and later cannot sustain a fight he and Sandy "had only managed to fight Yellow-Robe to a stalemate" during even with divine backup. The chapter quietly argues that the pilgrimage's earlier successes depended on one disproportionately powerful member, not the group's collective competence.

**Comic bathos runs directly through mortal danger.** Pigsy's inflated vow to "draw fire from ice and oil from snow," his self-pitying comparison of Tripitaka to a child, and his invented excuse for napping are played for laughs even as Tripitaka is genuinely about to be eaten a few pages later. This tonal whiplash is sustained to the end, where Pigsy's battlefield desertion ("Need the toilet... You carry on") is both a coward's punchline and the direct cause of Sandy's capture.

**Captivity is answered with cunning, not conquest.** Baihuaxiu, "kidnapped" by Yellow-Robe exactly as Tripitaka now is, does not free him by force or appeal to justice — she invents a dream and a childhood vow, manipulating the very affection that binds her captor to her. The rescue-by-deception mirrors the demon's own trickery (his lie about "human dumplings" to bait Pigsy) and briefly makes her, rather than either disciple, the chapter's most effective actor.

**Bureaucratic legibility persists beneath the supernatural plot.** Even a chapter about goblins and shape-shifting monks runs on paperwork: the emperor's travel document is presented, read, and stamped with a jade seal before any monster business is addressed, and the princess's letter is recognized as authentic by her parents purely through its content and the act of writing itself, not any physical proof of her survival. Identity and legitimacy travel by document and seal rather than by presence — a small but genuine echo of documentary [[legibility-as-machine-core]] running under the fantastic surface.

## Notable passages

Pigsy's sleepy self-comparison to Monkey ("When Monkey was still with us, he always managed to keep Tripitaka happy...") sets up the chapter's core irony: his very awareness of his own inadequacy is what causes it, as the passage ends with him dozing off entirely. The description of Yellow-Robe asleep in the pagoda ("an indigo-faced monster... a gleaming sword") plays against genre expectation — the ornate, beautiful setting hides horror the same way the demon's polite interrogation of Tripitaka masks cannibalism. Baihuaxiu's confession of her own thirteen-year captivity ("I'm the third daughter of the king of Precious Image...") reframes the demon's household as a mirror of Tripitaka's predicament, and her fabricated dream-vow ("While asleep just now, I dreamed of a god in golden armor...") is the chapter's real turning point, resolving the crisis through domestic manipulation rather than any disciple's strength. The princess's letter, read aloud at court ("Your unfilial daughter Hundred Flowers' Shame touches her head to the ground..."), is the emotional center of the Precious Image scenes, converting the court's skepticism into a rescue mission. Finally, Pigsy's mid-battle flight ("Need the toilet," Pigsy told Sandy. "You carry on.") delivers the chapter's parting reversal, undoing the earlier rescue by costing Sandy his freedom.
