# The Pilgrims Reach the Buddha and Are Charged for the Scriptures

After fourteen years and a final week's travel, Tripitaka, Monkey, Pigsy, and Sandy arrive at Thunderclap Monastery on Soul Mountain; they cross a bottomless-boat river that strips Tripitaka of his mortal body, are received by the Buddha in the Great Hero Hall, and are handed three baskets of scriptures — first blank, wordless scrolls, because they bring the Buddha's own attendants no gift, and only the written canon after Tripitaka surrenders his golden alms bowl as payment, with Guanyin closing the frame narrative by auditing the pilgrimage's length against a preordained number of days.

## Themes

- **Enlightenment as transaction, not free grace.** The Buddha's household openly extorts payment for scripture even from the emperor's chosen pilgrims, and the Buddha himself ratifies the practice rather than condemning it — corruption is folded into the sacred order, not opposed by it.

- **The crossing as literal death and rebirth.** The "bottomless boat" scene stages apotheosis directly: Tripitaka watches his own corpse float past and is told it is his, dramatizing the doctrine that reaching the far shore means shedding the mortal senses rather than merely completing a march.

- **The wordless scriptures as a failed test of understanding.** The Buddha of the Archive deliberately palms off blank scrolls on the pilgrims, reasoning that they are "too dim" to grasp that unwritten scripture is "just as good and true" as written — a joke that doubles as real doctrine (truth beyond language) which the pilgrims, and the waiting emperor, cannot accept on its own terms.

- **Contempt folded into salvation.** The Buddha's catalogue of Chinese "greed, violence, licentiousness, bullying, and trickery," and his verdict that the whole country deserves damnation, sits uneasily beside the pilgrimage's stated purpose of delivering them from exactly that fate, and is immediately followed by his own attendants shaking the pilgrims down for a bribe.

- **Numerological closure of the quest.** Guanyin's precise count — 5,040 days completed, eight short of "the perfect canonical number of 5,048" — reveals the entire pilgrimage as an audited, already-scripted ledger; the Buddha corrects the shortfall on the spot by dispatching eight attendants for exactly eight days, turning what read as open trial into settled arithmetic.

## Notable passages

The unit opens on comic anticlimax: after fourteen years of Tripitaka asking how much farther, Monkey announces they have arrived and Tripitaka tumbles off his horse in shock — deflating the pilgrimage's solemnity at the very moment of arrival.

The crossing itself is the unit's central image: unable to walk the single-log bridge or cheat with cloud-travel, the pilgrims take a boat with "no base," piloted by the Guiding Buddha in disguise; when a corpse floats past, Monkey tells the terrified Tripitaka, "Don't be afraid. It's you," and the group reaches "the other shore, breaking free of their mortal senses."

The Buddha's long indictment of China — its people "disloyal, unfilial, mendacious, immoral, unkind, and cruel," doomed to be "pounded, pestled, pounded some more" in Hell — sets an oddly hostile frame around the gift of "three baskets of scriptures" he is ostensibly overjoyed to bestow.

The bribery episode is the unit's comic engine: the attendants demand a "treat," the empty-handed pilgrims are fobbed off with scrolls that turn out to be entirely blank, and only after Tripitaka hands over the Tang emperor's alms bowl do they receive the full, written canon of 5,048 scrolls — with the Buddha explicitly defending the shakedown as the cosmic norm ("you don't get something for nothing... and certainly not scriptures of salvation").

The closing exchange between Guanyin and the Buddha reframes the whole preceding adventure as a scheduling problem: the mission is "accomplished" only once the day-count is patched to hit exactly 5,048, and eight warrior-attendants are given eight days to fly the pilgrims home and back, ending the unit on pilgrims "buoyant with enlightenment" mid-flight.
