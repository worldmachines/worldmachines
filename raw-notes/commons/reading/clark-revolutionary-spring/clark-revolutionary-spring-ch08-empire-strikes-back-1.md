# The Habsburg Counter-Stroke, Summer 1848: Prague, Lombardy and Vienna

Clark follows the Habsburg monarchy's recovery of initiative between February and late August 1848 across three theatres. In Prague, news of Paris arrives at a costume ball and sets off a familiar pattern of meetings and demand lists; a moderate, Czech-dominated committee briefly outfaces the viceroy Stadion, but the radicalization of students and workers in June meets Windischgrätz's guns in the six-day June Uprising, after which guards, committees and clubs are dissolved. In northern Italy, Radetzky's disciplined army, combined with terror and targeted concessions, exploits Italian disunity and ends in Charles Albert's armistice after Custoza. In Vienna, the liberal ministry cuts the wages of the public-works 'earthworkers', women and workers demonstrate, National Guards sabre the crowd at the Praterstern on 23 August, and the Committee of Safety dissolves. Clark reads the last as a breach in the structure of the revolution, a muted echo of the Paris June Days.

## Themes

**Instability built in from the start.** The Prague movement was Czech in a city with a largely German ruling class, and its liberal leaders ignored or feared the workers of the suburbs (the bourgeoisie saw them as an underclass to be 'held in check by coercion, not reform'). Radical demands, such as the 'organization of labour and wages', were toned down while the nationalism was dialled up. Guards and legions were split along German/Czech and class lines. This is Clark's fissuring of the liberal-radical coalition, and of the national question, in miniature.

**A counter-revolution that is not centrally commanded.** Windischgrätz acts independently of Vienna, withdraws and recommits, and refuses to lift the state of siege at the government's request. Clark reads this as evidence of how well the 'loose constellation of powers' making up the Habsburg system kept functioning under 'semi-acephalous conditions'. The empire's capacity to respond was distributed, not a matter of a single directing centre. This is a useful rhyme with the [[history-machine]] idea of assembly from independent components, though Clark makes no such claim and the analogy is ours.

**Repression works through paper and surveillance as well as guns.** After 17 June come searches, opened letters, a flood of denunciations, purged guards, dissolved committees and press monitoring; the absurd fate of the Votice guard (wooden rifles, a clay one, a banner returned to service as a weathervane) shows how thin the revolutionary institutions were. This bears loosely on legibility (the state making participants readable to itself; compare [[legibility-as-machine-core]]), though Clark's interest here is the comic and human texture.

**Concession as a weapon of counter-revolution.** In Lombardy-Venetia Radetzky's atrocity at Castelnuovo is paired with Hartig's proclamations promising tax cuts, more municipal powers and liberation of 'the good peasantry' from agitators. Clark generalizes: conceding key revolutionary demands was part of the counter-revolution's armoury. This is the seed of his later argument that counter-revolution was modernizing rather than merely restorative, though this unit only states the tactical point.

**Italian disunity: volunteers versus regulars, republicans versus monarchists, and an exogenous shock.** Coordination failures, arms shortages, regular commanders' contempt for irregulars, Charles Albert's dynastic calculus, popular tribunes such as the miller Zoia who alarmed the propertied, and Pius IX's Allocution of 29 April all eroded cohesion. The Pope's move removed a 'generative illusion' of the movement and drove Catholics away from the national cause. Compare [[nationalism]] for the general problem of how a national cause holds heterogeneous interests together, though Clark's story here is of one failing.

**Vienna: the Social Question splits moderates from the street.** The property-owning moderates in government, including democrats such as Schwarzer and Bach, run out of willingness to fund public works for the poor, echoing French republican impatience with the Parisian workshops. The wage cuts of 18 and 21 August, the first political demonstration by women in Austrian history, the carnivalesque 'Kreutzer-Minister' funeral, the sabre charge, and the passivity of the Academic Legion and Committee of Safety show the radical leadership decoupling from the workers it claimed to represent. Workers who carried the 'flags of 26 May' treated the revolution as 'a process, and not as an event'.

**Women appear as political actors, with condescension attached.** The 400-strong women's meeting in Prague (praised by Wawra with 'a mixture of enthusiasm and condescension') and the Viennese women workers' march are both firsts. Clark keeps the contemporaries' voices distinct from his own reading.

## Notable passages

**c1, Prague's built-in instability.** The passage sets out the Czech-German, moderate-radical and bourgeois-proletarian fault lines in the earliest organs of the revolution, including the unusual worker share (10 per cent) of the Svornost guard.

**c2, Windischgrätz's independence.** Clark's argument that the Habsburg system continued to function 'under the semi-acephalous conditions of the summer of 1848', with casualty figures (43 insurgents killed) and the participation of women.

**c3, Radetzky and Hartig.** Terror and promise combined; the concession of revolutionary demands as a counter-revolutionary weapon.

**c4, Italian internal ailments.** Republican splits, pop-up folk leaders who were 'despots of liberty' to the well-off, and the Papal Allocution shattering a generative illusion.

**c5, The Viennese wage cuts.** The Social Question puts governmental moderates and street radicals on opposite sides, as in Paris.

**c6, 23 August.** Clark's reading of the Praterstern violence as the first serious defeat, a breach in the revolution's structure, and a muted repeat of the Paris June Days. He quotes the Wiener Gassenzeitung and Rosenfeld for the contemporary sense of a turning point; the 'phalanx against property' is the journal's projection, not Clark's finding.
