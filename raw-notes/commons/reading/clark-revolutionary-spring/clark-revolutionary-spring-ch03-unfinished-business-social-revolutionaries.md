# From the paving stone to the Society of Seasons: the social left after 1830

Clark opens with a satirical newspaper fable, the 'story of a paving stone' (La Glaneuse, Lyons, 1831), which says that the 1830 barricade stone that once protected the people against a king now protects a king against the people. He uses it to frame the disillusion of the left after July 1830. He then itemizes what 1830 did change (an elected king, a wider but still tiny franchise of 0.3 to 0.5 per cent of the population, a weakened Catholic settlement, a *Charte révisée*) and argues these were 'very liberal achievements' that offered nothing to those whose grievance was social. Strikes and the Lyons uprising of 1831 followed at once. The second half traces the new republican opposition: the Society of the Friends of the People, then the Society of the Rights of Man and the Citizen, then Blanqui, Barbès and Bernard's Society of Seasons. Clark follows this line through the insurrections of June 1832, April 1834 (with the rue Transnonain massacre and Daumier's lithograph) and May 1839. He closes by contrasting the prison memoirs of Silvio Pellico and Martin Bernard as evidence of a new kind of revolutionary self.

## Themes

**The political revolution that stalls before the social question.** Clark presents 1830 as the triumph of the juste milieu: real but incremental constitutional gains that expressed the worldview of an educated, propertied class. The fable's bitterness (the stone as memoirist, history 'stopped in its course') is offered as the mood of dissent after 1830, not as Clark's own verdict. The unit sets up the later fissure between liberals and radicals that Clark develops elsewhere. Compare the [[gramsci-gap]] only loosely: what is at stake here is a settlement that satisfies one class, not an interregnum in which no order holds.

**Radicalization through repression and the making of a conspiratorial left.** Each insurrection is followed by prosecution, a new association and a more radical successor. Clark traces the shift from the 'relatively mellow, bourgeois figures of 1830' to skilled workers preferring direct action, and then to a hard left whose agenda was 'less on the removal of monarchy' than on 'revolutionary social transformation'. The 1834 rising was planned rather than spontaneous, and the police response was faster. The Society of Seasons' week / month / season / year hierarchy, built to defeat informers and the Law on Associations, echoes the Carbonari. It is a case of a state's surveillance and association law shaping the form that opposition takes, though Clark does not put it that way in these terms.

**Isolation, dictatorship and the loss of the 'progressive' label.** Clark grants the prosecutor Mérilhou's point that these militants were socially isolated, and reads their conspiracy as born of the insight that they lacked the means to raise the masses. Their captured papers anticipated a 'dictatorial power' held by the 'smallest possible number of men'. Mérilhou, once a Carbonaro, now a pillar of the July establishment, recasts their programme as a social revolution and a return to 1793, which 'ousted the far left from the progressive historicity of the moderate liberals'. The fight over who owns the future is itself a stake. This has some kinship with [[intellectual-vs-revolutionary]] and with [[romanticism-and-political-danger]], though the fit is only partial because Clark's actors are organizers and prisoners, not theorists.

**Order as force.** Barbès's refusal to plead ('There are only questions of force') is read by Clark as an 'ontological radicalism' that collapses the court's normative claim into the violence of the regime. He adds that this view of political order as the interplay of force was for the moment a speciality of the left, and infiltrated liberal and conservative discourse only after 1848. That is a claim about a discursive shift, which sits near the WMP interest in legitimacy draining out of an institution, but it is a historian's observation and not a machine model.

**A new political self, and a transnational milieu.** The Pellico / Bernard comparison shows two ways of being a prisoner: Pellico's evacuation of politics and tender sympathy with his warders (the source of his success with readers) versus Bernard's disdain, silence and treatment of warders as wheels in an apparatus. Bernard attributes the gap to ideology: the 'flickering light of eternal rights' versus the 'sacred doctrine of equality'. Clark adds that by the later 1840s a German paper saw the 'cold and tranquil' communist as more dangerous than the old exaltado. Bernard's route from the Saint-Simonians ([[henri-de-saint-simon]] is only the school's precursor) through Fourier to Robespierre also shows how ideas moved. The exile networks (Polish, Italian, German) and the death of Ferrari, and the fate of 'Austen le Polonais', show a European, not merely French, milieu. The unit ends on Austen's breakdown and Bernard's recollection of him.

## Notable passages

**The paving stone (c1).** The full fable, with its inversion 'Once the paving stone protected the people against a king. Today it protects a king against the people.' It is the unit's organizing image of the gap between revolution's promise and its institutional outcome.

**What 1830 did and did not deliver (c2).** Clark's balance sheet of the July settlement and its class character, ending on the Lyons insurrection and the editor Granier's part in it.

**The Society of Seasons and its cells (c3).** The prosecution's reconstruction of the group's hierarchy from seized notebooks, and the aims of secrecy and evasion of the association law.

**Mérilhou's indictment (c4).** The turncoat Carbonaro's argument that this is a social, not a political, revolution, aimed at property and modelled on 1793.

**The trials and Barbès's silence (c5).** Using the court as a stage; Blanqui's and Barbès's refusals; Clark's reading of these as an 'ontological radicalism'.

**Pellico versus Bernard (c6).** The contrast of two prison-narrative selves and its link to ideology and to the coming 'cold' communist type.
