# Hooke as the Patron Saint of Cyberpunk

The text argues that Robert Hooke envisioned mechanized scientific reasoning not merely as a technical advancement, but as a political instrument for individual autonomy. By utilizing automated logical processes, thinkers could express and test ideas in a way that resists interference from "unjust authorities." This perspective frames Hooke as a foundational figure for a "cyberpunk" ethos—one that prioritizes technical tinkering and intellectual independence over institutionalized control.

## Themes

**Technological Autonomy and Resistance**
Central to the argument is the idea that automated reasoning provides a space for intellectual exploration that is resilient to external censorship. This resonates with the concept of [[capture-resistance]], where the machine acts as a buffer, allowing the individual's [[conatus]] to persist against the encroaching legibility and control of the [[modernity-machine]].

**The Tinkerer's Rebellion**
Hooke's personal history—specifically his tendency to bypass formal university lectures to tinker in Robert Boyle's laboratory—is presented as a rejection of institutionalized, standardized education. This preference for hands-on, experimental practice over formal curriculum mirrors a commitment to divergent, non-institutionalized learning paths.

## Notable passages

The author suggests that Hooke saw the utility of mechanized reasoning specifically as a means to "express, explore and test ideas freely" without fear of censorship. This subversive potential is what leads to the central claim: if Leibniz represents the orderly logic of cybernetics, Hooke represents its rebellious, countercultural edge, earning him the title of "patron saint of cyberpunk."
