# Cyberpunk Parallels in the 17th Century

The unit introduces a comparative historical thesis: the "high tech, urban sprawl, and do-it-yourself counterculture" characteristic of the cyberpunk genre were as fundamental to the 17th-century scientific revolution as they were to the 20th-century computer revolution. By drawing this comparison, the author suggests that the rapid progress of early modern science was driven by similar sociological and technological pressures.

## Themes

**The Cyberpunk Heuristic**
The author utilizes cyberpunk not merely as an aesthetic, but as a lens to describe the atmosphere of rapid scientific advancement. This implies a focus on decentralized, high-speed, and potentially chaotic "do-it-yourself" knowledge production that mirrors the subcultural drivers of the digital age.

**The Drive for Formalized Reason**
The text highlights the ambitions of Gottfried Leibniz to create a universal symbolic language. This drive to express all thought with the precision of arithmetic resonates with the [[legibility-as-machine-core]] of the [[modernity-machine]], as it seeks to make the internal processes of human reasoning as orderable, measurable, and scalable as geometric analysis.

## Notable passages

The argument is anchored by the connection between the philosophical ambitions of the 17th century and the emergence of modern cybernetics, specifically through the works of Leibniz and the endorsements of Norbert Wiener.
