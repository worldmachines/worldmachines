# The Racial Alibi: Colonial Violence and Its Critics, 1865–1912

This unit traces the "convenient Other" alibi for aggression through its colonial applications — Jamaica, German East Africa, the Congo Free State, German South-West Africa — and through the parallel, largely losing, intellectual campaign to discredit the racial pseudo-science that licensed them; it closes with a coda on the American South, where Jim Crow turns out to be a late-century invention rather than ancient custom.

## Themes

**Racial theory functioned as a portable alibi for violence across every empire in play.** Eyre's suppression of the 1865 Jamaica rebellion, Carl Peters's punitive expeditions in German East Africa, Leopold II's Congo Free State, and von Trotha's campaign against the Hereros in German South-West Africa are treated as instances of one pattern: a "higher race" doctrine that converted atrocity into duty and dissent into sentimentality.

**Imperial expansion dressed itself as sacred obligation, not appetite.** Manifest destiny, Kipling's *White Man's Burden*, and Josiah Strong's Anglo-Saxon Social-Darwinist creed cast domination as a civilizing mission owed by "higher" races to "lower" ones — Strong's explicit borrowing of Spencer's "survival of the fittest" for a coming "final competition of races" shows how easily race theory absorbed the competition alibi that runs alongside it elsewhere in the book.

**Even Eyre's prosecutors shared his racial premises.** The Jamaica Committee split British opinion into an Eyre Defense Committee and a prosecuting faction, but figures like Frederic Harrison, E. S. Beesly, and even Huxley — nominally on the anti-Eyre side — volunteered their own contempt for Black people; the fight was over the proper limits of aggression, not over racial hierarchy itself.

**Colonizers' violence answered narcissistic injury as much as economic calculation.** Peters and his contemporaries read native "insolence" as a personal affront requiring "manly energy" and "ruthless force" in response; Gay reads the display of manliness as reassurance the colonizer needed for himself, not only intimidation aimed outward — a variant of the manliness alibi running alongside the racial one.

**Atrocity at scale depended on domestic self-deception and, eventually, electoral ratification.** Belgians "cheerfully acquiesced" in the Congo Free State by simply not wanting to know what Leopold's agents did to secure ivory and rubber profits; von Trotha's explicit policy of extermination against the Hereros was not merely tolerated but affirmed when the German electorate returned the government's colonial policy at the polls in 1907, gutting the Social Democratic opposition that had questioned it.

**A century-long chain of skeptics dismantled the "science" of race without dislodging racism itself.** Bagehot, Hartmann, de Mortillet, William James, Josiah Royce, Salomon Reinach, William Graham Sumner, Jean Finot, and Anatole France all, independently, showed that "the Aryan race" and its cousins had no evidentiary basis; Franz Boas capped the sequence by showing empirically (via the cephalic index) that supposedly fixed racial traits shifted with environment across a single generation of immigrants. None of it mattered much in practice — Gay's diagnosis is that racist thinking survived because it "provided pleasure," and people do not easily surrender what pleases them. The racial pseudo-science at stake here is itself a project of sorting human populations into fixed, measurable, orderable categories in order to license domination — a use of classification that half-rhymes with [[legibility-as-machine-core]], though Gay's frame is the psychological wage of aggression rather than administrative control, and the sorting here serves domination rather than the state capacity Scott's legibility describes.

**Sumner's account of in-group/out-group morality anticipates Freud and gestures at how Other-making binds a group together.** "There are two codes of morals and two sets of mores, one for comrades inside and the other for strangers outside" — cohesion inside requires, and produces, hostility to outsiders, intensified rather than restrained by religion; Gay notes drily that "Freud would say it not much better."

**Jim Crow was a late invention retrofitted onto the South, not ancient custom.** Tocqueville had already observed that race prejudice ran stronger in free states than slave states; antebellum intimacy between the races made legal segregation "virtually unthinkable" before the 1880s–90s, and it took Northern acquiescence, the political defeat of Southern liberals like Cable and Blair, and *Plessy v. Ferguson* (1896) to nationalize what Woodward calls "permissions-to-hate."

## Notable passages

The Eyre affair's resolution is the unit's sharpest irony: Eyre escaped prosecution, but "the cause of understanding the racist alibi lost even more," since his own prosecutors — Harrison, Beesly, even Huxley — volunteered their own racial contempt in the same breath as their case against him; "the two contending parties had more in common than they thought."

Carl Peters supplies the clearest statement of colonial narcissistic injury: native "insolence" is experienced "as a dire narcissistic injury," and the colonizer's performance of "manly energy" and "ruthless force" reads, in Gay's analysis, as much as reassurance for the adventurer himself as intimidation of anyone else.

The Congo passage shows atrocity enabled by ordinary people's incuriosity rather than active cruelty: Belgians "could resort to the comfortable psychological mechanism of denial" about Leopold's Congo Free State, because, bluntly, "it was best not to know."

The Herero passage gives the unit's starkest body count: three-quarters of eighty thousand Hereros dead or exiled by 1906 under von Trotha's explicit aim "to destroy the rebellious tribes with streams of blood and streams of money" — a policy the German electorate rewarded rather than punished in 1907.

Sumner's double-code passage is the unit's clearest statement of the mechanism behind the alibi, not just an instance of it: cohesion "inside" is manufactured by hostility "outside," and religion — supposedly a restraint on violence — instead "intensified ethnocentrism."

Finot names the alibi outright: race science "gave our secret bloodthirstiness free scope" and "was, in a word, an alibi for the ugliest aggression" — a sentence that could stand as the thesis of the whole unit.
