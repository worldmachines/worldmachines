# Bruno's Cabala: the Egyptian Magus who kept the counters and changed the pattern

Yates opens from Pico's ideal Renaissance Magus, who joined Ficinian natural magic to practical Cabala and Pseudo-Dionysian angelic hierarchies, and asks what Bruno, already abnormal in his total "Egyptianism", does with the Cabalist half. Her answer runs through the *Cabala del Cavallo Pegaseo* and the Ass of negative theology, the Apuleian and Agrippan sources of that Ass, the magical treatises *De magia* and *De vinculis*, the Spaccio's Egyptian passage and its Cabalist sequel, the *Thirty Seals* and its four guides of religion, and ends with a bold, openly speculative comparison to Freemasonry. Throughout, Yates stresses that Bruno's material is second-hand (largely Agrippa's *De occulta philosophia*, copied out by Besler in the Noroff manuscript) and that he rearranges familiar counters into a new pattern.

## Themes

**Egyptianism over Cabala, but not total rejection.** In the *Cabala del Cavallo Pegaseo* Bruno appears to reject Cabala for Egyptian insight, consistent with his inverted history of the *prisca theologia* in which Egyptians are earliest and best, Jews and Christians later and worse. Yates argues he does not hold this consistently: just as he thought Egyptianism could ground a reform inside the Church, he retains something of Cabala. His new "cabala" is defined in his own words as a cabala of theological philosophy and a theology of philosophical cabala. Yates hedges: Bruno probably knew little Hebrew, and his Sephiroth, Names and angel orders come from Agrippa (possibly Reuchlin and Trithemius).

**The Ass and the sweeping-away of "metaphysics".** The mystical Nothing beyond the Sephiroth is symbolised by the Ass of negative theology. Yates does not think Bruno means to be blasphemous; the ass had been allegorised as humble negation. In the *Asino Cillenico* the "naturalissimo asino" belongs to a Pythagorean academy of the physical, with no metaphysics, and is the same as the Triumphant Beast of the Spaccio. Yates traces the figure to Apuleius's Golden Ass (Apuleius being the supposed translator of the *Asclepius*) and to Agrippa's *De vanitate*. Bruno thereby discards the Cabalist and Pseudo-Dionysian superstructure the Christian Magus built to guard natural magic against demons.

**Language, hieroglyphs and demonic magic.** In *De magia* Bruno follows Agrippa's three-world structure but omits the Hebrew language, Sephiroth and angel orders, and praises Egyptian hieroglyphs as "capturing the language of the gods". Yates connects this to Corpus Hermeticum XVI (Egyptian not to be translated into Greek) and to Bruno's war on "pedants". His magic is frankly demonic, working through "links", above all the magically excited imagination, the "link of links"; this is what his memory systems were for. Yates presents Bruno as the direct logical result of the Hermetic glorification of man as a divine Miracle, [[giordano-bruno]] taking the Pico-style logic in a direction Pico never intended.

**Cabala survives, naturalised.** Bruno keeps the Genesis-Pimander equation (Moses drawing on Egyptian wisdom) and, in the Spaccio, reads the Cabalist expansion of the Tetragrammaton into seventy-two angels as a scheme for unity in multiplicity, brought down from super-celestial metaphysics into the physical, and probably in practice as demon-summoning. The Egyptian planetary passage remains the fundamental statement of his outlook. Lullism is more important for his unifying aim but Yates sets it aside.

**Religion in the Thirty Seals and the disappearance of the Christian Magus.** Following Agrippa's Book III, Bruno's "contractions" and four guides (Love, Art, Mathesis, Magic, avoiding Agrippa's trinitarian three) shape the religious Magus. The Christian, Trinitarian side of the Renaissance Magus has vanished, though Christ remains a good Magus and Bruno feels solidarity with the Catholic Middle Ages. Yates cautions that he is not simply a late-antique Neoplatonist: the whole Ficino-Pico apparatus reached him.

**Toleration, good works and Freemasonry (speculative).** Against the religious intolerance of the sixteenth century, Bruno preaches an Egyptian Counter Reformation, moral reform and an ethic of social utility. Yates asks where else such a combination occurs and answers, with explicit hedging ("fumbling in the dark"), Freemasonry and the Magic Flute.

For WMP this is a case of a [[modernity-machine]] precursor working with inherited legible categories (Sephiroth, hierarchies, hieroglyphic signs) and refusing their abstraction, magic as the will to operate on nature; it rhymes loosely with the [[world-machines]] picture of old infrastructure being reused and reshuffled, though Yates herself offers no machine theory and the fit is only suggestive.

## Notable passages

The opening framing (c1) sets Pico's Magus against Bruno's Egyptianism and states the thesis that Cabala is retained but subordinated. The Ass passage (c2) shows how Bruno replaces metaphysics with natural philosophy through an Apuleian and Agrippan symbol. c3 gives Bruno's self-definition of his cabala and Yates's reading of it as a conscious departure from the Christian Magus. c4 links Egyptian hieroglyphic language to Corpus Hermeticum XVI and to Bruno's anti-pedantry. c5 gives the linking-with-demons method and the imagination as its centre, ending with the contrast to Pico's use of magic to prove Christ's divinity. c6 is the Freemasonry conjecture and Bruno's place in sixteenth-century toleration, which Yates flags as speculation.
