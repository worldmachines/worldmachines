# Built on the old machine's bones

## The idea

There is no pristine, unclaimed territory to build the next machine on; the
Divergence Machine has to run on the Modernity Machine's carcass - its
archive, its identifiers, its institutional standards - and the AI trained
on that inheritance absorbs the older machine's own buried structure and
errors along with its useful content. The practical acceptance is that you
cannot start clean: build the new system on the old one's bones, keep a
good-enough inherited floor rather than demanding a perfect new one, and
treat ongoing human exception-making as a permanent feature rather than a
temporary patch. The failure mode is a specific kind of invisibility:
inherited bias or structure becomes silently load-bearing underneath
something that presents itself as new, producing a Ship-of-Theseus
situation where no one can say anymore what was actually swapped out or
why.

## Where it comes from

One row ("Built on the old machine's bones") of the same bet-table.

## How settled this is

A working hypothesis; the associated bet is that AI bias gets increasingly
traced to corpus provenance and that legacy-system integration becomes AI's
dominant cost center - offered as a testable claim rather than an
established result.

## Open

Whether inherited structural bias is something that can actually be
surfaced and corrected once identified, or whether it is definitionally the
kind of error that stays invisible because the system built on top of it
still functions, is left as the practical question this bet is meant to
track.
