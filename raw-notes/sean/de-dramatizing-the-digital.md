# De-Dramatizing the Digital

## The idea

Star Wars and Star Trek both depict completed technology stacks - nobody in
them finds their computers interesting, because the friction has been
resolved and daily life (love, ambition, boredom) reasserts itself. The
essay's wager is that AI is a completing technology for the present stack,
not an intensifier of it, and that digital life will get less dramatic on
long timelines even though it will look more dramatic first.

The argument runs through two borrowed models. Stewart Brand's pace layers
say civilization runs in layers moving at different speeds - fashion,
commerce, infrastructure, governance, culture, nature - and attention
concentrates in the fast layers when they are not good enough. Christensen's
Conservation of Attractive Profits says that when a layer in a value chain is
inadequate, profit migrates to whichever layer gets integrated to fix it. Put
together: our current digital layer is inadequate (fragmented tools,
incompatible formats, unresolved friction), which is why so much capital and
attention pool there. As AI matures into the infrastructure layer, it
commoditizes what sits above it, and the difficulty - and the profit -
migrates downward.

The precedent is the printing press. Reading did not decrease after print; it
exploded. What print removed was the ordeal of access - copying by hand,
travelling to a manuscript, trusting an uncertain provenance. AI is proposed
as doing the same thing to the "ten thousand small frictions" of digital
life: not less use, but de-dramatized use, the way plumbing became invisible
once it worked.

The sharper claim is about which kind of completion arrives. Spinoza's
natura naturans (nature actively, endlessly self-generating, never settling
into a final form) versus natura naturata (nature as finished, produced
world) supplies the fork. A Leibnizian completion is one platform, one
ecosystem, pre-harmonized - "the Modernity Machine in a hoodie" - a finished
product that forecloses the plurality the Divergence Machine needs. A
Spinozan completion is a pervasive, generative capability with no canonical
interface and no final release. The essay's stated hope is that the most
powerful AI stack will be the most boring one - powerful enough to end the
friction, unassuming enough not to become the new object of obsession - but
it names the real danger plainly: infrastructure monopolies can be more
extractive than attention monopolies, because their extraction is invisible.
If AI arrives as natura naturata owned by three firms, the Gramsci Gap's
monsters don't disappear; they put on boring clothes and move downstairs into
the infrastructure layer. Brian Arthur's observation that technologies never
truly finish, only stratify their complexity into a maintainer layer, is the
essay's own hedge against its optimism: boring for most people, more
intricate than ever for whoever tends the machine.

## Where it comes from

Sean Stevenson, "De-Dramatizing the Digital," *The Schematism* (Substack),
published 2026-04-12: https://seanstevenson.substack.com/p/de-dramatizing-the-digital.
Draws on Stewart Brand's pace layers (*The Clock of the Long Now*), Clayton
Christensen and Michael Raynor's Conservation of Attractive Profits (*The
Innovator's Solution*), Elizabeth Eisenstein's *The Printing Revolution in
Early Modern Europe*, Spinoza's *Ethics* (via Matthew Stewart's *The Courtier
and the Heretic*), and W. Brian Arthur's *The Nature of Technology*.

## How settled this is

The pace-layers/Christensen mechanism is a borrowed, well-established pair of
models applied to a new case - solid as an analogy, not as a prediction. The
printing-press precedent is a real historical case but an analogy, not a
proof that AI will de-dramatize rather than intensify. The Spinozan/Leibnizian
fork is the essay's own framing device, offered as a wager rather than a
forecast - the essay is explicit that it is not "best of all possible worlds"
optimism, only a bet on which kind of completion is more resistant to
capture.

## Open

Sean's own hedge stands: which kind of completion is actually arriving is not
yet decided, and Brian Arthur's stratification point suggests the honest
answer may be "both at once" - boring on the surface, a new priesthood
underneath. Worth tracking which infrastructure layer AI actually
consolidates into, and whether that consolidation stays legible.
