# Pluribus as Divergence Machine art

## The idea

The premise of Pluribus - a hive-mind that joins nearly everyone into a
single collective consciousness and makes them content - plays on screen as
horror rather than as the utopia its premise promises, and that inversion
is the point worth naming directly: uniting everyone and making them happy
turns out to be the nightmare, not the dream. What the joined collective
optimizes for is efficiency, visibly and almost exclusively - the show's
own answer for why the joined people behave the way they do is that all
power routes to a single antenna broadcasting one shared message, an image
of coordination purchased entirely at the price of individual perspective.
Read against the Divergence Machine's own values - proliferation, plurality,
coexistence without convergence - Pluribus depicts the Modernity Machine's
opposite temptation taken to its endpoint: total convergence, total
legibility, total efficiency, and the show insists this is a horror rather
than a resolution. It is also, on this reading, a sharp and specific
critique of any collectivist reading of Buddhist or Hindu non-duality that
mistakes the dissolution of separate selves for the same thing as this kind
of enforced, efficiency-driven merger - they share a vocabulary of joining
and lose everything else.

## Where it comes from

A single observation from Sean's own notes on film and television,
developed from watching Pluribus.

## How settled this is

A critical reading of a single television show, offered as an interpretive

## Open

Whether the show's critique lands specifically on efficiency-as-value, or
more broadly on any total merger of perspective regardless of what it
optimizes for, is worth distinguishing before leaning on this reading
further.
