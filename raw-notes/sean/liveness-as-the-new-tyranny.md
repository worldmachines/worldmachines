# Liveness as the new tyranny

## The idea

Freedom from the Modernity Machine's rigid schedule is real, but it is not
freedom from imperative as such - it is traded for a new one. The
Divergence Machine's version of the demand is to be current, to be present,
to be reachable and responsive at all times, which seeds directly into what
the World Machines project calls the coming Liveness Machine. The
difficulty migrates from keeping time to sustaining presence itself, and
presence turns out to be the one resource in the whole system that
genuinely does not scale, because it draws on a finite human capacity
rather than on infrastructure that can simply be expanded. The practical
acceptance this asks for is straightforward to state and hard to practice:
no one can be fully live all the time, asynchrony has to be actively
protected rather than apologized for, and presence has to be treated as a
finite, budgeted resource rather than a standing moral obligation. The
failure mode is an inability to sustain the demanded presence at all -
burnout, the fear of missing out, an asynchronous self that has learned to
perform synchrony it does not actually have - because the one resource the
system runs on is exactly the part of a person capable of being used up.

## Where it comes from

## How settled this is

A working hypothesis; the associated bet is that presence and attention
become an explicitly priced and managed resource, that right-to-disconnect
norms spread more widely, and that live-presence burnout becomes a
clinically recognized category rather than an informal complaint.

## Open

Whether "presence as a finite, budgeted resource" can actually become a
widely adopted norm, or whether the competitive pressure to be maximally
live simply overrides any individual or institutional attempt to budget
it, is the practical question this bet is tracking.
