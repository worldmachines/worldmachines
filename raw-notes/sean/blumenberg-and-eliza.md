# Blumenberg and ELIZA

## The idea

Long before current large language models, Blumenberg wrote a little-known
text on Joseph Weizenbaum's ELIZA, one of the first AI systems to interact
successfully with humans. Weatherby's reading of that text (in the 2022 New
German Critique special issue on Blumenberg) finds a phenomenological
critique of interactive AI that reads as strikingly current.

The core move is not to ask whether the machine really understands.
Blumenberg's claim is that intelligence of any kind - human included - is
"intermittent": only unreliably and periodically connected to a world
outside itself, constituting and continually re-constituting itself,
patching its own interruptions. That claim does real work because it runs in
both directions at once. It lets Blumenberg compare human consciousness to a
computer without anthropomorphizing the machine, because the comparison
isn't "the machine is like a mind" - it's "the mind was already structurally
like a machine's alternation of data and instructions." The comparison
de-natures the human rather than elevating the machine.

ELIZA, in this reading, is of the "rhetorical" type: it puts a user in a
space of artificial meaning, or better, it shows how artificial meaning was
in the first place. The program doesn't successfully fake human semantics by
accident; its success reveals that human meaning-making was always a
rhetorical production rather than a transparent window onto genuine
understanding. The polemical payoff is a way to sidestep the usual argument
entirely - instead of litigating whether AI has "real" semantics (a
functional or anthropological question that never gets settled), critique
the artifact on the plane of rhetoric and semiotics, where its success or
failure can actually be evaluated.

## Where it comes from

Nicholas Weatherby, "Intermittent Legitimacy: Hans Blumenberg and Artificial
Intelligence," in *Hans Blumenberg at 101*, special issue of New German
Critique 145, Vol. 49, No. 1 (February 2022), ed. Hannes Bajohr (Duke
University Press). Reading notes captured 2026-07-17 in the vault; primary
paper text is in-copyright (New German Critique / Duke UP) and kept for
personal research, not redistribution - this note paraphrases the secondary
reading-notes summary rather than the paper itself.

## How settled this is

This is a fair, well-sourced summary of a specific scholarly argument
(Weatherby's reading of a real Blumenberg text), not an independent claim.
Its extension to current LLMs is an implication the reading notes draw but
that Weatherby's paper (published 2022, about ELIZA specifically) does not
itself make about present-day systems - worth flagging that the jump from
ELIZA to now is an inference, however natural.

## Open

Weatherby's paper reads Blumenberg's ELIZA text as "surprisingly relevant" to
current, much more powerful systems, but the paper itself predates the
current generation of LLMs by several years - whether "intermittent" and
"rhetorical" hold up as descriptions of a model that maintains coherent
long-context state is worth testing directly rather than assuming.
