# Eras are measurements

## The idea

People periodically try to work out whether the world has moved into a new era —
whether the post-1989 period deserves a name, whether the Anthropocene supersedes
or merely continues the postmodern, whether AI marks the kind of break that the
atomic bomb or the fall of the Berlin Wall once did. The question is usually
asked as though it had a fact of the matter waiting to be discovered: either an
era has begun or it hasn't, and careful historical judgment should be able to
tell which.

There's a simpler diagnosis available. An era is not a natural kind that
periodically gets born; it's a measurement — a unit laid over a continuous,
undifferentiated flow of events so that change can be talked about, taught, and
periodized at all. Asking whether "the Divergence Machine era" has really
started is like asking whether an inch has really started: the underlying
continuity is real, and the measurement describes something real about it, but
the measurement itself is an artifact of the describing, not a feature
discovered in the world.

This reframes the anxious question. Instead of "has a new era begun, and if so
when," the productive question becomes "what unit would make the current
pattern legible, and who gets to set it." That moves the problem out of
metaphysics and into the more tractable territory of naming and periodization.
It's exactly the kind of problem reoccupation is built to describe: a new unit
for measuring time is just another slot, waiting to be filled with a new
answer once the old measuring-frame stops fitting.

## How settled this is

This is a diagnostic move rather than a worked-out theory — useful for
dissolving a particular kind of stuck question, not a claim that has been
tested against how historians actually periodize the past. It would weaken if
it turned out that some era-boundaries really do mark discontinuities sharp
enough to be more like discoveries than measurements (a genuine phase
transition rather than a gradual drift that gets an arbitrary cut point).

## Where it comes from

Sean's own move, developed while working out why declaring the Divergence
Machine a successor era to postmodernism kept feeling presumptuous no matter
how the argument was phrased — the discomfort turned out to be a category
mistake rather than a lack of evidence.
