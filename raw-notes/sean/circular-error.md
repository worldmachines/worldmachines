# Circular Error

## The idea

Galileo assumed a pendulum's swing was isochronous regardless of amplitude;
it isn't quite - a wide arc takes fractionally longer than a narrow one, a
discrepancy horologists named circular error. The circle was the natural,
nearly-right assumption: good enough to build a clock, not good enough for
precision. History then offered two ways to handle an inherited near-miss.
Substitution replaces the flawed curve with the true one (Huygens's
cycloidal cheeks, mathematically exact). Domain restriction instead narrows
the conditions under which the flaw operates until it stops mattering (the
anchor escapement, which restricts the pendulum's swing to a few degrees
where circle and cycloid coincide). Clockmakers chose restriction: the
elegant exact solution was abandoned because it introduced problems of its
own, while the crude restricted one just worked and could be reproduced.

The generalizable claim is the Conservation of Difficulty: bounding an error
relocates its difficulty rather than eliminating it. Once the pendulum's
geometry was tamed by restriction, the escapement itself became the new
load-bearing part and the new site of difficulty - and when a stage in a
chain gets solved or standardized, difficulty and value both migrate to
whichever adjacent stage becomes newly decisive. This repeats across the
whole history the essay traces: the marine chronometer abandoned the
pendulum (which cannot survive a ship's roll) for a balance wheel that
carries its own new circular errors (temperature, friction, uneven mainspring
force) which then get bounded and compensated in turn; and privatized,
personal time then hands the difficulty of self-discipline to the
individual, where it shows up as the low hum of guilt about how one's hours
are spent.

Applied to the present: AI's harnesses - retrieval scaffolds, guardrails,
agent loops - are anchor escapements, not repairs to the underlying model.
They don't fix a model's confident wrongness; they narrow the conditions
under which that wrongness does damage, converting a headlong torrent into
something countable and inspectable. The design rule the clock history
offers is to keep the correction legible and beside the core rather than
inside it - Harrison and Le Roy got accuracy by leaving the balance nearly
free and adding control alongside it. Lose that discipline and the
compensations tangle into an opaque, unremovable patchwork whose difficulty
has migrated not to a useful adjacent layer but into permanent opacity.

The essay reads this as a general theory of how a World Machine ages: in
Dawn, almost-right inventions are optional; in Day, the winners harden into
standards and institutions and the difficulty migrates into maintaining
them; in Dusk, the compensations can no longer be sustained and collapse
into debt service. A machine survives by borrowing against the future -
keeping the almost-right core because it still works, and paying interest
through the compensations required to keep it working.

## Where it comes from

Sean Stevenson, "Circular Error," *The Schematism* (Substack), published
2026-06-25: https://seanstevenson.substack.com/p/circular-error. Draws
principally on David Landes, *Revolution in Time* (the Contraptions Book
Club's May 2026 pick), plus Lewis Mumford (*Technics and Civilization*) and
Sachin's "archival time" framing (*Summer Lightning*).

## How settled this is

The horological history (Galileo, Huygens, the anchor escapement, Harrison,
Le Roy) is established fact, sourced to Landes. The generalization - that
every load-bearing near-solution behaves this way, and that AI harnesses are
the present instance - is the essay's own extended claim, offered as a
working theory ("I'm putting forward a theory") rather than a settled
result. The Dawn/Day/Dusk debt-accumulation reading is explicitly speculative
scaffolding for the World Machines project.

## Open

The essay names its own fork explicitly: whether the compensations built
around a flawed core stay legible (retrieval that points to its source,
guardrails that mark their own boundary) or tangle into an opaque patchwork
no one can remove. Which way that goes for AI specifically is unresolved and
worth tracking directly.
