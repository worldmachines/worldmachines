# The vanishing now

## The idea

Everyone now owns their own access to the clock and syncs on demand, which
sounds like freedom and functions as an erosion: a shared, synchronous now
is the resource that quietly disappears. The difficulty does not vanish, it
moves - from the old problem of keeping accurate time to the new problem of
convening people at the same time at all. A workable response accepts a
thinner shared now rather than trying to re-impose universal synchrony
everywhere; the practical move is to protect a small number of high-value
synchronies (civic moments, genuine crises) instead of insisting on
constant collective presence. The failure mode is a democracy or a
coordination system that has lost the capacity to act together because it
no longer has a common present to deliberate inside - publics splinter into
timelines that never overlap, and collective response stalls exactly when
it is needed.

## Where it comes from

One row ("The vanishing now") of a working bet-table for the psychohistory
/ World Machines project, tracking structural errors of the Divergence
Machine and where their difficulty migrates.

## How settled this is

A working hypothesis in a bet-table explicitly designed to be falsifiable -
the associated bet (that live, synchronous attention keeps rising in price
while recorded content trends toward free) is offered as a testable
prediction, not an established finding.

## Open

Whether "a few high-value synchronies" is actually a stable equilibrium, or
whether the erosion continues until no shared synchrony survives at all, is
the bet this note is built to test against events rather than argue from
first principles.
