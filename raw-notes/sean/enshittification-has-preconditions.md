# Enshittification has preconditions, and AI may not meet them

## The idea

A common reflex holds that AI products will inevitably degrade the way
platforms have: good experience while building a user base, value
increasingly siphoned to business customers, then to shareholders, at
users' expense, protected by lock-in once leaving is costly enough. Cory
Doctorow's account of enshittification names this pattern precisely and
gives it specific preconditions rather than treating it as an automatic
consequence of corporate size or ambition. Applying that account
critically, rather than assuming the pattern repeats, means actually
asking whether those preconditions hold for AI specifically: whether
delivering the experience currently costs more than what's charged for it,
whether that gap is sustainable or has to close through some other means,
and whether advertising or a comparable value-extraction layer is creeping
into the experience the way it did for search and social platforms.

The claim here isn't that AI companies are somehow immune to this pattern —
some plainly will enshittify by exactly this mechanism. It's that the
reflexive version of the worry treats "large company, powerful product" as
sufficient on its own, when Doctorow's own account says the mechanism
requires more specific conditions than that. If AI really is a completing
technology in the sense argued elsewhere in this corpus, the more
consequential shift may be that the highest-value work — judgment, the
reoccupied practices a completing technology can't easily absorb — moves to
places lock-in doesn't reach as easily as it reaches a social feed or a
search results page.

## Grounding

Cory Doctorow's enshittification concept, describing the mechanics of
platform decay. The application and pushback specifically to AI, checking
the concept's stated preconditions against this case rather than assuming
the pattern transfers, is original to this note.

## How settled this is

Doctorow's account of enshittification's mechanics is a real, specific,
checkable argument about a documented pattern in platform behavior.
Whether AI specifically lacks the preconditions for the same pattern is
this note's own claim, offered as a plausible pushback against a reflex
rather than a demonstrated conclusion — and it may simply be too early in
AI's commercial history to tell either way.
