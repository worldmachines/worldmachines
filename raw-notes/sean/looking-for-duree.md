# Looking for Duree

## The idea

The essay sets out to locate duree - Bergson's continuous, lived duration,
the flowing time supposedly underneath the counted, public clock - in the
one place it seemed guaranteed to be found: the body. What turns up instead
is another clock. Split-second timing runs through the cerebellum; the felt
present is assembled in the insula from three converging streams - attention,
safety, and bodily state (heartbeat, breath, gut) - and the heartbeat itself
behaves like an escapement, gating and counting duration rather than simply
flowing. A 2025 study found the reverse of the expected pattern: clips that
slowed the heart most were judged shortest, meaning the mind reads duration
off the organ's count rather than off a felt stream. There is no river under
the counting; there is another counter.

A month of unlabeled meditation looking for the same current came up equally
empty: things arose and passed, and that was the whole of it. Any sense of
duration that appeared during practice wasn't the deep truth surfacing - it
was practice going badly, a thought about sequence, and thoughts have no
special ontological status. The parallel to the Buddhist search for a self
is explicit and intentional: looking for a self finds no one home; looking
for duration finds no flow. Dogen's uji supplies the position this leaves
you in - spring does not turn into summer; each is its own complete
being-time, not a station on a hidden river. What links one moment to the
next is causal order, not a current - the same structure relativity uses
(light cones constraining later events without anything flowing between
them), applied to a life instead of to physics, which is what karma names.

The essay's verdict is that Bergson's duree is a phenomenology of a
particular kind of subject - memory-saturated, forward-leaning, affectively
continuous - mistaken for an ontology of time itself. Real, but a
description of experience, not evidence of a hidden current. The
consequence: there is no common now to sync up with, only common counted
time and the deliberate, effortful work of making two trajectories actually
intersect - a shared moment is not given by a shared river, it is
manufactured by two people arriving at the same coordinate.

## Where it comes from

Sean Stevenson, "Looking for Duree," *The Schematism* (Substack), published
2026-07-03: https://seanstevenson.substack.com/p/looking-for-duree. Draws on
David Landes's *Revolution in Time*, Jimena Canales's *The Physicist and the
Philosopher* (the Einstein-Bergson debate), Dogen (via *Zen Time*), the 1922
Einstein-Bergson debate at the Societe francaise de philosophie, and
2025-2026 interoception research on heart-rate and duration perception.

## How settled this is

The neuroscience of interoception and duration (insula involvement, the
heart-rate/duration reversal finding) is cited primary research, though the
essay is careful to flag that the correlation runs more through
interoceptive awareness than raw accuracy, and that a single study is thin
evidence. The meditation report is a genuine first-person account over one
month, explicitly offered as field notes rather than a settled finding - a
month is short against a claim (Bergson's) that duration is supposed to be
immediately given. The Einstein-over-Bergson verdict follows the historical
debate's actual outcome (nothing in relativity requires a flow) but the
essay's extension of that verdict to karma-as-causal-structure is its own
construction, not physics.
