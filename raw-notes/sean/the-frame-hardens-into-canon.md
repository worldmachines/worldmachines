# The frame hardens into canon

## The idea

Every navigational frame - a social media feed, a map, an AI-generated
summary - starts life as a known, useful approximation: a compression of
something more complicated, adopted because it works well enough. The
specific failure this row names is that such frames tend to harden,
gradually and without anyone deciding it should happen, into the only
reality anyone actually perceives - the map is mistaken for the territory,
and people, in the model's own phrase, "drive into the lake." The practical
acceptance is that navigating by frames is unavoidable; what can be built
in is friction and reminders that keep the frame visible as a frame, and a
deliberate cultivation of frame-literacy - the discipline of remembering
that the map is not the territory, practiced rather than assumed. This is
named as the deepest circular error of the whole set because it recurses: a
frame mistaken for the world is unfalsifiable from the inside, since the
approximation's own errors become, for whoever is inside the frame, simply
how reality is.

## Where it comes from

One row ("The frame hardens into canon") of a working table of bets for the World Machines project: a running list of structural errors in how today's fixes for the Divergence Machine's limitations themselves fail, each paired with a falsifiable, near-term bet about how the failure will surface.


## How settled this is

A working hypothesis; the associated bet is that over-reliance harms in
navigation, medicine, and hiring trigger explainability and contestability
mandates, and that "automation complacency" becomes a recognized, named
incident category.

## Open

Whether frame-literacy can actually be taught and sustained as a discipline
at scale, or whether the hardening this row describes is close to
inevitable once a frame becomes convenient enough, is left unresolved.
