Annotated bibliography

Where the claims come from

Start with reviews; use primary papers for detail. DOI links are persistent identifiers, though some publisher pages may be paywalled. Sources were independently researched and cross-checked against DOI metadata.

Accessible foundations

  • National Academies (2008), Science, Evolution, and CreationismConsensus-level, plain-language foundation.
  • UC Museum of Paleontology, Understanding Evolution 101Well-scaffolded reference for selection, drift, and phylogeny.
  • OpenStax, Biology 2e, evolution unitsFree textbook refresher at the intended prerequisite level.

Population genetics and molecular evolution

  • Kimura (1968), neutral molecular evolutionFoundational theory.
  • Ohta (1973), slightly deleterious substitutionsNearly-neutral extension.
  • Charlesworth (2009), effective population sizeExpert review.
  • Eyre-Walker & Keightley (2007), fitness effects of new mutationsExpert review.
  • Kern & Hahn (2018), neutrality in light of selectionModern interpretation and critique.
  • Sella & Barton (2019), “Thinking About the Evolution of Complex Traits in the Era of Genome-Wide Association Studies”Why genomic selection inference requires caution.

Genomics, ancestry, and networks

  • Green et al. (2010), Neanderthal genomePrimary ancient-DNA landmark.
  • Reich et al. (2010), Denisova genomePrimary discovery and ancestry analysis.
  • Huerta-Sánchez et al. (2014), adaptive introgression at EPAS1Primary study.
  • Human Pangenome Reference Consortium (2023)Diverse draft pangenome.
  • Soucy, Huang & Gogarten (2015), HGTReview of the “web of life,” with microbial scope.
  • Degnan & Rosenberg (2009), gene-tree discordanceMultispecies coalescent review.
  • Good et al. (2017), 60,000 generations of E. coliPrimary long-term evolution experiment.

Regulation, development, and inheritance

  • Wray (2007), cis-regulatory mutationsReview of regulatory evolution.
  • ENCODE Project (2012)Primary biochemical activity map; read with the definitional critiques.
  • Graur et al. (2013), function and ENCODEEvolutionary-function critique.
  • Müller (2007), evo-devo and synthesisExpert review.
  • Uller et al. (2018), developmental biasRegulatory-network review.
  • Levis & Pfennig (2016), plasticity-first criteriaCritical empirical framework.
  • Heard & Martienssen (2014), epigenetic inheritanceAuthoritative myths-and-mechanisms review.
  • Laland, Odling-Smee & Feldman (1999), niche constructionFoundational evolutionary model.

Levels, symbiosis, and culture

  • Price (1970), selection and covarianceFormal basis for multilevel partitioning.
  • Queller (2000), relatedness and major transitionsExpert theory/review.
  • Sagan [later Margulis] (1967), “On the origin of mitosing cells”Historically foundational endosymbiotic hypothesis; later genomic evidence established organelle bacterial ancestry.
  • Bordenstein & Theis (2015), holobiont caseStrong position.
  • Moran & Sloan (2015), hologenome critiqueCritical companion.
  • Laland, Odling-Smee & Myles (2010), culture and the human genomeGene–culture synthesis.
  • Tishkoff et al. (2006), “Convergent adaptation of human lactase persistence in Africa and Europe”Primary case of convergent gene–culture coevolution.

The synthesis debate

  • Laland et al. (2015), EES structure and predictionsProgrammatic affirmative case.
  • Futuyma (2017), evolutionary biology and the EES callCritical/bridging assessment.
  • Müller (2017), why an EES is necessaryDirect affirmative position.

Editorial rule: a citation supports only the claim it tested. One taxon or mechanism does not establish universality. When a lesson says “debated,” the paired sources are an invitation to inspect assumptions rather than vote by authority.
