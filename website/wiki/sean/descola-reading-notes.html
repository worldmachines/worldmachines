Descola reading notes: beyond the grid

Sean · updated 5 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Each of Descola's four ontologies generates its own internal two-by-two — naturalism crossing sincerity with literalness, animism crossing masking with approach-or-flight, analogism crossing vertical-or-horizontal with binding-or-dissolving, totemism crossing singular-or-choral identity with stillness-or-motion — and the Modernity-Divergence-Liveness sequence traces an L-shaped path across the grid, naturalism to analogism to animism, with a further, more speculative reading treating analogism itself as the alchemical operator fusing the other two rather than a waypoint between them.

The idea

Beyond the grid itself, each of Descola's four ontologies turns out to generate its own internal two-by-two, with its own existential question and its own four named positions. Naturalism's internal axis is sincerity crossed with literalness - Scientists, Goths, Gamers, and Poets - and the finding worth keeping is that sincere-and-metaphorical ("Goths") is the dominant attractor across cultures and generations, with ironic-and-literal ("Gamers") the rarest; irony reads as a late, culturally expensive achievement rather than a default stance. Animism's internal axis crosses masking with approach-or-flight - Shapeshifters, Sorcerers, Ecstatics, and a ground-state position ("Deer," naked and fleeing) that the other three read as escape strategies from, since animism's default condition is exposure rather than power. Analogism's internal axis crosses vertical-or-horizontal with binding-or-dissolving - Architects, Fountains, Weavers, and Alchemists

  • with the Weaver quadrant specifically identified as where sympathetic magic's similarity principle lives (correspondences binding like to like) and the Alchemist quadrant as where solve et coagula operates (correspondences used to dissolve categories back into raw material). Totemism's internal axis crosses singular-or-choral identity with stillness-or-motion - Monoliths, Songlines, Corroborees, and Murmurations - and totemism is flagged as the hardest ontology for a naturalist observer to think from, because its questions read less like strategies than like moods: a Monolith does not act, a Songline does not choose its route.

The Modernity-Divergence-Liveness sequence maps onto three of the four ontologies as an L-shaped trajectory across Descola's own grid, not a diagonal and not a closed loop: first across the physicality axis (Modernity's naturalism to Divergence's analogism - pluralizing a single shared world into many singular, correspondence-linked essences), then up the interiority axis (Divergence's analogism to Liveness's animism - distributing agency outward into nonhuman entities). The framing note attached to this mapping is worth repeating exactly because it is easy to lose: this is not a progress narrative, and no ontology in the sequence is better or worse than another - the claim is only that each machine's character drives movement between ontologies, not that later stops supersede earlier ones. A further, more speculative proposal treats analogism (Divergence) not as a waystation on that line but as the operator that fuses the other two - structurally equivalent to Mercury in the alchemical schema, the mediating spirit binding body (Modernity) and soul (Liveness) into a coniunctio, which would make the trajectory closer to a triangle with Divergence at the binding vertex than to a line passing through it.

The Spinoza-Leibniz pairing recurs here as the naturalism/analogism split in miniature: Spinoza as the deepest possible Scientist (one substance, pure geometric sincerity, no correspondences, no hierarchy), Leibniz as analogism wearing a naturalist costume (monads in pre-established harmony are a correspondence-web dressed as a single substance). Whether modernity ultimately reads as Naturalist, with Spinoza's century won, or as Analogist, with Leibniz getting the last laugh once correspondence returns at infrastructural scale in the Divergence Machine, is posed as the open question this framing exists to sharpen.

The research file's institutional-applications section reads each Descola ontology as operating inside modern institutions that officially profess naturalism while actually running on a different ontology entirely - the professed ontology carries legitimacy, the operative one carries efficacy. Analogist enclaves include legal reasoning by precedent (a correspondence operation performed to distinguish or bind a case), technical analysis in trading (divination by resemblance of chart shape), and embeddings in AI systems (the doctrine of signatures made computable - vector similarity offers resemblance-at-a-distance with no causal mechanism, but the output arrives wearing a number, which reads as naturalist legitimacy over an analogist operation). Animist enclaves include the routine personification of trading floors ("the market wants to go higher") and of software (daemons, zombies, processes that "don't like" being called a certain way), and the persistence of tacit, unwritten "good hands" knowledge in bench science despite its official naturalist self-description. Totemic enclaves include federal socioeconomic set-asides and NAICS classification codes, where a firm's assigned essence permanently gates which parts of a market it may enter - a genuinely well-formalized moiety system, more so than most ethnographically documented ones - alongside the more familiar cases of sports fandom and military unit insignia.

The religious material extends the grid past its own edges rather than filling in a fifth quadrant. The Tibetan Buddhist doctrine of the three kayas sits inside an analogist apparatus (mandalas, deity hierarchies, subtle-body mappings) but does something the grid cannot easily absorb: Dharmakaya recognizes that the analogist multiplicity has no ontological foundation, which renders Descola's own interiority/physicality distinction conventional rather than final - a meta-move that collapses the grid rather than occupying a cell in it. The Dzogchen radicalization goes further, treating essence, nature, and energy as three simultaneous aspects of every moment rather than sequential levels, which reads less like moving through Descola's grid and more like a moire pattern laid across it. Read this way, the three kayas function as a technique for relating to ontological multiplicity, not merely a description of it - closer to Descola's grid being used than being mapped.

Finally, current AI discourse is read as running two of Descola's ontological grammars simultaneously without generally noticing the seam: an animist grammar (alignment, wants, refusal, "Claude's character") that treats the system as a unified, soul-bearing agent, alongside an analogist grammar (attention as cognition, layers, emergence) borrowed from biology and physics that implies a correspondence-web with no single self at all. Whether the culture eventually settles on the animist reading or the analogist one is treated as genuinely open, and - given the four-hundred- year framing this whole grid inherits from the World Machines project - the note's honest position is that this may be the opening moves of that settlement rather than anything close to its result.

Where it comes from

Working research document developed alongside the already-committed descola-four-ontologies note, drawing on Philippe Descola's Beyond Nature and Culture (2005/2013), Eduardo Viveiros de Castro's Cannibal Metaphysics (multinaturalist perspectivism), Venkatesh Rao's World Machines framework, Matthew Stewart's The Courtier and the Heretic (Spinoza-Leibniz), and Tibetan Buddhist sources on the three-kaya doctrine and Dzogchen. This note deliberately leaves out this same research file's discussion of specific day-job institutional examples, which are held back from this batch for separate review.

How settled this is

Descola's four-ontology grid and its paradigm cases are established anthropological theory, already covered by the committed note this one extends. The internal two-by-twos for each ontology, the L-shaped trajectory, the Divergence-Machine-as-alchemical-operator proposal, and the institutional-enclave readings are this research file's own synthesis and speculation, held at varying degrees of confidence - the alchemical-operator idea is explicitly the most speculative of the set, offered as a proposal rather than a finding. The Trikaya/Dzogchen material draws on real Tibetan Buddhist doctrine but its use here, as a lens for stress-testing Descola's grid rather than as Buddhist scholarship in its own right, is this synthesis's own move.
