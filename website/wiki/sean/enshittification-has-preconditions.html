Enshittification has preconditions, and AI may not meet them

Sean · updated 13 September 2026 · source on GitHub
The reflex that AI will inevitably 'enshittify' the way platforms do skips a step: Cory Doctorow's account of enshittification names specific preconditions for the pattern, and it's worth actually checking whether AI meets them rather than assuming the pattern repeats because the companies involved are large.

The idea

A common reflex holds that AI products will inevitably degrade the way platforms have: good experience while building a user base, value increasingly siphoned to business customers, then to shareholders, at users' expense, protected by lock-in once leaving is costly enough. Cory Doctorow's account of enshittification names this pattern precisely and gives it specific preconditions rather than treating it as an automatic consequence of corporate size or ambition. Applying that account critically, rather than assuming the pattern repeats, means actually asking whether those preconditions hold for AI specifically: whether delivering the experience currently costs more than what's charged for it, whether that gap is sustainable or has to close through some other means, and whether advertising or a comparable value-extraction layer is creeping into the experience the way it did for search and social platforms.

The claim here isn't that AI companies are somehow immune to this pattern — some plainly will enshittify by exactly this mechanism. It's that the reflexive version of the worry treats "large company, powerful product" as sufficient on its own, when Doctorow's own account says the mechanism requires more specific conditions than that. If AI really is a completing technology in the sense argued elsewhere in this corpus, the more consequential shift may be that the highest-value work — judgment, the reoccupied practices a completing technology can't easily absorb — moves to places lock-in doesn't reach as easily as it reaches a social feed or a search results page.

Grounding

Cory Doctorow's enshittification concept, describing the mechanics of platform decay. The application and pushback specifically to AI, checking the concept's stated preconditions against this case rather than assuming the pattern transfers, is original to this note.

How settled this is

Doctorow's account of enshittification's mechanics is a real, specific, checkable argument about a documented pattern in platform behavior. Whether AI specifically lacks the preconditions for the same pattern is this note's own claim, offered as a plausible pushback against a reflex rather than a demonstrated conclusion — and it may simply be too early in AI's commercial history to tell either way.

Connections

Open

Are the "boring infrastructure" bet in de-dramatizing-the-digital and this note's enshittification-preconditions pushback actually consistent with each other, or does that essay's own hedge — that invisible infrastructure extraction is a distinct, harder-to-see risk — already answer this note's optimism before it gets going?
