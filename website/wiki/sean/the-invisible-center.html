The invisible center

Sean · updated 5 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Coordination designed to avoid a center tends to rebuild one anyway, at a layer no one is watching - the shared platform, protocol, or infrastructure everything else silently depends on - and profit and power migrate down the pace layers to that invisible layer, which is hardest to govern precisely because it cannot be pointed at.

The idea

A system built to avoid centralization often ends up rebuilding a center anyway, just at a layer no one is looking at - the shared platform, protocol, or piece of infrastructure that every supposedly independent instrument silently depends on. This is the Modernity Machine reappearing "in a hoodie": Leibniz's pre-established harmony relocated to the infrastructure layer, coordinating everyone's private tools in advance without anyone noticing the coordination happening. The practical acceptance this asks for is favoring process over product - no final, completed version, resisting the temptation to let any one platform become the completion everyone converges on. The failure mode is an invisible infrastructure monopoly: extraction people do not resent, because they cannot see it, unlike the platforms and feeds people already recognize and resent as extractive.

Where it comes from

One row ("The invisible center") of a working table of bets for the World Machines project: a running list of structural errors in how today's fixes for the Divergence Machine's limitations themselves fail, each paired with a falsifiable, near-term bet about how the failure will surface.

How settled this is

A working hypothesis; the associated bet is that infrastructure and protocol concentration increases, that AI's margin migrates toward the compute and cloud layer, and that antitrust attention shifts from applications to infrastructure - offered as testable rather than settled.

Connections

  • de-dramatizing-the-digital - "monsters in boring clothes, downstairs" and this row's invisible infrastructure monopoly are the same image, arrived at independently from two different essays
  • substrate-and-criterion-split - a Leibnizian, pre-harmonized substrate is exactly what an invisible, uncontested infrastructure center would feel like from the inside
  • the-patchwork-becomes-the-machine - a related error: the scaffolding around a model becoming an unremovable layer is one specific way an invisible center gets built without anyone deciding to build it

Open

Whether an infrastructure center that stays genuinely invisible is more or less dangerous than one that becomes visible and contested, and what would actually make such a center visible before it consolidates, are both left open.
