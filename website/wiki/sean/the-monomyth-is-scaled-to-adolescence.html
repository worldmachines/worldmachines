The monomyth is scaled to adolescence, not a whole life

Sean · updated 13 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Erikson's stages of psychosocial development suggest the monomyth maps well onto one specific passage, adolescence into young adulthood — and that later stages (midlife, old age) have no equivalent culturally-sanctioned adventure template, so a culture keeps applying the adolescent template past the age it fits and mistakes the mismatch for personal failure.

The idea

Erik Erikson's stages of psychosocial development treat the passage from adolescence into young adulthood — identity versus role confusion, then intimacy versus isolation — as a specific, bounded developmental task. Joseph Campbell's monomyth maps onto that particular passage well: a call to leave home, trials that test and form an identity, a return that confers new standing. It's a good fit for one stage of life.

What has no equivalent is any culturally-sanctioned adventure template for the stages that come after. Erikson's own later stages — generativity versus stagnation in midlife, integrity versus despair in old age — don't get a comparable mythic structure the way youth does. The consequence, on this reading, is that a culture without midlife or dying myths keeps reapplying the adolescent template well past the age it was built for: measuring a forty- or sixty-year-old's life against "do I have a hero's journey to point to" produces a felt sense of stalled failure-to-launch that isn't actually about failing to launch. It's a category error — using a template built for one developmental stage to judge every later one — when what's missing is a different, not-yet-written kind of mythic transformation appropriate to the stage actually being lived.

Grounding

Erik Erikson's eight stages of psychosocial development; Joseph Campbell's monomyth structure. The initial observation that the monomyth becomes an obstacle once applied past adolescence is drawn from cultural criticism circulating on Substack (attributed there to a writer using the handle "Riverkenna"); the specific mapping onto Erikson's named later stages, and the failure-to-launch diagnosis, are this note's own extension, not part of that original piece.

How settled this is

Erikson's stages and Campbell's monomyth are both standard, independently checkable reference points. The claim that their mismatch specifically explains a contemporary feeling of stalled adulthood is interpretive and unverified — a plausible diagnosis offered here, not a tested one.
