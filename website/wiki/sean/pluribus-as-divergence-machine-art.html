Pluribus as Divergence Machine art

Sean · updated 5 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Pluribus reads as the first Divergence Machine artwork - its hive-mind premise, joining everyone into one content collective, plays as horror rather than utopia because the joined collective optimizes for pure efficiency at the cost of individual perspective, and the show frames that total convergence as the actual nightmare.

The idea

The premise of Pluribus - a hive-mind that joins nearly everyone into a single collective consciousness and makes them content - plays on screen as horror rather than as the utopia its premise promises, and that inversion is the point worth naming directly: uniting everyone and making them happy turns out to be the nightmare, not the dream. What the joined collective optimizes for is efficiency, visibly and almost exclusively - the show's own answer for why the joined people behave the way they do is that all power routes to a single antenna broadcasting one shared message, an image of coordination purchased entirely at the price of individual perspective. Read against the Divergence Machine's own values - proliferation, plurality, coexistence without convergence - Pluribus depicts the Modernity Machine's opposite temptation taken to its endpoint: total convergence, total legibility, total efficiency, and the show insists this is a horror rather than a resolution. It is also, on this reading, a sharp and specific critique of any collectivist reading of Buddhist or Hindu non-duality that mistakes the dissolution of separate selves for the same thing as this kind of enforced, efficiency-driven merger - they share a vocabulary of joining and lose everything else.

Where it comes from

A single observation from Sean's own notes on film and television, developed from watching Pluribus.

How settled this is

A critical reading of a single television show, offered as an interpretive claim rather than as a settled analysis - genuinely a one-paragraph observation rather than a worked-out argument, and treated here at that scale.

Connections

  • chimera-of-mismatched-parts - a maintainer priesthood reading signs for everyone else is one way a joined or highly coordinated collective avoids exactly the total-efficiency horror Pluribus depicts, by keeping some interpretive plurality alive at the margins
  • substrate-and-criterion-split - Pluribus's antenna-and-single-message image is a vivid, negative illustration of what a Leibnizian, pre-harmonized "New Nature" would actually feel like lived from inside: coordinated, efficient, and horrifying

Open

Whether the show's critique lands specifically on efficiency-as-value, or more broadly on any total merger of perspective regardless of what it optimizes for, is worth distinguishing before leaning on this reading further.
