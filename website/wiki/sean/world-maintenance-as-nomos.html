World-maintenance as nomos

Sean · updated 7 September 2026 · source on GitHub
A world machine should be understood on the model of Peter Berger's nomos — a symbolic order held against entropy by continuous legitimation-work — rather than on the model of infrastructure that gets repaired; the difference matters because it puts world-maintenance on the same shelf as the literature on how worlds stay believable once their founding answers erode, not on the shelf of repair history.

The idea

It's an easy mistake to reach for the history of repair and maintenance — how bridges, machines, and infrastructure get kept running — as the model for how a world machine stays alive. That literature is about physical upkeep: a thing built once and then periodically fixed. A world machine isn't that kind of object. It's closer to what Peter Berger calls a nomos: a symbolic, plausibility-granting order that exists only so long as it's continuously re-legitimated, and that decays not through wear but through anomie, the loss of the shared sense that the order still makes sense.

Putting world-maintenance on this shelf rather than the infrastructure shelf changes what counts as upkeep. A bridge is maintained by inspection and repair regardless of what anyone believes about it; a nomos is maintained only by the ongoing, often invisible work of legitimation — the ordinary reproduction of belief and behavior that keeps a plausibility structure plausible. On this reading, Blumenberg's reoccupation isn't just a historical account of how eras succeed one another; it's already a maintenance theory, describing one of the specific techniques (re-staffing an inherited frame) that keeps a nomos from collapsing into anomie when its original occupants stop being believable.

Grounding

Drawn from Peter Berger's The Sacred Canopy — the concepts of nomos, plausibility structures, and anomie are his. Reading a world machine specifically as a nomos rather than as infrastructure, and rereading Blumenberg's reoccupation as one of the techniques that keeps a nomos from collapsing, are both original applications of Berger's vocabulary.

How settled this is

Berger's account of nomos and plausibility structures is established sociology of knowledge, checkable directly against The Sacred Canopy. That a world machine specifically fits this model better than a physical-infrastructure model is this note's own framing choice — a corrective offered against one specific mistake, not a claim Berger makes or one that's been tested against the infrastructure analogy it argues against.

Open
