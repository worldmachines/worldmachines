Who keeps the time: a four-cell coordination grid

Sean · updated 5 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Crossing plurality (one shared clock versus many) with sovereignty (the clock holds you versus you hold it) yields four coordination positions - Iron Cage, Modern Competency, Fragmentation, and Liveness - with the central open question being whether groups can actually coordinate by translating between untethered tempos rather than synchronizing to one shared tick.

The idea

David Landes's real thesis in Revolution in Time is not that the clock measured time; it is that mechanical timekeeping manufactured shared time, and shared time was the precondition for industrial modernity - work- discipline, the schedule, the railway, a synchronized world. Read this way, convergent canonicity in its most literal register is not an imposed idea but an imposed rhythm, and the master clock is the master escapement of the whole social order.

A two-by-two follows from crossing plurality (one clock, or many) with sovereignty (does the clock hold you, or do you hold it). Four positions result. Iron Cage: one clock, no sovereignty - the schedule holds you and there is only one of it. Modern Competency: one clock, sovereign - you have mastered the single shared tempo and it serves you. Fragmentation: many clocks, no sovereignty - plurality without the capacity to hold any of it, scattered rather than free. Liveness: many clocks, sovereign - plurality genuinely held, which is why Liveness cannot be reached by returning to one clock; it requires the plurality Modern Competency lacks. Involution is not a fifth cell but a trajectory out of Fragmentation in the wrong direction: rather than gaining sovereignty over many clocks, you amputate the plurality and wall yourself into one private clock you built yourself - a degenerate, self-made return to the Iron Cage.

The personal watch is the seed of this whole dynamic, planted inside the synchronization regime it eventually loosens: putting an escapement in every pocket makes the master clock negotiable, because once everyone can privately verify the time, the center need not hold it alone. This yields a specific, three-stage taxonomy of how a culture relates to its master clock: modernity aligns everyone to one canonical clock; postmodernity contests the clock (whose time - colonial, standard?) while remaining oriented toward it; divergence renders the master clock irrelevant, not smashed, simply no longer the thing anyone reconciles to. Many local tempos run coherently with no canonical tick, which is one more way of naming "understanding each other less but getting along better," applied specifically to time.

The neuroscience makes this literal rather than metaphorical: the body's own timekeeping (an insula-based synthesis of attention, safety, and interoceptive signal) is recruited most heavily exactly when external cues disappear, so the body's clock does not need to be found - it takes over automatically the moment the master escapement is removed.

A distinct and load-bearing addition names the mechanism that turns de-synchronization into Fragmentation rather than Liveness: the severing of time from place. Time and place used to be fused - "be at the mill at six" is a single spacetime coordinate, not two separate facts - and place did half the coordination work for free, bounding who you owed time to, giving the obligation edges, and giving you the release of leaving. COVID cut that weld precisely: the clock survived, place dropped out. Three consequences follow together and constitute Fragmentation as a lived condition rather than an abstraction: the boundary dissolves (an online claim on you has no edge, no commute, no threshold you visibly cross to leave); the release disappears (you can only "log off," a state always suspected of being faked, not a door you walk through); and plurality goes spatially flat (every time now piles onto the same placeless screen, demanding to be held simultaneously, without the bodily crossing of thresholds that used to do the holding). The claim this supports is specific: place was the technology of non-attachment, and losing it means losing the bodily means of holding a tempo lightly.

The largest structural claim is a fork between two models of coordination, not just of time-experience. Synchronization means one shared tick, a canonical function mapping everyone onto The Time. Translation means no shared tick, with local tempos relatable without being unified - coordination as translatability rather than as identity of rhythm. The wager is that AI functions as a completing technology precisely by making translation cheap and ambient, so that groups no longer need one canonical clock in order to coordinate at all.

Where this is being taken (working synthesis)

Developed as a companion piece to "Circular Error" and "Looking for Duree," growing out of the Contraptions Book Club's May 2026 reading of David Landes's Revolution in Time. Draws on E.P. Thompson's Time, Work-Discipline, and Industrial Capitalism for the social-synchronization claim, 2025-2026 interoception research on duration perception, and a first-person observation about COVID's effect on the fusion of time and place.

How settled this is

Landes's historical claim about mechanical timekeeping manufacturing shared time is well-established and directly sourced. The two-by-two grid and its four named cells are this brief's own construction, locked as a working decision rather than derived from an external source. Whether "grip" (sovereignty) is genuinely independent of "shape" (the topology of time - cyclical, linear, punctuated) or is actually a consequence of it is explicitly flagged as untested - the brief's own elegance-is-a-warning-sign instinct applies here. Whether translation-without-synchronization is coherent as a durable coordination mechanism at the institutional level, rather than only between individuals, is named as an open question rather than resolved.

Open

Whether the four-cell grid's sovereignty axis is truly independent of the shape (topology) of the time in question, and whether translation between untethered tempos actually scales to institutions rather than only to persons, are the two questions the brief flags most explicitly as untested rather than settled.
