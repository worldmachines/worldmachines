The descent chain: how AI becomes New Nature

Sean · updated 5 September 2026 · source on GitHub
How AI becomes New Nature, as a four-step mechanism rather than an observation: profit pulls AI down the pace-layer stack to infrastructure (Christensen); security fuses it to cryptographic protocols, because strangers can only trust a machine at machine speed if the math checks it; the clock's bargain — holding still below buys freedom above — sinks the fused pair beneath governance; and competition strips away every remaining place to complain, because each one is an attack surface, a delay, and a cost. Remove any step and you get a familiar failure: vaporware, AI that churns, standards that fossilize, or a monopoly you can still sue.

The idea

Stewart Brand's pace layers stack a civilization from fast to slow: fashion, commerce, infrastructure, governance, culture, nature. The normal direction of travel is up — nature was here first and everything else was built on it. Sean's essay argued that AI is moving the other way, from the fast layers toward infrastructure, because that is where the money is. This note continues the argument past infrastructure, and turns what was an observation (AI and cryptographic protocols keep showing up together) into a mechanism with four steps. Each step has a driver and a precedent, and they can be said in one breath: profit pulls it down, security fuses it together, the bargain sinks it, selection seals it shut.

Step 1 — Profit pulls AI to infrastructure. Clayton Christensen's rule: attractive profits go to whichever layer of a stack is not yet good enough, and right now that is the plumbing. So AI gets built into the plumbing, and the layers above it become commodities. This is the argument of Sean's essay and it is already visible in where capital is flowing. Status: established.

Step 2 — Security fuses AI to cryptography. An infrastructure built from AI alone cannot be trusted by strangers without inspection: it can be prompted, spoofed, injected, and you cannot audit a vibe. Someone would have to keep watching it, and being watched is the opposite of being infrastructure. The only thing that checks on your behalf with no trusted party is cryptography — signatures, hashes, consensus, payment rails. So trust at machine speed exists only where the math checks the model. The converse also holds: protocols without something adaptive around them fossilize (Rao's question about how protocols stay evolvable). Neither survives alone, so the fused form is the stable one. The plain image is a bank: loan officers plus a ledger. Officers without a ledger is fraud; a ledger without officers is an abacus. That fusion is why you never think about your bank. Status: conjecture — the speculative step. The three questions in appeal-foreclosure are its falsifiers.

Step 3 — The bargain sinks the pair below governance. Florian Lohse's case: clocks worldwide agree to within a millionth of a second, and because the bottom holds perfectly still, everything above it is free to run out of step. Rigidity below buys liberty above. Generalized: a thing moves down the stack when the freedom it buys above is worth more than the rigidity it costs below. The fused AI–protocol pair makes that trade, and the trade carries it beneath the governance layer. Status: precedented (clock time; public-key cryptography).

Step 4 — Competition seals off appeal. Every remaining place where you could petition the system — a support desk, an override, a committee — is simultaneously an attack surface (social engineering, SIM-swaps), a tempo bottleneck (humans in the loop at machine speed), and a cost. Systems that shed those places win against systems that keep them. So unpetitionability is selected for, not stumbled into, and irreversibility becomes the security property rather than a side effect. Sean's de-dramatization is what this step feels like from inside: drama is what it feels like to have someone to argue with. Status: precedented (irreversibility as security is standard practice in cryptographic design).

Remove a step, get a failure

The chain also explains the failures, which is the main reason to believe it.

  • No pull → the Semantic Web: well designed, technically sound, and no money ever pulled it downward. Never descends.
  • No fusion, AI side → AI alone: endlessly generative, never accumulates. Churns at the top.
  • No fusion, protocol side → protocol alone: the fixed order with nothing alive in it. Fossilizes.
  • No seal → the platform monopoly: it descends through step 3 but keeps its CEO and its courtrooms. It can be captured, sued, and outlived. This is the answer to "isn't Google already New Nature?" — no, because you can still sue Google.
  • All four → arrives alive: fixed underneath, still generating above.

How settled this is

Steps 1, 3 and 4 rest on precedent and I would defend them. Step 2 is the load-bearing conjecture: it claims the fused form is the unique stable equilibrium, and that claim could be wrong if some non-cryptographic trust mechanism at machine speed turns out to exist. The chain as a whole should be read as a mechanism that licenses a long-horizon expectation (see skyhooks-and-cranes — this is meant to be a crane), not as a forecast.

Where it comes from

Working synthesis from Sean Stevenson's preparation for "De-Dramatizing the Digital: How AI Descends Into New Nature" (September 2026). Step 1 is Sean's essay (Christensen). Step 3 is Florian Lohse's clock-time argument, generalized. The endoskeleton image (hard core, adaptive surface) is from Rao's New Nature slides. The four-step structure, the step names, and the failure map were developed in the talk preparation and are not something Sean has published; treat them as a proposal.

Open
