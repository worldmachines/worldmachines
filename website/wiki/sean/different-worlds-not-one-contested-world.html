Different worlds, not one contested world

Sean · updated 11 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Descola's ontological pluralism reframes disagreement: not always one person right and one wrong about a shared world, but sometimes two people carving up what counts as a world differently in the first place — a reframe that changes the shape of a dispute without resolving it.

The idea

Most disagreement assumes a single contested world: two claims about the same referent, and someone is wrong. Philippe Descola's ontological pluralism complicates that assumption at the level of whole cultures — animism, totemism, analogism, and naturalism are not competing beliefs held about one shared nature/culture divide, but different schemes for dividing up interiority and physicality in the first place. Two people working from different schemes are not disagreeing about the same object; they may not share an object to disagree about.

Applied to ordinary disagreement rather than to comparing whole cultures, the reframe is unsettling in a useful way. It doesn't require granting that every position is equally true, and it doesn't dissolve real disputes. It does change what a dispute is: less a correction to be won, more a difference of scheme to be identified — sometimes lived alongside, sometimes not. The practical effect is a kind of loosening. If two people are not straightforwardly fighting over the same world, the stakes of "winning" the disagreement shift.

Grounding

Drawn from Philippe Descola's Beyond Nature and Culture — the four-way ontology typology is his, laid out in more detail in descola-four-ontologies. The extension to ordinary interpersonal disagreement, rather than comparison between whole cultures, is original to this note.

How settled this is

Descola's typology is an anthropological argument built from ethnographic comparison and checkable directly against the book. Whether it's the right lens for everyday disagreement between two people who share a culture — as opposed to comparing societies that don't — is this note's own extension, offered because it's a useful loosening device, not because it's been tested as a general theory of disagreement.
