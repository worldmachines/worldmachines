Disenchantment was a relocation, not a loss

Sean · updated 5 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Disenchantment is better read as a relocation than a subtraction - meaning did not vanish when the enchanted cosmos was rationalized away, it moved into the Subject, with Romanticism as the receipt - and Riskin supplies the missing mechanism: seventeenth-century science banished agency from nature as a condition of its method, requiring a monopoly god to hold that agency, so when the god later receded the agency never flowed back.

The idea

Weber's Entzauberung names modernity's rationalization of the world until magic, spirits, and intrinsic meaning drain out, leaving a calculable mechanism - and the lament built on top of that description assumes a fall from a meaningful, enchanted cosmos into a barren one. Taylor's term for this shape of argument is a subtraction story: it treats the meaningless mechanical cosmos as a neutral residue exposed once illusions are removed, rather than as a positive, constructed picture in its own right. Once the subtraction premise is dropped, disenchantment stops being a loss and becomes a relocation - meaning did not disappear, it changed address, moving out of an external sacred cosmos and into the Subject. Romanticism is the receipt for the move: the self swells to hold what the cosmos used to hold, which is what licenses Bloom's line that the self is the modern version of the soul. The world went quiet exactly as the Subject grew large enough to carry the meaning the world had been carrying.

Two further moves sharpen the case. First, the enchanted past is partly retrojection: the medieval world was not experienced as "enchanted" until moderns invented the contrast in order to mourn it - enchantment is a modern category, born of its own supposed absence. Second, in Blumenberg's terms this is a reoccupation rather than a subtraction: the structural position "where meaning lives" was vacated at the cosmic level and reoccupied at the level of the self. Disenchantment of the world and enchantment of the Subject are one event with two faces.

Riskin's The Restless Clock supplies what Taylor's version was missing: a dated mechanism with named actors. The seventeenth-century banishment of agency, perception, and will from nature was not an incidental byproduct of the new science - it was an informing principle of modern theology at the same time, because a material world stripped of its own agency required an external god to hold a monopoly on it. Descartes, Boyle, and Newton did the banishing as the condition of their method. The consequence follows directly: when that external god later receded from explanatory duty, the agency did not flow back into nature. It went nowhere - biologists kept (and still keep) the verbs of agency for unmeant final causes, writing that genes "dictate" and proteins "control" as habits of speech no one quite believes and no one has fully let go of either.

If disenchantment and Subject-inflation are a single event, Subject- exhaustion should collapse the same binary that produced "disenchantment" in the first place. Properly understood, "re-enchantment" would not mean summoning spirits back - it would mean the subject/object split failing, not one side of it winning.

Where it comes from

Working synthesis, developed as the object-pole companion to Sean's Subject-exhaustion seed. Draws on Max Weber (Entzauberung), Charles Taylor (A Secular Age, Sources of the Self - subtraction stories, the buffered self), Hans Blumenberg (Work on Myth - distance from the absolutism of reality), Harold Bloom (the self as the modern version of the soul), and Jessica Riskin, The Restless Clock (the dated mechanism and its theological condition), supplemented by a 2026-08-29 vault reading of Riskin against a companion volume, Grammars of Creation.

How settled this is

Weber's disenchantment and Taylor's subtraction-story critique are established readings, not conjecture. The Blumenberg-flavored reframe (relocation, not loss) and its extension to Subject-exhaustion are this seed's own synthesis. Riskin's historical claim about the theological condition of early-modern mechanism is a scholarly argument from a specific, cited book, not independently verified here beyond the vault's own reading notes.

Open
