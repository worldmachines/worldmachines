The instrument becomes the self

Sean · updated 5 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Past a certain threshold of intimacy, a personal instrument - a feed, a model, a companion app - stops functioning as a tool the self uses and starts functioning as identity itself, making an unautomated interior, a self able to step outside its own instruments, an increasingly scarce condition rather than a default one.

The idea

David Landes's own observation that a personal timepiece could become a "psychic luxury" - no longer a tool but part of one's identity - generalizes past clocks to any sufficiently intimate personal instrument: a feed, a model, a companion. The claim this row makes is that once an instrument crosses that threshold, the difficulty migrates from having tools to having any unautomated interior left at all - a self that retains the capacity to step outside its own instruments and notice them as instruments becomes the scarce resource, not the default condition. The practical acceptance is that some delegation of memory and decision-making to instruments is inevitable and not itself the problem; what has to be actively protected is a private interior, maintained on purpose through periodic "instrument fasts" rather than assumed to persist on its own. The failure mode inverts the usual worry about tools: the map becomes the navigator rather than a guide the navigator consults, and a billion privately-tuned selves, each oriented by its own instrument, cannot share a world with each other - which loops directly back to the vanishing of a shared present described elsewhere in this set.

Where it comes from

One row ("The instrument becomes the self") of a working table of bets for the World Machines project: a running list of structural errors in how today's fixes for the Divergence Machine's limitations themselves fail, each paired with a falsifiable, near-term bet about how the failure will surface.

How settled this is

A working hypothesis; the associated bet is that AI-companion attachment produces documented harms serious enough to trigger regulation in at least one major jurisdiction, and that "digital autonomy" or "the right to an unoptimized self" enters public discourse as a named concept.

Connections

  • vanishing-now - explicitly linked by the table of bets itself: a billion privately-tuned selves cannot share a shared present, which is the same erosion of collective synchrony from a different cause
  • four-cell-coordination-grid - the "instrument fast" as a deliberate practice for protecting an unautomated interior is a personal-scale version of the sovereignty this grid asks for at the level of whole tempos, not just single instruments

Open

Whether "periodic instrument fasts" are a genuinely sufficient protection against this drift, or whether the pull toward instrument-as-identity is strong enough that no merely personal discipline holds against it, is left as the open practical question.
