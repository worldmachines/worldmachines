The vanishing now

Sean · updated 5 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Private, on-demand access to time erodes the shared, synchronous present it seemed to free everyone from, so the difficulty migrates from keeping time to convening it - synchrony itself becomes scarce, and value concentrates in whoever can still summon a live public.

The idea

Everyone now owns their own access to the clock and syncs on demand, which sounds like freedom and functions as an erosion: a shared, synchronous now is the resource that quietly disappears. The difficulty does not vanish, it moves - from the old problem of keeping accurate time to the new problem of convening people at the same time at all. A workable response accepts a thinner shared now rather than trying to re-impose universal synchrony everywhere; the practical move is to protect a small number of high-value synchronies (civic moments, genuine crises) instead of insisting on constant collective presence. The failure mode is a democracy or a coordination system that has lost the capacity to act together because it no longer has a common present to deliberate inside - publics splinter into timelines that never overlap, and collective response stalls exactly when it is needed.

Where it comes from

One row ("The vanishing now") of a working table of bets for the World Machines project: a running list of structural errors in how today's fixes for the Divergence Machine's limitations themselves fail, each paired with a falsifiable, near-term bet about how the failure will surface.

How settled this is

A working hypothesis in a table of bets explicitly designed to be falsifiable - the associated bet (that live, synchronous attention keeps rising in price while recorded content trends toward free) is offered as a testable prediction, not an established finding.

Connections

  • four-cell-coordination-grid - the same shared-present problem, developed at length there through the One Clock/Many Clocks grid and the synchronization/translation fork
  • substrate-and-criterion-split - losing a shared present is one concrete cost of the criterion slot (what confers legitimate direction) being left vacant rather than refilled

Open

Whether "a few high-value synchronies" is actually a stable equilibrium, or whether the erosion continues until no shared synchrony survives at all, is the bet this note is built to test against events rather than argue from first principles.
