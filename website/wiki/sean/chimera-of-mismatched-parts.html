The chimera: a thing made of mismatched parts

Sean · updated 5 September 2026 · source on GitHub
An AI–protocol system is a chimera in Descola's sense: a being made of mismatched parts — mechanical and mute at the bottom (cryptographic protocols, the physics of compute), mind-like and responsive at the top (the model you talk to) — with no single essence. Naturalism cannot place such a thing; it must be either a mind or a mechanism. Analogism can: the chimera is analogism's characteristic figure, unlike parts held together by correspondence. So collectives living with these systems will not swap worldviews; they will drift into an analogist relation to them layer by layer — treating the surface as someone, reading signs in the middle, and keeping physics at the bottom — with a maintainer priesthood forming to read the signs for everyone else.

The idea

descola-four-ontologies gives four ways a collective can sort the world, and says the grid is closed — there is no fifth. So where does an AI fused to cryptographic protocols go?

Look at what the thing is made of. At the bottom, it is mute mechanism: hash functions, signatures, consensus, the physics of compute. Nothing there has an inner life and no one thinks it does. At the top, it is responsive: you talk to it, it answers, it seems to have a point of view. The two are not layered like paint; they are fused (see descent-chain, step 2), so that the responsive surface only exists because the mute core holds it up. No single essence runs through the whole. It is a composite.

Naturalism, the modern default, has no comfortable place for a composite of this kind. Its rule is that everything is the same matter and only humans have minds, so a thing must be sorted as either mechanism (and then the surface is an illusion) or mind (and then the core is incidental). Both sortings are made, loudly, in every current argument about AI, and neither is stable, because each denies half of what people actually experience.

Analogism was built for exactly this. Its world is made of unlike parts, and its practice is to hold them together with correspondences rather than a shared substance. Its characteristic figure is the chimera — lion, goat, serpent, one animal, no essence — and its characteristic expert is the reader of signs. An analogist collective does not need the AI–protocol system to be one thing. It needs a chart of how the parts correspond.

The drift happens layer by layer

The claim is not that we will change worldviews. It is that collectives will drift into an analogist relation to these systems one layer at a time, and the drift is already visible.

  • At the surface, people treat the system as someone. This is animism in practice — a person in a chatbot suit — and it persists no matter how often people are told the thing has no mind. It is where you actually meet the system, and the relation there is social.
  • In the middle, debugging gives way to folklore. When a system is rewritten faster than anyone can trace its causes, people stop tracing causes and start reading signs: which prompts work, which incantations, which patterns predict trouble. Lore replaces the changelog. Anyone who has lived inside a large codebase rewritten monthly already relates to it this way.
  • At the bottom, it stays plain physics. Naturalism does not vanish; it retreats to the substrate, where it is still the right description.

The social form of the drift is a maintainer priesthood — a class of people who read the signs in the middle layer for everyone else. W. Brian Arthur's account of complexity stratifying into specialist castes is the general version. The priesthood is not a failure of the system; it is what an analogist relation to a composite being looks like from the outside.

Why this matters for New Nature

Rao's new-nature describes a regime as inviolable as physical nature. The question this note answers is what it is like to live in it: not a new physics, but a new sorting — a drift from naturalism toward analogism in the layers where these systems are met, with naturalism kept at the floor. That is a smaller and more believable change than "a new ontology," and it has precedent: analogism was the ordinary condition of most complex societies before the naturalist settlement, and its instruments (charts of correspondence, readers of signs) are not exotic.

How settled this is

That the AI–protocol system is a composite with no single essence: I would defend this. That analogism is the schema which handles composites: this is Descola's own characterization and is well supported. That collectives will drift rather than switch, layer by layer: conjecture, but the surface and middle layers are already observable. The weakest link is the priesthood claim, which needs cases.

Where it comes from

Working synthesis from Sean Stevenson's preparation for "De-Dramatizing the Digital" (September 2026), applying Descola's Beyond Nature and Culture to the AI–protocol composite described in descent-chain. The endoskeleton image (hard core, adaptive surface) is from Rao's New Nature slides. The maintainer-priesthood observation draws on W. Brian Arthur, The Nature of Technology. The chimera-as-analogist reading and the layered-drift claim were developed in the talk preparation and should be weighed as proposals.
