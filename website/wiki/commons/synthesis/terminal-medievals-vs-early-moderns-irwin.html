Terminal Medievals vs. Early Moderns: Irwin's Ibn Khaldun

Commons · Synthesis · updated 28 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Irwin's reception history supplies the evidence for the retrofitting problem in the Ibn Khaldun case and refines it: remaking him began with his own disciples and the Ottomans, long before modernity needed origin myths; the mechanism is each reader's need for an ancestor; and the discarded parts (law, occultism, futurology) are exactly what makes him terminal-medieval.

Extends terminal-medievals-vs-early-moderns with the reception history in irwin-ibn-khaldun.

The parent page names ibn-khaldun as one of its two primary cases and explains the pattern by modernity's need for origin myths. Irwin's biography is the fullest evidence for the case. Read across its chapters, it also refines the explanation in three ways that no single reading note states on its own.

1. The retrofit is older than modernity

The parent page treats retrofitting as something modernity does to the premodern. Irwin's evidence shows the same move made by readers who were not modern at all:

  • His own disciple. Al-Maqrizi kept the conviction that history matters, the pessimism and the idea of history as warning, but ignored ‘asabiyya and tribesmen and moralized the economics. Irwin says he “failed to grasp Ibn Khaldun's underlying methodology” (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch05-autobiography-outsider).
  • The Ottomans. Katib Çelebi and others took up the “organic life cycle of regimes” when they began to doubt their empire's eternity, but “unlike Ibn Khaldun, entertained hopes that the decline of their regime could be arrested” (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch10-afterlife-french-germans).
  • The Nahda. The Arab rediscovery came as a re-export from European editions, with al-Tahtawi treating patriotism as a kind of ‘asabiyya (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch10-colonialist-mahdi-gellner).

Irwin's diagnosis is therefore wider than the parent's. What drives the remaking is any discipline's “hunger for an ancestor” and the self-perpetuating gravity of a heavily studied subject. Scholarship shows “many agendas”, each making an Ibn Khaldun in its own image (Gibb's pious Christian finding a pious Muslim, Lacoste's Marxist, Mahdi's Straussian) (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch10-arab-american-novels-orientalism). Modernity's origin myths are one agenda among these.

2. The catalogue of retrofits

The modern readings form a spectrum of politics, and that spread is itself evidence that the text is being used rather than read:

  • Colonial and anti-colonial. De Slane's translation was commissioned by the French Ministry of War. Gautier gave Ibn Khaldun “a perfume of the Renaissance” and cut him off from the Arabs. Monteil, who opposed torture in Algeria, still made him a precursor of Machiavelli, the Enlightenment, sociology and Darwin (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch10-afterlife-french-germans). Lacoste's Marxist is “as anachronistic and culture-bound as Gautier's” (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch10-colonialist-mahdi-gellner).
  • Philosophical. Von Kremer's Hegelian, Mahdi's concealed Straussian rationalist, Gellner's value-free sociologist, Hodgson's secular faylasuf, Dale's Aristotelian (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch10-afterlife-french-germans, irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch10-colonialist-mahdi-gellner, irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch10-toynbee-rosenthal, irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch10-arab-american-novels-orientalism).
  • Right and left economics. Reagan's supply-sider and Engels's witness to Islamic stasis are opposite political poles, and Irwin rejects both (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch08-economics).
  • Civilizational. Toynbee's “long-lost relative”, fitted to Toynbee's own laws of withdrawal and return (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch10-toynbee-rosenthal).

3. What gets cut off is what makes him terminal-medieval

Irwin's closing list of what modernizing readers discard is Maliki jurisprudence, occultism, futurology and bizarre scientific ideas (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch11-ending-up). Each item is documented elsewhere in the book: the principles of history modeled on jurisprudence (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch04-aristotle-theology-maliki-law), sorcery real but forbidden (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch07-supernatural-occult), the Mahdi and the End of Time (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch07-future-last-days), and a Sufism the nineteenth-century positivists preferred not to see (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch06-sufi-mystic). Put together, the parts the retrofitters remove are the parts that tie him to a dying intellectual mode, and the parts they keep (‘asabiyya, the cycle, the critique of credulous historians) are the ones that can be detached. That is the parent page's claim about working “within received frameworks”, supported in detail: in Irwin's phrase the Qur’an and Maliki law books “did his thinking for him” (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch11-ending-up), in a culture where “originality was at a discount” (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch09-teaching-writing). See muqaddima-enchanted-frame-irwin.

Where Irwin does not follow the parent page

  • Irwin gives no account of what modernity had to clear away. His Ibn Khaldun is not foreclosed by a Modernity Machine; he is simply read wrongly by later people. The structural explanation on the parent page (modernity-machine) is Rao's, not Irwin's.
  • Irwin grants more than the “terminal” label suggests. Ibn Khaldun's abstract generalizing is “almost unique among medieval historians” (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch04-aristotle-theology-maliki-law). His “perhaps delusive appearance of modernity” is real enough to need explaining (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch03-wisdom-literature-ibar). And his models bear comparison with Darwin, Marx and Durkheim while being inapplicable to modern societies (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch11-ending-up).
  • In the sense of the Cervantes extension (terminal-medievals-vs-early-moderns-cervantes), Ibn Khaldun is inside the dying mode rather than diagnosing it. Irwin's picture of a man expecting the End of Time and seeing no progress (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch07-future-last-days) fits that.

A caution for World Machines

Irwin's catalogue puts WMP's own use of Ibn Khaldun on the list of possible agendas. A project that wants a cyclical ancestor for secular-cycles or world-machines is making the move Irwin describes, and Irwin includes himself among possible offenders (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch10-arab-american-novels-orientalism). The discipline his book recommends is that “the contrasts matter more than the comparisons” (irwin-ibn-khaldun-preface): resonances are resonances, not anticipations.
