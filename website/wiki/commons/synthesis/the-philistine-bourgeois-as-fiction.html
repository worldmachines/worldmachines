The Philistine Bourgeois as Fiction: Gay's *Pleasure Wars* beside Carey

Commons · Synthesis · updated 26 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Read together, the Pleasure Wars notes show Gay's 'philistine bourgeois' doing the rhetorical work Carey attributes to 'the mass' (an elastic, homogenizing enemy category that licenses the artist's self-image) while differing in one decisive respect: the despised class was the paymaster, so the contempt ran alongside dependence and courtship rather than exclusion, and the fiction outlived that contradiction by passing into historiography.

Kind: synthesis across the gay-bourgeois-experience-v5 notes. Almost every note in the batch compares Gay's bourgeoisophobes with carey-intellectuals-and-masses or the-mass-as-fiction, and each note says the fit is partial. Read one at a time, these look like scattered asides. Read together, the parts that fit and the parts that do not each form a consistent pattern.

What Fits: The Elasticity Test

the-mass-as-fiction treats the instability of the mass-images as evidence that the category is fictional. The Gay notes supply a parallel set of shifting targets:

  • Flaubert's "bourgeois" covers the smock and the frock coat, the Communards, and "all humanity." The note's own qualification: "the family resemblance is in the elasticity, not the referent" (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-bexp-s1-flaubertus).
  • Zola chants "Bourgeois! bourgeois!" until the word carries no content, and also concedes "we are all bourgeois" (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-bexp-s2-six-pack-philistines).
  • Berlioz's and Joachim's "public en gros" erases responses Gay has just shown to be earnest, such as Behncke's honeymoon diary (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch01-s2-affordable-masterpieces).
  • Shaw "needs a single stupid antagonist to justify his own vocation as guide": "that great booby ... the British bourgeoisie" (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch03-s1-guides-to-pleasure).
  • Frans Coenen's uniformly "stiff" Victorian bourgeois is "a polemical caricature ... that later historians mistook for sociological description" (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch06-s4-against-the-need-for-probability). Saint-Saëns's "bon bourgeois" is "as much a rhetorical construct as Carey's 'mass'" (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch06-s2-modernists-vs-modernists).

Gay's counter-method also mirrors Carey's. He breaks the monolith into individuals. The press and private responses to Wilde's fall scatter across the whole range (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch03-s3-from-zola-to-wilde). "Generalizations suffer shipwreck on the rock of diversity" (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch06-s2-modernists-vs-modernists). The Coda's emblem for the class is Gropius's favorite color, "bunt," meaning multicolored (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-coda-a-bourgeois-experience).

What Does Not Fit: The Fiction's Object Paid

The notes' hedges point the same way.

  1. Target and timing. Flaubert in 1850s Rouen was reacting to "an established commercial class, not a newly enfranchised reading public" (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-bexp-s1-flaubertus). The bourgeoisophobes attacked "a specific class fraction, often one that is buying their books and pianos" (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-bexp-s2-six-pack-philistines).

  2. Dependence.

    • The artist's independence was "often achieved not in the face of bourgeois but with their aid" (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-bexp-s2-six-pack-philistines).
    • Morris scorned the class that alone could afford his goods (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch01-s3-limits-and-possibilities).
    • "It was the middle classes that nourished their sworn enemies" (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch06-s1-a-profusion-of-alternatives).
    • Bourgeois collectors rescued Picasso and built modernist architecture (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch06-s4-against-the-need-for-probability). The Maecenases were modernism's "patrons and midwives" (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch04-s3-the-victorian-maecenas).
  3. Function. Denouncing the bourgeoisie was "a screen" for the struggle to get access to dealers, critics and museum directors (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-bexp-s3-confusing-confrontations). It was leverage inside a market, not a way of shutting out a public.
  4. Direction. Much of the contempt pointed backward, at the artist's own family and class of origin (Gauguin, Picasso) (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch06-s4-against-the-need-for-probability), or sideways within the class (Lichtwark's reforming attack; the Liebermann portrait as "intrabourgeois conflict") (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch05-s3-the-perspiring-philistine-on-trial). As the note on Baudelaire puts it, the sleight of hand "runs the other way": not inventing a contemptible mass, but scapegoating a class that "actually built the museums" (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-bexp-s3-confusing-confrontations).

The Connection

These readings belong to this page. No single note makes them.

  1. The fiction-making move is not tied to one target. In Gay's material the operation Carey identifies (a homogenizing enemy category that dignifies the person who names it) appears a generation or more earlier. It is aimed at a different class, with a different relationship to the people making it. That suggests the move is part of how the modern artist legitimated himself, not a specific reaction to the mass-literacy shock. The genealogies differ, though. Gay's runs from Werther to Nietzsche (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-bexp-s2-six-pack-philistines, gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-coda-a-bourgeois-experience). Nothing in the notes corresponds to Carey's theological ancestry.
  2. A paymaster cannot be shut out, only scorned. Carey's intelligentsia could fence off a mass readership with difficulty (illegibility-as-elite-defense). Gay's artists could not shut out the class that bought their work. So their contempt stayed rhetorical and ran alongside courtship: Baudelaire's dedication to the bourgeoisie (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-bexp-s3-confusing-confrontations), Apollinaire's "We are not your enemies" (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch06-s2-modernists-vs-modernists). Contempt worked as a credential, not a gate: for Debussy's generation, being despised by ordinary audiences was "a virtual credential of authentic modernism" (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch06-s2-modernists-vs-modernists).
  3. The fiction outlived its own contradiction by changing hands. The artists kept the myth while living on bourgeois money. Then historians "took this self-interested polemic literally" (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-coda-a-bourgeois-experience). Gay's whole project is a response to that second transmission. For Gay the persistence of the fiction is a problem of historiography, not only of aesthetics.

What This Reading Must Hold

  • Gay is an advocate. He grants that the caricature sometimes fits (Carnegie, the Vanderbilts, Krupp) (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch04-s2-collecting-as-autobiography), and that wealthy donors really did make riskless taste permanent (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch05-s2-buttresses-for-the-establishment). Rehabilitating the bourgeoisie is his thesis, so his evidence is selected for it.
  • The two accounts partly pull apart. Carey's intellectuals hold their contempt "as settled doctrine." Gay insists, through Tonio Kröger, that it "was never uncomplicated even among the bourgeoisophobes themselves" (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-bexp-s3-confusing-confrontations).
  • Not every critic seceded. Gay's Victorian critics are "a narrower, less totalizing cousin" of Carey's modernists, still trying to educate their bourgeois audience (gay-bourgeois-experience-v5-ch03-s1-guides-to-pleasure).
  • Both readings stand. This page does not reconcile Carey's account of the mass with Gay's account of the bourgeois. It records that the same rhetorical operation appears in both, and that it differs in whom it targets and what it costs the people who use it.
