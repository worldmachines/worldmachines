The Anti-Market Needs a Market: Capitalism Keeps the Floor Beneath It Competitive

Commons · Synthesis · updated 24 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Read together, Braudel's Volume II notes on haulage, shipping, manufactories, mining, plantations, putting-out, printing and the trading hierarchy show capitalism's anti-market not merely coexisting with the competitive market economy but depending on, and acting to preserve, a fragmented and specialized floor beneath it, from which its price-setting margin comes; Braudel himself claims only coexistence.

Kind: synthesis across the braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2 notes on production, transport, colonial agriculture and the trading hierarchy. Braudel says capitalism coexists with the market economy "over and alongside" it (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2-ch05-retrospective-geography-rationalism-florence). Read together, these notes support a stronger claim that none of them states. In sector after sector, the actors of the anti-market depend on the floor beneath them staying fragmented, competitive and specialized, and they act to keep it that way. Their monopoly above and the competition below are one arrangement, not two neighbouring ones.

The series

  • Road haulage. Overland carriage moved most of old-regime goods. Merchants kept it unrationalized. Peasant carters were paid "at less than its true cost price." A 1782 edict declared haulage "absolutely free," and merchant opposition defeated repeated royal attempts to monopolize the messageries. The note's own gloss: the merchant "simply swam with the current," because unorganized competition among hauliers let merchants, not carriers, set the price. "Capitalism prefers to sit above a competitive substrate it does not need to own" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2-ch03-industrial-profits-overland-transport).
  • Shipping. Sea transport ran on small competitive carriers with thin margins (Dutch voyage profits averaged 6%). Two attempts to found an Amsterdam marine-insurance monopoly failed because merchants judged the premiums would exceed the profits they protected. The owning merchant treated a ship as "a secondary asset in service of trade" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2-ch03-river-sea-transport-balance-sheet).
  • Manufactories. Even concentrated manufactories were "virtually without exception" half embedded in putting-out. They kept outworkers beside a concentrated core "so output could flex with demand without payroll risk" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2-ch03-mining-manufactories-finance).
  • Mining and plantations. Once European mining costs rose, capital relocated extraction to the periphery. It kept the long trading chains, where "the real profits were made," "not so much at the stage of extraction itself" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2-ch03-mining-manufactories-finance). Production is "consistently the most exploited, least remunerative link" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2-ch02-markets-and-their-geography). The engenho returned 4–5%, the St Domingue planter ate "a little bread with preserves," and Bordeaux's wholesalers were "the masters of the machine" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2-ch03-second-serfdom-american-plantations).
  • Textiles and guilds. The Verleger took over sourcing and sale while "outward appearances were maintained." Guild masters became wage-earners inside a shell of guild autonomy (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2-ch03-merchants-guilds-verlagssystem). In Bourgin's Category B the merchant-entrepreneur profits from coordination alone and owns none of the workshops (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2-ch03-fourfold-classification-pre-industry).
  • Printing. At Lyons the master-printers owned the presses but "did not really represent capital." They depended on merchant publishers and had wage suppression as their only lever (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2-ch05-revolutions-workers-revolts-order).
  • The trading hierarchy. Specialization "intensif[ies] only at the bottom," while the wholesaler refuses it. In the note's words, "specialization is a mark of subordination, not mastery" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2-ch04-trade-hierarchy-and-success).

The connection

Each note reads its own case as capital avoiding a poor sector or declining an unprofitable commitment. Placed side by side, they show more than avoidance.

  1. The same kind of actor holds both positions. Merchants defended free haulage and refused an insurance monopoly priced against them. Merchants are also who other notes show cornering Loire wine, stockpiling in Amsterdam warehouses or rigging the Dunkerque quay (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2-ch04-monopolies-currency-and-profits, braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2-ch04-capitalist-mentality-and-long-distance-trade). As a class, they opposed monopoly on the floor they bought services from and pursued it where they sold.
  2. Fragmentation below is where the margin comes from. Carriers, outworkers, small shipowners, indebted planters and dependent master-printers were all numerous, specialized and substitutable. That is why the price was set above them and why they, not the merchant, carried the costs of idle capacity, seasonal labour and ruin. The trading hierarchy's rule, that specialization is subordination, is the general form of these cases.
  3. So the "negative balance sheet" is partly a position, not only a failure. Braudel's verdict that pre-industrial capitalism "scarcely penetrated production" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2-ch03-river-sea-transport-balance-sheet) and his rule that industrial gains were dwarfed by trading profits (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2-ch03-industrial-profits-overland-transport) read, in these notes, as reasons capital stayed out. Together they suggest that staying out was also what kept the price-setting position. Owning the fragmented floor would have meant inheriting its thin margins and its risks.

What this reading must hold

  • Braudel does not state it. His explicit claim is coexistence. The logic of monopoly grows out of free competition "without abolishing it" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2-ch05-retrospective-geography-rationalism-florence). The claim that competition below is maintained by the anti-market is this page's.
  • The anti-market also rigs the floor directly. "Competition without competitors" catalogues Parisian meat cartels, a nine-year glass-bottle monopoly and the Dunkerque rule pricing small buyers off the quay (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2-ch04-capitalist-mentality-and-long-distance-trade). Leyden's manufacturers ran a wage-fixing cartel across Holland (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2-ch05-revolutions-workers-revolts-order). The pattern above is common, not universal.
  • Mining is the exception that became a hinge. In deep mining, merchant capital did take over production and made a wage proletariat, before withdrawing when returns fell (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2-ch03-merchants-guilds-verlagssystem). After the industrial revolution, machinery made industry itself a profit sector and capitalism invaded production (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2-ch03-river-sea-transport-balance-sheet). The reading describes the pre-industrial arrangement, not a law.
  • Some floors could not be netted. The Gévaudan's custom of immediate cash payment at the local market defeated a deliberate attempt to install the Verlagssystem. The note offers a loose rhyme with capture-resistance, "structural rather than substantive" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v2-ch03-merchants-guilds-verlagssystem).
