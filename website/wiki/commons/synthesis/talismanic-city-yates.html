Talisman, memory, city: the Hermetic image-machine at three scales (Yates)

Commons · Synthesis · updated 28 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Read across Yates's chapters on Ficino, Bruno and Campanella, one Hermetic technique of arranging celestial images to draw their good influences appears at three scales (the talismanic object, the trained memory, the image-guarded city), and Bruno and Campanella carry it in opposite directions, inward and outward, which matches their different fates.

The pieces

Across yates-giordano-bruno, one operation recurs at different sizes: arranging celestial images so that what they are turned toward (a body, a mind, a people) takes on their good influences and escapes the bad. Yates treats each case in its own chapter.

  • The object. Ficino's "figure of the world" is a heaven-model (a medal, a ceiling, perhaps a clock) built to draw down Solar, Jovial and Venereal influence and avoid Saturn and Mars. It is looked at and then held within, so that the viewer sees things unified by a higher image (yates-giordano-bruno-ch04-ficino-natural-magic-2). Yates's hedged extensions of the same logic are Botticelli's Primavera as a talisman (same note) and Catherine de' Medici's 1581 Ballet comique as "an extended and complicated talisman" (yates-giordano-bruno-ch10-religious-hermetism).
  • The mind. Bruno's memory systems place talismanic images on memory wheels to tune the Magus to cosmic powers (yates-giordano-bruno-ch11-bruno-first-paris). The Spaccio's reform of the forty-eight constellations is, on Yates's reading, a reform of personality that masters the stars by choosing their good gifts (yates-giordano-bruno-ch12-hermetic-reform-2). In Germany the animated statues of the Asclepius are moved inward as "an inner cult, not an outer cult with temples and rites" (yates-giordano-bruno-ch17-bruno-germany). His last book encodes the same reform in twelve principles and their talismanic images, to form a Solar, Jovial and Venereal Magus (yates-giordano-bruno-ch18-last-published-work).
  • The city. The Picatrix's Adocentyn is a city founded by Hermes and guarded by images, where astral magic enforces virtue (yates-giordano-bruno-ch03-hermes-and-magic). Yates lines it up with the Asclepius's prophesied city, Bruno's reformed heavens, Campanella's City of the Sun and More's Utopia by their shared ethic of social utility (yates-giordano-bruno-ch12-hermetic-reform-3). Campanella's city has walls painted as an encyclopedia of nature and its stellar correspondences, and a Sun-priest who is Hermes's priest-philosopher-king (yates-giordano-bruno-ch20-campanella-1). In it, a priesthood permanently does what the private anti-eclipse rite for Urban VIII did once (yates-giordano-bruno-ch20-campanella-2). Its science is real but "held and regulated by a supreme priesthood, as in Egypt" (yates-giordano-bruno-ch20-campanella-3).

The connection

Yates links Adocentyn to the Spaccio and to the City of the Sun, and she links the figure of the world to Bruno's memory art. No single chapter sets all three scales side by side. Doing so shows that scale is the variable in this Hermetic technique, not its content. The same planetary lists, the same preference for Sol, Jupiter and Venus over Saturn and Mars, and the same belief that images carry spiritus are used on a bedroom object, on a trained imagination and on a whole walled city.

The two heirs of Ficino move the technique in opposite directions. Bruno moves it inward: from the Spaccio's public reform to the Thirty Statues and the memory treatise for Hainzell, an interior cult. Campanella moves it outward and makes it permanent: the city is the talisman, and its priesthood runs it without pause. Yates's own contrast between the two men fits this pattern, though she does not state it in these terms. Bruno is the more extreme, lacks cunning and is burned. Campanella shifts his reform into orthodox channels, Spain, the papacy and then France, and survives (yates-giordano-bruno-ch20-campanella-1, yates-giordano-bruno-ch20-campanella-3). This page's reading, not Yates's, is that the civic scale was the one a patron could adopt, while the interior scale left its practitioner nothing to offer a patron but himself.

What this implies for the frame, with its hedges

  • A pre-modern image of designed order. At the civic scale the Hermetic city is a founding text that carries a designed social order. The notes flag the rhyme with world-machines and legibility-as-machine-core as loose, because the order is astral and ritual, not administrative (yates-giordano-bruno-ch12-hermetic-reform-3, yates-giordano-bruno-ch20-campanella-3).
  • Legibility pointed the other way. Campanella's wall-encyclopedia makes the cosmos legible to the governed, since children learn every art from the pictures, and the images act on the inhabitants rather than making them readable to a ruler (yates-giordano-bruno-ch20-campanella-1). Set against Legibility as readability by a dominant system, this is the inverse direction. That inversion is this page's gloss.
  • Not a blueprint. Yates insists the City of the Sun is not a modern plan of a well-governed state: its breeding is astrological and it is arranged to be right with the stars. Compare more-utopia, which she treats as a secondary, literary source for Campanella and as a work with an odd magical atmosphere in its worship (yates-giordano-bruno-ch20-campanella-1, yates-giordano-bruno-ch12-hermetic-reform-3).
