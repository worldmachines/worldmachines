The Birth of Thickets

An Interregnum in Space

The legibility regime runs one operation. It converts Space into Place, and everything else it does follows from that.

Space is the undifferentiated field. It holds every possibility and offers no orientation, which is why standing in it feels less like freedom than like vertigo. Draw a boundary around part of it, name what is inside, and you have made a Place. A farm is a Place; beyond the fence is forest. Drawing that boundary is the first act of thought, because you cannot know anything you have not first cut out from everything else. The trouble begins when the operation runs everywhere, on everything, at once, until it stops being thought and becomes weather.

The Modernity Machine ran it at that scale for four centuries, always to the same pattern: take the living thing, name it, standardize it, and ship it somewhere it did not grow. The rubber tree becomes a cog expected to perform identically wherever it is planted, and then the plantation dies of its own tidiness. What four centuries of that produced is the terraformed plain, a landscape the map has already predicted and optimized, where the only way to move is the way the map allows. It is the habitat of the tourist.

Today, putting an idea into the world costs five orders of magnitude less than it did a millennium ago, and the cost of naming, summarising and ranking fell with it until it became free, and often negative. Anything you can say, a model can summarise, and what it summarises it averages. Give a hundred people the same model and each of them improves while the range of approaches collapses: everyone performs well, nobody goes sideways, and the group ends up reasoning worse than it did when its members were all wrong in different directions. Ideas arrive pre-commodified now, the same one surfacing in a dozen heads in the same week with no originator and no common knowledge among the people holding it, several of whom set off chasing an advantage that is already gone.

So the plain has reached back to the moment of having the thought. Two machines are working that ground at once, and the confusion between them explains a great deal. The Modernity Machine is in its Dusk, which does not mean it has gone: a machine in Dusk keeps its apparatus and loses its future. Most of the world’s energy still moves through it, and the reflex to name a thing so that it can be ranked is the part that runs longest. The Divergence Machine has held the Day since about 2000, and it pushes where the old one pulled, scattering people into separate realities instead of drawing them toward a single canonical direction. Which means the flattening you feel is not the work of the machine now in charge. Divergence supplies the material, and the old apparatus, still running, reads it back into something it can index.

The obvious answer would be to leave. You go somewhere else and build: twelve people convened around privacy, a charter, a members’ channel. But you have made a Place, and a Place is what the operation eats, so the plain will absorb it by the end of the quarter. The reflex is old and hard to see from inside. Grow a new shell quickly, armor up, become legible again. Resistance organised as an institution is only the regime with better intentions.

The escape runs instead through the thicket: Space that declined to convert. Its shape is a portal of curated Places opening onto an interior that stays open, and a shared vocabulary or a way of arguing is what those Places are made of. They are real, and they are the fence rather than the point. Mistake the fence for the interior and you fall into container metaphysics, the belief that the anchor is the goal, at which point you stop exploring and the thing you built settles into another tidy square on the plain. The interior has to stay high-entropy, because averaging needs nothing more than an inside orderly enough to summarise.

Keeping an interior open ought to cost something, and under the map it always does. Place is security, Space is freedom: once you are visible you have to choose between bounded and protected or open and exposed, and you pay for whichever you did not pick. Drop below the map’s resolution, though, and the choice does not get split. It stops being available. A regime extracts from what it can see and constrains what it can name, and inside a thicket it can do neither, so security and freedom stop behaving like opposite ends of one axis and arrive together as one property. Both words were only ever naming the same condition: not being handled.
