Politics of description (Clark)

Commons · Entities · updated 28 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Clark's argument that how pre-1848 Europe saw, counted and narrated social distress (the Social Question as a genre of statistical and literary enquiry) was itself political, fed opposed programmes, missed what it could not see, and after 1848 passed into state administration.

Clark's name for the argument that how the social question was seen, counted and narrated before 1848 mattered as much as the suffering itself, and that description was always a political act (clark-revolutionary-spring). The phrase heads the first section of his first chapter. The concern runs through the whole book, both as a way of reading his sources and as a theme in its own right.

The social question as a genre of knowledge

Distress was the backdrop to 1848, not its cause: if misery caused revolution, revolutions would be far more common (clark-revolutionary-spring-ch01-politics-of-description). What changed in the 1830s–40s was a Europe-wide 'culture of enquiry' of official reports, prize essays, journalism and fiction, with numbers, tabulation and a 'period eye' for precise description. The Nantes study by Guépin and Bonamy shows one death per seventy-eight residents a year on a rich quay against one per seventeen on the rue des Fumiers. The enquiry was mostly posed in the third person: what should be done about them? The coinage 'pauperism' registered poverty as collective, permanent and now reaching artisans and smallholders (clark-revolutionary-spring-ch01-precarity-and-crisis).

Same facts, opposed politics

One body of evidence fed Engels's class war, Guépin's Saint-Simonian technocratic reform, Frégier's moralizing policing of the poor and Buret's claim that poverty is inherent in industrial competition. Louis Blanc used Guépin's life-expectancy data to call competition 'a system of extermination for the people' (clark-revolutionary-spring-ch01-politics-of-description).

What the gaze missed

Clark warns that the genre's 'reality effect' hid things. Middle-class diagnosticians fixated on slums and contagion and missed working-class vitality: thickening commercial networks and new forms of labour organization (clark-revolutionary-spring-ch01-politics-of-description). The Lyons weavers answered with their own journal, L'Écho de la Fabrique, which replaced the Social Question's alienated third person with a lexicon drawn from their own experience. The republican Lagrange spoke of proletarians and prostitution in stereotyped terms that the weavers did not use of themselves (clark-revolutionary-spring-ch01-weavers).

Who gets described, and by whom

  • Contested narratives. In Galicia almost every element of the 1846 story is disputed between Austrian and Polish accounts, so nations and classes become legible only through partisan sources (clark-revolutionary-spring-ch01-galicia-1846-conclusions).
  • Women. Women were in virtually all the tumults of 1848, but newspapers, police records and images are mostly male work. The pictures of radical women's clubs are caricatures, and the armed Vesuviennes were a hoax (clark-revolutionary-spring-ch06-waving-from-windows). Earlier, Flora Tristan proposed to break 'the reign of privacy' so that servitude scattered across 'myriad individual biographies' could become visible as systematic oppression (clark-revolutionary-spring-ch02-its-a-mans-world).
  • The countryside. Rural Europe was the 'black box' of 1848. Clark rejects Marx's image of peasants as homologous potatoes and shows a stratified, local countryside that urban revolutionaries preached to rather than listened to (clark-revolutionary-spring-ch07-town-and-country). Against Marx's reading of the Bonaparte vote, he shows that it crossed class lines (clark-revolutionary-spring-ch08-iron-net-very-small-place).
  • Labels that stop seeing. In Vienna, the working-class dead of the inner city joined the heroic narrative while suburban machine-breakers were suppressed as 'riff-raff' (clark-revolutionary-spring-ch04-we-are-dead-shall-we-be-slaves). In Naples the victors of 15 May cast their enemies as 'communists' and 'republicans', a category Massari said existed only in their imagination (clark-revolutionary-spring-ch08-naples-in-summer). Clark's closing claim about the second wave is that 'communism' was a way of not seeing radicals' actual, ameliorative demands: credit institutes, subsidized timber, lower taxes (clark-revolutionary-spring-ch08-the-second-wave).

After 1848: description enters the state

The social question's ways of seeing and mapping passed into administration. Hygiene committees, statistical societies, Hinckeldey's tables in Berlin and a Strasbourg commission report that 'could have been drawn from the pages of' Blanc, Guépin, Buret or Engels all show it. Clark offers three readings, co-option and immunization, victory of an idea, or both, and declines to choose (clark-revolutionary-spring-ch09-material-progress-city).

World Machines resonances (hedged)

The notes repeatedly read this as legibility-as-machine-core: the poor and the city become readable, countable and governable. Clark, however, treats it as a historical moment in the birth of social science and realism, and stresses that the descriptions were moral and literary performances too (clark-revolutionary-spring-ch01-politics-of-description). Two twists complicate the machine reading. Some visibility was claimed from below (Tristan, the weavers), the opposite of state-imposed legibility. And the post-1848 statisticians were often reformers rather than agents of top-down imposition. The threat-labels ('riff-raff', 'communists') resemble the category work described in the-mass-as-fiction, though Clark does not use Carey's frame.
