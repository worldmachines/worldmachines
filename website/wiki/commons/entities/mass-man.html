The Mass-Man

Commons · Entities · updated 24 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Ortega y Gasset's central concept: a psychological type, found in every class, defined by inert self-satisfaction, entitlement without obligation, and ingratitude toward a civilization he mistakes for nature — contested in this wiki as an instance of the mass as rhetorical fiction.

Source: José Ortega y Gasset, The Revolt of the Masses (1930) — see ortega-revolt-of-the-masses, jose-ortega-y-gasset

Definition

The mass-man is “someone who sets no value on himself … based on specific grounds, but feels himself ‘just like everybody,’ and yet is not concerned about it” — indeed is “quite happy to feel himself identical to everyone else.” His opposite, the select man, is not one who thinks himself superior but “the man who demands more of himself than the rest, even though he may not fulfill in his person those higher exigencies.” Both types occur in every class, working class and nobility alike (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch01-the-reality-of-the-masses).

Ortega sharpens the definition twice. “Mass” applies “not so much because of his multitude as because of his inertia” (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch07-noble-life-and-vulgar-life). And at the level of a single thought: the mass-man “is satisfied with thinking the first thing he finds in his head,” while the excellent man “condemns what he finds in his mind without previous effort” (n. 16, ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-endnotes).

Traits

  • Free-expanding desire and radical ingratitude — the two “fundamental traits” of the “spoilt child,” who never meets a world that teaches “here begins another more powerful than I am” (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch06-the-dissection-of-the-mass-man-begins).
  • Self-satisfaction, not vanity. The vain man needs others' validation; the mass-man “regards himself as perfect,” hermetically closed to any external standard (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch08-why-the-masses-intervene-in-everything). In crisis he “will want to listen, and will discover [he is] deaf” (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch07-noble-life-and-vulgar-life).
  • Direct action. Refusing appeal to standards, he intervenes in everything by imposition — “the Magna Carta of barbarism” (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch08-why-the-masses-intervene-in-everything); the whole chain restated as ease → closure → direct action (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch11-the-age-of-the-self-satisfied-dandy).
  • Felt sovereignty. The rights of man, once realized, harden from ideal into “unconscious assumption” (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch02-the-rising-of-the-historic-level); in politics, “the vulgar mind, knowing itself to be vulgar, has the gumption to proclaim the rights of such vulgarity” (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch01-the-reality-of-the-masses).
  • Rights without obligations, under any mask. Reactionary and revolutionary postures are interchangeable covers for the same entitlement; the cult of “youth” turns a temporary credit into a permanent exemption; the levers are the “blackmail of violence and blackmail of humor” (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch15-arriving-at-the-real-issue).

Genealogy (Ortega's account)

  • Quantitative: Sombart's figures — 180 to 460 million Europeans between 1800 and 1914 — flooded history faster than traditional culture could form the newcomers, who were “instructed” in technique but not “educated” in its spirit (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch05-a-statistical-datum).
  • Qualitative: technicism produced, in the specialist scientist, “the prototype of the mass-man” — the “learned ignoramus” (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch12-the-barbarism-of-specialization).
  • Causal principle: “we are what our world invites us to be”; a world of safe, limitless possibility teaches that “nobody is superior to anybody” (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch07-noble-life-and-vulgar-life).

Figures of the Type

  • The primitive / Naturmensch — using civilization “as if it were a natural force,” a primitive who has “slipped through the wings” onto civilization's stage; Rathenau's “vertical invasion of the barbarians” (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch09-primitivism-and-the-technical, ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch05-a-statistical-datum). He believes civilization “as spontaneous and self-producing as Nature, and ipso facto he is changed into primitive man” (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch10-primitivism-and-history).
  • The heir-man — one species of a genus with the degenerate hereditary aristocrat and the spoiled child: “the heir who behaves exclusively as a mere heir” (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch11-the-age-of-the-self-satisfied-dandy).
  • The State-craving citizen — “L'Etat, c'est moi,” a false identity based on shared anonymity, “because neither of them is named John” (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch13-the-greatest-danger-the-state).
  • Mass-peoples — at national scale, the small nations that go wild once Europe, the schoolmaster, leaves the room (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch14a-who-commands-in-the-world-1-3).
  • The parasite — “a mere negation … an effective parasitism,” living off a civilization it did not build and does not maintain (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch15-arriving-at-the-real-issue).

Ambivalence

Ortega does not close the verdict: the mass-man is “pure potency for the greatest good and for the greatest evil as well” (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch05-a-statistical-datum). Guerrero's introduction glosses the concept as a homogenizing, “flattening force” set against an “aristocracy of the mind” he insists is meritocratic rather than inherited — Guerrero's framing, not Ortega's words (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-essays-introduction).

Contested

This wiki also holds the opposite reading: that “the mass” is an invented rhetorical category whose function is to withhold personhood — see the-mass-as-fiction and carey-intellectuals-and-masses. Ortega's psychological definition is his answer to exactly that charge, yet his own text also grounds the type in a headcount (Sombart's statistic engendering the mass-man “quantitatively,” ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch12-the-barbarism-of-specialization). Both readings stand; the tension is not resolved here.

In World Machines Terms

The notes read the mass-man as an inversion of Conatus: contentment in sameness where conatus is a striving to persist and distinguish oneself (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch01-the-reality-of-the-masses); Ortega's own claim that “all life is the struggle, the effort to be itself” and that resistance is what makes a self felt (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch11-the-age-of-the-self-satisfied-dandy); and a subject who craves capture by the State rather than resisting it, inverting the usual valence of capture-resistance (ortega-revolt-of-the-masses-ch13-the-greatest-danger-the-state). The hedge travels with it: Ortega's frame is moral-psychological and existentialist-vitalist, not a theory of capture regimes suppressing striving by statistical averaging.
