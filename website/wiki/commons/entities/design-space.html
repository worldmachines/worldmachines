Design Space

Commons · Entities · updated 6 August 2026 · source on GitHub
Dennett's unified space of all possible designs, with the Borges-derived Library of Mendel as its combinatorial substrate — replacing all-or-nothing biological possibility with accessibility, sorting features into forced moves versus QWERTY-style virtual necessities, and ending as a theory of value.

Source: Daniel C. Dennett, Darwin's Dangerous Idea (1995) — see dennett-darwins-dangerous-idea.

The idea

Design Space is Dennett's name for the space of all possible designs, with a vertical axis measuring how much design a thing embodies. It is introduced alongside the Cosmic Pyramid's inversion in dennett-darwins-dangerous-idea-ch03-universal-acid and becomes the book's principal formal device: a place where biological and human-made design can be located on one map rather than related by analogy.

Dennett is careful to separate two vertical axes that are easy to conflate (dennett-darwins-dangerous-idea-ch04-the-tree-of-life): the Tree of Life's vertical axis is time (later = higher), while Design Space's is designedness (more designed = higher). Whether more-designed forms could precede less-designed ones is left open rather than assumed away.

The Library of Mendel

The combinatorial substrate comes from Borges. All the 500-page books printable from a 100-character alphabet form a Library that is finite but so large that ordinary hyperbole fails, so Dennett mints two technical terms — Vast and Vanishingly — for quantities that dwarf any actual physical quantity while remaining strictly finite. Restrict the alphabet to A/C/G/T and the length to genome length and you get the Library of Mendel: the space of all possible DNA sequences, of which only a vanishing fraction correspond to viable organisms. This is Dennett's naturalised gloss on Dawkins's claim that there are far more ways of being dead than alive (dennett-darwins-dangerous-idea-ch05-the-possible-and-the-actual).

The device recurs. dennett-darwins-dangerous-idea-ch15-emperors-new-mind extends it to the Library of Toshiba, the Vast but finite space of all megabyte-length bit strings runnable on Dennett's old laptop, precisely in order to pin down how Vanishingly small a subset of it Gödel's Theorem actually constrains: consistent arithmetic-truth-provers, and nothing else.

Worth naming as a contrast rather than an equivalence: the universal-library concept in this wiki tracks an aspirational project — gathering all recorded knowledge, generation after generation, with the recurring tension between inclusivity and quality. Dennett's libraries are the opposite kind of object: not collections anyone builds but exhaustive combinatorial spaces that exist by definition, whose interest lies entirely in how little of them is reachable. Both share the observation that the overwhelming majority of the space is noise; only Dennett's makes that a mathematical certainty rather than a curatorial problem. See also universal-library-river-of-noise.

Drift versus lifting

Random genetic drift accumulates only distance in Design Space, not design — most mutations are neutral typos and most non-neutral ones are damaging, so unselected drift is "inexorably downward." Natural selection is what converts sideways wandering into upward lifting. Dennett's analogy: random typographical edits to Moby Dick are "idle . . . R-and-D work," almost always neutral or harmful, never improving the text (dennett-darwins-dangerous-idea-ch06-threads-of-actuality-in-design-space).

Possibility as accessibility

Design Space's main analytic payoff is to replace an all-or-nothing metaphysical question with a measurable one. Dennett nests four grades of possibility — logical, physical, biological, historical — then proposes that x is biologically possible iff x instantiates a genome accessible from some stipulated starting point in the Library of Mendel, given some means of travel (natural selection, breeding, genetic engineering). Possibility becomes a matter of degree and distance, commensurable with probability, and the burden of proof shifts onto anyone claiming a true "law of biology."

Two related constraints keep this from being a free-for-all. Genotype-to-phenotype mapping is non-linear, so intermediate phenotypes that are easy to describe — thirty-centimetre tusks between working ten- and forty-centimetre designs — may simply be inaccessible. And a genome is a recipe, not a blueprint: it presupposes a co-evolved reader-constructor it does not itself specify, which is why "complete" dinosaur DNA without a dinosaur-DNA-reader yields no dinosaur.

Forced moves, virtual necessities, and QWERTY

Dennett's sharpest tool within the space is a two-grade account of why a design feature is where it is (dennett-darwins-dangerous-idea-ch06-threads-of-actuality-in-design-space). Some features are forced moves — solutions so obviously the only viable option (autonomous metabolism, having a boundary) that any lineage anywhere would be driven to them, the way a chess player in a lost position is forced into the one saving move. But reasons for necessity come in two grades: deep reasons (physics, logic, thermodynamics) and shallow ones, which are merely historical — an ancient accident closes off alternatives until only one path remains available, producing a virtual necessity rather than a true one.

The name for the second kind is the QWERTY phenomenon, borrowed from Seymour Papert: a keyboard layout adopted to solve a since-irrelevant key-jamming problem, then locked in by accumulated vested interest, with no rational explanation left, only a historical one (dennett-darwins-dangerous-idea-ch05-the-possible-and-the-actual). Dennett's provocation is that apparent universal laws of biology — no horned birds, Dollo's Law — may be nothing more than this. The pattern reaches all the way down: dennett-darwins-dangerous-idea-ch07-back-beyond-darwins-frontier calls purely syntactic selection among equiprobable self-replicating fragments, where whichever wins early rounds for no reason but chance occupies more slots later, "the birth of the ultimate QWERTY phenomenon."

The extraterrestrial thought experiment is how Dennett proposes to tell the grades apart: we would not be surprised if aliens had metabolisms, locomotion, eyes, or even our arithmetic, but we would be floored if they used arabic numerals — because numerals, unlike numbers, are arbitrary symbols with a Vast space of equally good alternatives, so similarity there could only mean shared history.

Homology, and the Central Salvo

Which yields the chapter's evidentiary backbone, echoing Gould: imperfections, not perfections, are the best evidence of descent with modification. Perfect forced-move solutions get reinvented independently all the time; idiosyncratic near-perfection is "a dead giveaway of copying." Dennett draws the parallel out at length to nineteenth-century philology — scholars reconstructing the genealogy of Plato's manuscripts, or Otto Neugebauer deducing from an idiosyncratic numerical pattern alone that a Greek papyrus fragment was a translation of a Babylonian lunar ephemeris — and notes that Darwin himself cited the same homology/analogy distinction at work in the genealogy of languages.

The Central Salvo follows: "there is only one Design Space, and everything actual in it is united with everything else." Human artifacts — books, engineering, mathematics, this book's own bibliography — are extended phenotype, produced by the same cranes-on-cranes process that produced prokaryotes. This is the claim that licenses Part III's treatment of language, art, religion and science.

Calibrating what is actually reachable

Dennett insists on error in both directions (dennett-darwins-dangerous-idea-ch09-playing-with-constraints). Actualism — only the actual was ever possible — would make Darwinism vacuous if it were the rule. But assuming the Vast "Identikit Space" of every imaginable variant is equally reachable through the Library of Mendel overestimates real possibility just as badly. Adaptationists must keep testing at every level rather than settling the question by fiat in either direction. Gould's "hidden constraints" — inherited developmental pathways like "railroad tracks lying across the landscape" — are granted as real and then converted into a research problem, via a chess analogy: detect a secret handicap by acting as if all legal moves are available and watching for systematic departures from the best available move.

Design Space as a theory of value

The book's last chapter turns the device on value itself (dennett-darwins-dangerous-idea-ch18-the-future-of-an-idea). A run of intuition pumps — condor versus cow, redwood versus algae, a popsicle-stick model versus the popsicle sticks, an original Leonardo versus a perfect copy — argues that our sense of what is precious tracks the amount of irreplaceable design work a thing embodies. Dennett is explicit that this does not resolve the incommensurabilities; it explains why we feel them as incommensurable rather than as a utilitarian sum. It is what licenses his discriminating conservationism toward biological and cultural diversity alike, including the treatment of a faith as a population of memes that "must evolve or go extinct when the environment changes."

A related distinction, following Nicholas Humphrey: forced-move discoveries (Newton's Principia) are replaceable, since someone else would have reached the same destination, while idiosyncratic creations (Shakespeare's plays) are not, because the value lies in the particular trajectory rather than the destination.

World Machines resonances, with their hedges

  • history-machine, for QWERTY. Lock-in assembles from millions of individual typists' habits rather than a deciding authority, and becomes unquestioned infrastructure. The decay-sequence is the near-miss worth naming rather than the fit: QWERTY never decays to Ornamental or Invisible, because the constraint stays functionally binding after its reason is gone — which is precisely Dennett's point.
  • Configurancy, faintly. Recasting possibility as relative accessibility from a stipulated starting point shares Rao's refusal of teleology, but Configurancy is a relational co-emergence of agents and worlds, where Dennett's design-space accessibility is blind combinatorics with no agent at either end. The rhyme is in the refusal, not in any implied agency.
  • pluripotency-and-exaptation, for the homology material read the other way: a machine's powers exceed what it was designed for, which is the synchronic version of the repurposing Dennett tracks diachronically through sadirons-become-bookends, mainframes-become-boat-anchors, and maple leaves whose colour becomes a real adaptation once human tourism creates a selection pressure for it (dennett-darwins-dangerous-idea-ch09-adaptationist-thinking-and-leibniz).
  • Prime Radiant, for the ladder of interpretive levels — pixels to gliders to a Turing-machine tape to chess moves — though Dennett's ladder is about prediction cost, not a living, editable historical model.
