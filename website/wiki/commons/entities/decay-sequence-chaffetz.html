The Horse's Performative Afterlife: Decay-Sequence Candidates (Chaffetz)

Commons · Entities · updated 23 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Candidate instances of Florian Lohse's decay sequence drawn from David Chaffetz's Raiders, Rulers, and Traders (the Tang horse cult, the riderless funeral horse, the 1911 Delhi durbar, buzkashi), plus a Qing counter-case showing the sequence is indexed to a context, not an artifact.

Candidate instances: the horse's long performative afterlife (Chaffetz)

Surfaced by the chaffetz-raiders-rulers-traders ingest. As with the Adams cases on decay-sequence, these are candidates: Chaffetz never states the sequence, and each is a reader's reading. What they add that the existing set does not is a domain where the Functional stage is unusually easy to date and unusually well evidenced — a cavalry either wins battles or does not — which makes the slide into the later stages checkable rather than merely plausible.

  • The Tang horse cult (chaffetz-raiders-rulers-traders-ch06-equine-mania) is the Functional → Performative step with the transition narrated in the same administrative register on both sides. The Functional stage is seven hundred thousand horses, fifty-eight stud farms, ninety thousand remounts a year. The Performative stage arrives when later emperors preferred admiring horses (or paintings of horses) to riding and hunting as Taizong had, and it peaks in Xuanzong's corps of dancing horses. Chaffetz's own verdict is unusually blunt — the aestheticizing tendency "was not necessarily a healthy one," and "the dancing horses of Xuanzong could not save his reign." The image that closes it belongs with the sequence: after the An Lushan rebellion the dancing horses were dispersed into ordinary military units, where they remained identifiable because they would break into dance at the sound of music. Ornament surviving inside a functional unit, still legible as ornament.
  • The riderless horse with the saddle reversed (same note) is a candidate Ornamental-stage instance with an exceptionally long tail: a steppe funerary practice at khans' graves, absorbed centuries later into Western state funerals, where nobody present is mourning a horseman.
  • The 1911 Delhi durbar (chaffetz-raiders-rulers-traders-ch12-last-horse-powers) is the sharpest single instance, because the infrastructure is still fully built while the function is already void — which is exactly what decay-sequence means by Performative rather than Ornamental. Ten thousand princely horsemen parade in chain armor and family colors; the Taj Mahal is restored; the capital moves to Delhi; George V enters the Red Fort on a coal-black Waler. The performance is of a martial order — mass cavalry as the arbiter of Asian empire — that Balfour had already privately written off, staged for maharajas who had once fought Britain and now saluted it. The Indian public's question, recorded in the note, is the diagnostic in plain language: why does "the most expensive army in the world for the poorest people in the world" exist?
  • Buzkashi as national sport (chaffetz-raiders-rulers-traders-epilogue) runs the sequence on a practice rather than an institution. An unbounded steppe brawl over a carcass — no rules, no field, no teams, no prizes — is converted by Zaher Shah into a hundred-yard field, forty-five-minute halves, uniforms, referees and a scoring circle called "the circle of truth," so that it can serve as a unifying symbol for a fractious state. The riders bolting past the barriers are the friction of the conversion showing.

The case that cuts the other way, and is the more useful one. decay-sequence already raises the objection that the ornament diagnostic "may over-read ornament as death." Chaffetz supplies a hard test of it. In 1860 the Qing bannermen — thirty thousand strong, arrayed in the old encirclement tactics, in ceremonial order — take heavy losses against European rifle volleys at Tianjin on marshy ground that denies cavalry room to manoeuvre, and the emperor concedes trade access. Read as ornament-as-evidence, that is a dead framework performing itself. But the same cavalry, deployed inland against Yakub Beg's Xinjiang revolt, crushed the rebellion with undiminished effectiveness (chaffetz-raiders-rulers-traders-ch11-the-great-game). Fully Functional in one theatre and apparently Performative in another, at the same date, with the same men and horses.

The lesson for the diagnostic is worth stating precisely: the sequence is indexed to a context, not to an artifact. What looked ornamental at Tianjin was terrain-and-vector obsolescence — the maritime attack surface, not the horse — and it stayed Functional wherever the old terrain persisted. Before ruling a structure dead on ornamental evidence, check whether it has simply been moved somewhere it does not work. That is a sharper version of the objection than "decoration and function are not mutually exclusive," and it is testable: find the theatre where the thing still works, and see whether it is ornamental there too.
