‘Asabiyya (Irwin's reading of Ibn Khaldun)

Commons · Entities · updated 28 September 2026 · source on GitHub
‘Asabiyya as Irwin presents Ibn Khaldun's usage: a dynamic, divinely used group feeling that drives toward royal authority and decays in a three-to-four-generation dynastic cycle, which can be engineered (the mamluks) and need not rest on kinship, but whose scope Ibn Khaldun limited, and whose meaning drifted with every translator and reader.

‘Asabiyya, usually rendered “group feeling”, is the central term of ibn-khaldun's Muqaddima. This page follows how robert-irwin presents it in irwin-ibn-khaldun, which differs from the compressed “cohesion rises, peaks, declines” gloss common in this wiki (secular-cycles, ibn-khaldun).

The word and what it does

The word appears over five hundred times. Irwin gives two etymologies (the binding of persons, or a turban or headband) and prefers the first, hedging with “may”. In his reading ‘asabiyya has “a life of its own” and drives toward power: “royal authority is the goal of group feeling”. It is also “one of God's tools”. Religion reinforces it, through prophecy or sainthood, but the Merinids, Mongols and Chagatai Turks show that religion was not always needed. Irwin adds that Ibn Khaldun was an ethnographer only in a restricted sense, because ‘asabiyya was the only thing that interested him (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch03-nomads-asabiyya). Irwin insists that Ibn Khaldun's sense of the term “always had a positive and dynamic quality” (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch11-ending-up).

The cycle it drives

Conquerors from the desert or mountains lose their ‘asabiyya within roughly three or four generations. They succumb to luxury, tax extortionately to pay other soldiers, come to be seen as corrupt and impious, and fall to the next puritanical wave (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch03-nomads-asabiyya). The fiscal side runs from lawful taxes under a new dynasty with desert virtues to arbitrary levies under a senile one. Irwin's point is that political and moral decline come before the tax spiral, not after it (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch08-economics). The fall has two sufficient causes, social development and God's just verdict on sin and greed, which Irwin likens to overdetermination (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch01-masudi-iram-weird-science). Genealogy serves ‘asabiyya as a socially useful fiction (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch03-bedouin-berbers-genealogy). Even the Mahdi “would need the support of ‘asabiyya” (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch07-future-last-days). A caliphal title without enough group feeling behind it, as with the Quraysh, is an empty title (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch05-office-holding-timur).

Irwin's source for the moral engine is an inherited trope rather than a new sociology: “It was, I think, this kind of moralizing, more than a newly invented sociology, which inspired Ibn Khaldun's engine of history” (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch03-bedouin-berbers-genealogy). Against Averroes's city-bound political philosophy, ‘asabiyya is what separates Ibn Khaldun, since prestige needs a group that makes one feared (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch04-philosophy-cyclical-history).

Engineered, and not only kin-based

Ibn Khaldun admired the mamluk system because it let a regime “artificially” reinforce its ‘asabiyya by importing warriors from remote tribal regions, whose nomadic virtues were preserved by slavery. Crone judged that the passage “brilliantly describes the mamluks as institutionalized tribal conquerors” (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch05-arrival-in-egypt). Irwin uses this against Gellner, who assumed ‘asabiyya arises only from kinship. Ibn Khaldun allowed proximity and barracks life to generate it (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch10-colonialist-mahdi-gellner). Irwin also says that jah (prestige) was nearly as economically consequential as ‘asabiyya (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch08-economics).

Where the model fits, and where it doesn't

  • It “seems to work quite well” for the early Arab conquests, the Almoravids and the Almohads, but not for the long-lived Merinids, Hafsids and ‘Abd al-Wadids. Ibn Khaldun himself exempted the eastern Islamic lands, Andalusia and the Mamluks, and Irwin concludes that its importance “has been overstressed in some modern readings” (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch03-nomads-asabiyya).
  • The Maghribi “nomads” were transhumants tied to towns by trade, taxation and service, and the concrete record of tribal levies deserting their sultans is political practice rather than theory (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch03-nomads-asabiyya, irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch02-topography-early-career).
  • Egypt described as tribe-free is contradicted by the 1389 civil war (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch05-arrival-in-egypt).
  • Irwin argues against Dale that the cycle must have been induced from the Caliphate, Almoravids and Almohads (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch10-arab-american-novels-orientalism). His last chapter lists Ibn Khaldun's inconsistent use of the term among the reasons the Muqaddima is not a closed system (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch11-ending-up).
  • Crone's comparison sharpens its viewpoint. Confucian historians put tribal conquest at the bottom of the cycle, while for Ibn Khaldun “tribal conquest marks the high point” because he saw the cycles “from the barbarians' point of view” (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch10-colonialist-mahdi-gellner, irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch04-philosophy-cyclical-history).

A concept that drifts under translation

Irwin's reception chapters show ‘asabiyya re-rendered by each readership:

  • “Esprit de corps” in De Slane's translation, which later French commentators gave “Bergsonian overtones of élan vital” (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch10-afterlife-french-germans).
  • “Civic spirit” or “national idea” for Von Kremer (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch10-afterlife-french-germans).
  • “The basic protoplasm” of all bodies politic for Toynbee, who denied it was a nomadic monopoly and argued that nomads are a symptom of decay, not its cause (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch10-toynbee-rosenthal).
  • A kind of patriotism (hubb al-watan) for al-Tahtawi. Rashid Rida found ‘asabiyya over religion un-Islamic (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch10-colonialist-mahdi-gellner).
  • Sheep, sheepdogs and wolves in Gellner's circulation of elites. Irwin's counterexample is the Dawawida, who were sheepdogs when paid and wolves when not, and never became sheep (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch10-colonialist-mahdi-gellner).
  • “Clannism” in the Arab Human Development Report 2004, a pejorative label for urban stagnation that inverts Ibn Khaldun's positive usage (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch11-ending-up).

Rosenthal's uniform rendering lacks flexibility, but Irwin defends it because it keeps the Arabic recoverable (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch10-toynbee-rosenthal).

World Machines use, with the hedges

Group cohesion as the engine of state formation and decay is the obvious hook for WMP, near chaffetz-raiders-rulers-traders on raiders as a political type and civilization-undone-by-its-own-success on luxury undoing the order that produced it. Three qualifications travel with it. The cycle is generational (three or four generations) and tribal-military, not a thousand-year infrastructure lifecycle of the kind in world-machines. Its engine is moral and providential in Ibn Khaldun's own framing. And Irwin treats it as a regional model with acknowledged exceptions, better handled as a set of models than a closed system (irwin-ibn-khaldun-ch11-ending-up). The Turchin lineage asserted on secular-cycles is not discussed in Irwin's book.
