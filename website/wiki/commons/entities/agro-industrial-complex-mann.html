The Agro-Industrial Complex (Mann)

Commons · Entities · updated 28 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Mann's agro-industrial complex: improved crops, high-intensity fertilizers and factory-made pesticides, rooted in the clonal potato, Peruvian guano and arsenic against the Colorado potato beetle, which lifted Europe out of famine and set the template of modern monoculture while breeding the blight, the pesticide treadmill and clonal rubber's vulnerability.

Mann's name in mann-1493 for the system of 'improved crops, high-intensity fertilizers, and factory-made pesticides' whose template the potato's adoption set, and which converged in the Green Revolution of the 1950s and 1960s (mann-1493-ch06-potato-wars-sea-of-genes). All three pillars have Columbian Exchange roots: the Andean potato, Peruvian guano, and arsenic against the Colorado potato beetle.

The potato: a teaspoon from a sea of genes

Andean farmers grew a 'bubbling stew' of landraces in mixed patches (10.6 traditional varieties per family in one Peruvian valley; 3,700 in the International Potato Center collection), on terraces, raised fields and ridged fields. Europe's stock was 'created by dipping a teaspoon into the sea of genes in Peru and Bolivia' and propagated as clones, and Parmentier, by promoting mass cultivation, 'unknowingly' created monoculture (mann-1493-ch06-potato-wars-sea-of-genes). The payoff was real. Mann reports McNeill's claim that the potato 'permitted a handful of European nations to assert dominion over most of the world between 1750 and 1950', qualifies the monocausal reading with the wider agricultural revolution, and calibrates it with a 2010 estimate that the potato explains about an eighth of Europe's population rise, comparable to the steam engine (mann-1493-ch06-potato-wars-sea-of-genes).

Guano: the template

'Guano set the template for modern agriculture'. Nutrients were moved from distant places, later synthesized in distant factories, into fields; land became 'a medium'; crops were grown for an international market, hence monoculture. Mann leans on Edward Melillo for guano as an earlier Green Revolution, and reports Smil's estimate that two in five people alive depend on factory nitrogen. Adoption waited on Justus von Liebig's authority, not on the long Andean practice or Humboldt's samples. The hidden input was coerced labor: Chinese zhuzai deceived or kidnapped in Fujian, about one in eight of whom died en route (mann-1493-ch06-guano-age).

The failures it bred

  • Blight. Jean Ristaino's herbarium DNA study puts Phytophthora infestans in the Andes, and Mann judges that it 'most likely' reached Europe on a guano ship. Ireland was vulnerable because its clachans planted one variety, the Lumper, and because improvers had abolished ridge-and-furrow lazy-beds, which Michael Myers's experiments found carried about half the blight of level fields. The blight was 'one kind of potato, on a terrain shaped for technology, rather than biology', and Mann calls the Great Hunger the first truly contemporary agricultural disaster (mann-1493-ch06-modern-famine-lazy-beds).
  • The pesticide treadmill. Uniform farms plus fast transport made pest outbreaks routine. Paris Green answered the beetle, resistance appeared by 1912, DDT lasted about seven years, and by the mid-1980s each new pesticide was good for about a season. Mann reports critics' term 'toxic treadmill' (mann-1493-ch06-war-upon-the-beetles).
  • Clonal rubber. Wild Hevea trees stand far apart; plantations pack them together so South American leaf blight spreads crown to crown, and selection for latex alone 'inadvertently produced varieties with even less resistance to blight'. Southeast Asia's rubber is a genetically narrow slice of Wickham's seeds, protected so far by distance (mann-1493-ch07-wickham-end-of-world). Botanists Mann reports warn that so wide a monoculture invites an epidemic and, 'just possibly, a global economic breakdown' (mann-1493-ch07-no-birds-grease-chemistry).

Escape from Malthus, with the bill attached

Mann credits industrial monoculture, improved crops and fertilizer with doubling or tripling living standards as population rose from under one billion in 1700 to about seven billion (mann-1493-ch06-guano-age). He keeps the bill attached: famine, resistance, and a science that for forty years blamed ozone, static electricity and locomotive smoke for the blight: 'The plea for help went out to Science, but Science couldn't answer' (mann-1493-ch06-modern-famine-lazy-beds). Rubber, on Susanna Hecht's account as Mann reports it, was with steel and fossil fuels one of the three fundamental materials of the Industrial Revolution (mann-1493-ch07-no-birds-grease-chemistry).

World Machines resonances, with their hedges

  • legibility-as-machine-core and modernity-machine. Level fields of one variety replacing locally adapted ridges read as legibility-driven simplification that created fragility. The note adds that the lazy-beds were a folk practice the reformers flattened rather than measured, and that Mann narrates agronomy, not machines (mann-1493-ch06-modern-famine-lazy-beds). No one designed the clonal potato for legibility (mann-1493-ch06-potato-wars-sea-of-genes).
  • epistemic-monoculture. The notes connect crop monoculture to this page by analogy only; the vulnerability here is genetic (mann-1493-ch06-modern-famine-lazy-beds, mann-1493-ch06-war-upon-the-beetles).
  • agricultural-revolution. Mann places the potato inside, not instead of, the transport, enclosure, clover and manure changes of that revolution (mann-1493-ch06-potato-wars-sea-of-genes).
  • Self-eroding optimization. Each step of the treadmill is locally rational and the aggregate is a self-reinforcing loop, which fits WMP interest in machines whose own optimization generates the pressure that erodes them, though Mann does not say this (mann-1493-ch06-war-upon-the-beetles).
