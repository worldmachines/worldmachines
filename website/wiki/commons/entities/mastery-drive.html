The Mastery Drive

Commons · Entities · updated 26 September 2026 · source on GitHub
The drive toward mastery (Freud's abandoned Bemächtigungstrieb) as Peter Gay rehabilitates it in The Cultivation of Hatred: aggression's constructive face, converting passivity into activity, and the psychic fuel Gay finds behind the Victorian empire of fact, the professions, social science, and the self-controlled bourgeois character.

The drive toward mastery (Bemächtigungstrieb) is Freud's early, later-abandoned hypothesis of an urge independent of sexuality. It aims not at harming objects but at converting passivity into activity, as when a child who has suffered at the dentist plays dentist. peter-gay makes it the positive counterweight of The Cultivation of Hatred (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3). Drawing on Ives Hendrick's “instinct to master,” his appendix argues that aggressive energy, redirected, is “the fuel for essential civilized and civilizing work.” Tool-invention, communications, trade techniques, and distributive institutions are all a kind of “attack” on the world, aimed at reducing pain “on the solid ground of reality rather than in the cloud land of fantasy” (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-appendix-theories-of-aggression). The introduction already frames bourgeois aggression as the drive “to get a grip on time, space, scarcity — and themselves as never before” (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-front-introduction).

Lineage of the idea

The idea has three roots. Freud's Bemächtigungstrieb and Veblen's “instinct of workmanship” both name the “gratified sense of closure” in solving a problem or acquiring a skill (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p6-moral-equivalents-safety-valves). George Moore's 1848 Man and His Motives found “the love of power” in the infant's first motor pleasures. Gay reads this as containing, “in embryo, Nietzsche's will to power and Freud's early hypothesis of a drive for mastery” (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p6-age-of-advice-self-control-thrift). Gay stresses that mastery is “ethically neutral.” It gratifies the sadist and the martinet as readily as the reformer, which is why sport, science, and empire can be treated as continuous (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p6-moral-equivalents-safety-valves).

Where Gay finds it at work

  • The empire of fact. Industrialization and the revolution in knowledge were “the most concerted series of aggressions ever launched against ignorance.” The Victorians canonized Bacon's “enlarging of the bounds of Human Empire.” Huxley demanded that one “sit down before fact as a little child.” Quetelet's social physics claimed statistical law “enlarges” liberty. Semmelweis's patient elimination of hypotheses about childbed fever is “constructive aggression at its best.” Gay also records the empire's chronic shortage of real facts (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p6-empire-of-fact-bacon-science).
  • Social science as antidote to helplessness. Booth's seventeen-volume survey of London poverty answered the “sense of helplessness” before mass poverty. Gay's gloss: “the quest for mastery is the antidote for impotence.” Brentano's discovery of German academic Streit shows the same combativeness relocated into scholarship rather than abolished (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p6-brentano-booth-social-science).
  • Specialization and the professions. Specialization and professionalization were “an impressive feat of harnessing aggression to constructive purposes.” The price was the Renaissance generalist, a “fateful division of power” between scholarship and citizenship, and benefits that tracked class lines (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p6-end-of-renaissance-man).
  • Self-mastery. The advice literature converged on self-control (Selbstbeherrschung, empire sur soi-même). Its hardest test was “the management of rage,” and it treated thrift as self-command in another guise. It never preached passivity, since idleness was denounced as viciously as immorality (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p6-age-of-advice-self-control-thrift). “Character” was “aggression disciplined and sublimated.” Its pathology, obsessional neurosis, parodied the virtues it prized (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p6-character-obsessional-neurosis-freud).
  • Counterphobic mastery. Theodore Roosevelt converted remembered asthmatic helplessness into the strenuous life (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p1-tr-extremist-of-the-center).
  • Women's mastery. Redefined competence was “the prerequisite for women's exercise of constructive aggression, a giant first step toward their exercise of mastery in the world” (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p4-competence-womanliness-redefined). Authorship gave women writers “aggression in, and through, self-sacrifice” (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p4-scribbling-women-motives-reputation).
  • Moral equivalents. William James's 1906 proposal to conscript youth into an army “enlisted against Nature” treated combativeness as a fixed drive to redirect, not suppress. Unusually for his time, James included his own class among those needing the cure (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p6-moral-equivalents-safety-valves).

Gay against Freud's pessimism

Gay is skeptical of Freud's 1920 Eros/Thanatos dualism, which he calls “a triumph of theoretical clarification” reached at “a risky distance from the clinical terrain.” He prefers Leo Stone's pluralist view of aggression as “the aggregate of diverse acts” (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-appendix-theories-of-aggression). He also faults Freud's cultural criticism for treating clinical patients as typical. The sublimated aggression that produced neurotics also collaborated with Eros to build cities, speed travel, and lengthen life. That achievement is what the Epilogue shows collapsing in 1914 (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p6-character-obsessional-neurosis-freud).

World Machines resonances (hedged)

  • Conatus. The mastery drive rhymes with Conatus as a basic drive to exercise and extend one's power. The notes narrow it each time. Gay's drive concerns effectance and control and is compatible with cruelty (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p6-moral-equivalents-safety-valves). The Victorian regime captured striving by redirecting and sublimating it, the opposite of the statistical averaging WMP usually describes (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p6-age-of-advice-self-control-thrift). And sublimation is not simply lossy, since it converts striving into cities and communications (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p6-character-obsessional-neurosis-freud).
  • Fuel for infrastructure. The appendix's account of tools and institutions as sublimated attack resonates with world-machines as interlocking institutions and technologies that channel a society's energies at scale. The resonance concerns what fuels infrastructure-building, not how it is administered (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-appendix-theories-of-aggression). See legibility-as-cultivated-aggression.
