The Jena Set (Wulf)

Commons · Entities · updated 28 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Andrea Wulf's 'Jena Set' as a scene: extends The Jena Circle with her account of the institutional accident that freed Jena, the print, salon, gossip and proximity infrastructure that held the group together, its hubs, and the material vicious circle through which it dispersed.

Extends The Jena Circle with Andrea Wulf's account in wulf-magnificent-rebels. 'Jena Set' is her coinage, justified by coincidence: they 'all came together at the same time in the same place' (wulf-magnificent-rebels-prologue-1). Her roll call puts Goethe at the centre as 'stern and benevolent godfather', with Novalis, Fichte, the Schlegel brothers, Caroline, Dorothea Veit, Schelling and Schiller around him, and Hegel and the Humboldt brothers on the periphery (wulf-magnificent-rebels-prologue-1). The reading notes treat her account as a picture of a scene or network, while stressing that she offers narrative, not a model.

Conditions: freedom by institutional accident

Wulf's explanation is structural. The German patchwork of over fifteen hundred states made censorship hard to enforce, and Jena's university was governed by four Saxon dukes who had to agree on everything, so 'no one was truly in charge' and professors taught what was banned elsewhere (wulf-magnificent-rebels-prologue-2, wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch03-finest-minds-2). She contrasts clergy-run Oxford and Cambridge and France's state-run schools (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch03-finest-minds-2). One note reads this as a small-scale history-machine-style story of a distributed, unintended arrangement producing a scene, while noting that Wulf herself gives a local explanation (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch03-finest-minds-2).

Infrastructure

  • Journals. Horen, which Wulf says made the Jena Set a group for the first time, reached far beyond its subscribers through reading societies, libraries and salons (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch03-finest-minds-1). The Allgemeine Literatur-Zeitung and the Musen-Almanach supplied money and audience (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch04-intellectual-friction-2). The Athenaeum sold a few hundred copies per issue but circulated widely (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch09-sublime-impertinence-2).
  • Proximity. A walking tour of Jena took under half an hour, with Fichte, Schiller, Goethe, the Humboldts and the journal editors minutes apart (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch06-splendid-circle-2); the Humboldts could wave to Schiller from window to window (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch03-finest-minds-2); the Leutragasse house held the group under one roof (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch14-schlegel-clique).
  • Letters and salons. Letters shuttled several times a week between Jena and Weimar (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch01-happy-event-2); Berlin's Jewish salons, hosted by women, were the only places in Prussia where religious, class and gender boundaries fell away (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch09-sublime-impertinence-1).
  • Gossip. The notes repeatedly treat gossip as the network's medium, through which members reinterpret one another (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch06-splendid-circle-2, wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch16-republic-of-despots-1).
  • Breadth. Philology, verse translation, Fichte's Wissenschaftslehre, animal electricity and colour theory circulated in the same rooms, the 'little academy' (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch07-little-academy-2); Wulf calls Jena's combined knowledge a 'great living encyclopaedia' (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch11-naturphilosophie).
  • One unwritten rule. In the Schiller-Goethe-Humboldt circle, everything could be discussed except politics (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch03-finest-minds-2).

Hubs

Goethe is the neutral hub, shuttling between factions and keeping each side's secrets (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch07-little-academy-2, wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch14-schlegel-clique). Caroline is the social centre and 'conductor' (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch14-schlegel-clique, wulf-magnificent-rebels-epilogue-after-jena-1). Wulf calls Schiller the 'unsung hero' who, through Horen and invitations, 'made possible' the 'Alliance of Minds'; his own line, that posterity would turn them 'from contemporaries into neighbours', cuts against a tidy group identity (wulf-magnificent-rebels-epilogue-after-jena-2).

Hardening and fragmentation

Under attack, the friends grew more combative rather than more careful ('We don't make enemies, we already have them') (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch15-new-confederation). Then Wulf writes: 'Gone were the days of symphilosophising... they fought among themselves', with causes that include the Schiller quarrel, Fichte's dismissal, the Lucinde scandal, a journal war, an exceptionally cold winter, money and Tieck's gout (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch16-republic-of-despots-1). Dorothea's 'republic of despots' (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch16-republic-of-despots-2) and Tieck's 'the unity has been split apart' after Novalis's death (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch17-black-fog-2) are the actors' verdicts. By 1801 'the literary whirlwind had almost blown itself out' (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch17-black-fog-2).

Dispersal and ruin

Wulf presents the end as a vicious circle: professors were paid directly by students, so departures cut income and prompted more departures (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch19-current-exodus). Schiller remembered being 'electrified by our intellectual friction', in nostalgia (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch19-current-exodus). Cotta's publishing list carried the ideas once the town no longer did (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch19-current-exodus). The Battle of Jena overwrote the circle's sites with the wounded, and student numbers fell from almost nine hundred in the 1790s to under two hundred in 1806; 'the scholarly exchange' was 'absolutely gone' (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch20-french-in-town-1, wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch20-french-in-town-2).

Hedges

The notes read the group's dependence on co-presence and a specific funding structure as implicit in Wulf's narrative, not argued by her (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch19-current-exodus, wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch20-french-in-town-2). The existing The Jena Circle page frames the break-up as the fragility of cognitive diversity under social stress; Wulf's account adds material causes (weather, money, illness, war, student fees) alongside the personal ones, and does not reduce the break-up to one cause (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch16-republic-of-despots-1). For the working method see symphilosophie-wulf; for a contrasting distance-based network, see the correspondence model on alexander-von-humboldt.
