The misdating of the Hermetica and Casaubon's redating (Yates)

Commons · Entities · updated 28 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Yates's account of how the Renaissance misdated the Hermetica (c. A.D. 100-300) as pre-Mosaic Egyptian wisdom, why humanist philology failed to catch it, and how Isaac Casaubon's 1614 redating ended the 'reign of Hermes' only slowly and unevenly: ignored by Fludd and Kircher, accepted with residue by the Cambridge Platonists, and used as a weapon by Mersenne.

The organising fact of yates-giordano-bruno. The Corpus Hermeticum and the Asclepius were written c. A.D. 100-300 by unknown, probably Greek authors, and contain popular Platonism and Stoicism with some Jewish and perhaps Persian influences. The Renaissance took them for the work of Hermes Trismegistus, an Egyptian priest older than Moses and the fount from which Plato drank. frances-yates calls the Renaissance return to a golden age of magic "based on a radical error in dating" (yates-giordano-bruno-ch01-hermes-trismegistus). Isaac Casaubon's 1614 demonstration that the texts are post-Christian is, for her, a watershed between the Renaissance and the modern world, "though nobody speaks of a 'pre-Casaubon' era" (yates-giordano-bruno-ch21-after-dating-1).

How the error was built and held

  • The Fathers. Lactantius embraced Hermes as a Gentile prophet of the Son of God and set him beside the Sibyls. Augustine condemned the god-making passage of the Asclepius but still granted Hermes great antiquity, placing him after Moses and before the Greeks (yates-giordano-bruno-ch01-hermes-trismegistus).
  • Ficino and the prisca theologia. In 1463 cosimo-de-medici ordered Ficino to translate Hermes before Plato, which Yates takes as the strongest evidence that what is older was thought closer to divine truth. Ficino's genealogy (Mercurius, Orpheus, Aglaophemus, Pythagoras, Philolaus, Plato) came from the Fathers, and the Pimander ran to sixteen editions by 1600 (yates-giordano-bruno-ch01-hermes-trismegistus).
  • A family of errors. The Orphic hymns and the Chaldean Oracles were misdated in the same way (yates-giordano-bruno-ch04-ficino-natural-magic-2), and Pseudo-Dionysius was taken for a contemporary of St Paul, so the error recurs at a second point of the synthesis (yates-giordano-bruno-ch06-pseudo-dionysius).
  • Piety pushes the date back. In Yates's words, "the holier and more Christian Hermes Trismegistus becomes, the more his date is pushed back." Vergerius's preface to Turnebus's 1554 edition puts him before Moses (yates-giordano-bruno-ch10-religious-hermetism). Bruno reverses the Moses-Hermes question outright, making Egypt older than both Greeks and Hebrews (yates-giordano-bruno-ch12-hermetic-reform-2).
  • Built into floors and walls. The Siena cathedral pavement shows Hermes "contemporaneous" with Moses (yates-giordano-bruno-ch02-pimander-asclepius-2).

Why the humanists did not catch it

Yates sets two Renaissance recoveries side by side. The Latin humanist knew the true date of the civilisation he wanted to return to and carried that realism into textual criticism, as Valla did on the Ad Herennium. Ficino was "unfailingly gullible" about Hellenistic texts taken as prisca theologia. The philological habit that would expose the Hermetica was present in humanism but not among the Magi, and she notes the irony that better philology might have stopped the Magi from becoming Magi (yates-giordano-bruno-ch09-against-magic). She is astonished that the sixteenth century had to pass before the ordinary content-and-style tools were applied to the Greek Hermetica (yates-giordano-bruno-ch21-after-dating-1).

Casaubon's critique

Casaubon attacked Cardinal Baronius's Annales Ecclesiastici, which repeated Lactantius on Hermes and the Sibyls. His arguments were from content (Plato and the Platonists, Genesis, John, Paul, the liturgy) and from style and anachronism (Phidias, the Pythian games, late Greek). He took the works for early-Christian forgeries made "to make the new doctrine palatable to the Gentiles". Yates notes that this premise shows how deeply the Christian reading had taken hold: Hermes is executed inside the same Lactantian frame that launched him. The Asclepius, which had no Greek text, fell with the Corpus, taking with it a major ally of magic (yates-giordano-bruno-ch21-after-dating-1, yates-giordano-bruno-ch10-religious-hermetism).

A watershed that was slow to bite

Yates hedges the effect: the dating did not act at once, other factors worked against the Renaissance traditions, and it was "one of the factors, and an important one" in releasing seventeenth-century thinkers from magic. She gives four examples of what followed:

  • Ignored. Robert Fludd lives in a world where Casaubon "might never have been born", and the Rosicrucian manifestos ignore the dating (yates-giordano-bruno-ch21-after-dating-1, yates-giordano-bruno-ch21-after-dating-2). Athanasius Kircher cannot give Hermes up, because Renaissance hieroglyph-reading needs him to be an ancient Egyptian priest (yates-giordano-bruno-ch21-after-dating-2).
  • Accepted, with residue. Henry More never mentions Hermes in his Conjectura Cabbalistica, and Ralph Cudworth laboriously sorts genuine from forged books. Both are "bereft of the Hermetic foundation" yet "reluctant to abandon the Hermetica" (yates-giordano-bruno-ch21-after-dating-3).
  • Used as a weapon. Mersenne soon has the Casaubon argument: if the Egyptian Moses is a fake, the corner-stone falls (yates-giordano-bruno-ch22-fludd-controversies-1).
  • Too early or too late. Bruno was burned fourteen years before the exposure, and Yates thinks he would never have lost faith in Hermes's antiquity (yates-giordano-bruno-ch21-after-dating-1). Campanella lived into the wane, "twenty years too late", when Mersenne found he could teach nothing and Descartes declined to meet him (yates-giordano-bruno-ch20-campanella-3).

The reign of Hermes, dated

In her conclusion Yates dates the reign exactly: it begins with Ficino's translation and ends with Casaubon. Within it the attitudes that led to modern science appeared, and the dating "came at the right moment", when the work was done (yates-giordano-bruno-ch22-fludd-controversies-2). She adds that correct dating does not invalidate the Hermetica as documents of religious experience, and that scholars still disagree about how much genuinely Egyptian teaching they contain (yates-giordano-bruno-ch21-after-dating-3).

World Machines readings, with their hedges

  • A founding text-corpus was misdated for about 150 years and was productive while misdated. Its correction came as a scholarly critique, not an event. The notes read this as a Legibility event (texts made checkable and datable) that bears on the modernity-machine, and flag that framing as theirs, not Yates's.
  • The correction was a contributing cause that acted slowly and unevenly. That is the argument-shape of legibility-as-precondition-not-cause.
  • The afterlife in secret societies and a Jesuit archaeology loosely echoes the decay-sequence. Yates describes an intellectual afterlife, not a machine.
  • For a print-history account of the same misdating, see the Eisenstein section of giordano-bruno.
