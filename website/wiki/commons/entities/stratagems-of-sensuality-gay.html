Stratagems of Sensuality (Gay)

Commons · Entities · updated 28 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Peter Gay's 'stratagems' of sensuality in The Tender Passion: the displacements, sublimations and reaction formations through which Victorian erotic energy found permissible outlets in music, food, nature, religion ('glandular Christianity'), the department store and the railroad, outlets that both policed and enriched middle-class sensual life.

"Stratagems" is peter-gay's term in The Tender Passion (gay-bourgeois-experience-v2) for the ways erotic energy under pressure from guilt and culture finds expression without a conventional mode for it. Spiritual and aesthetic attachments, he argues, often "prove to be displacements of erotic desire." The mechanisms are reaction formation (lust turned into prudishness), displacement onto poems, symphonies or landscapes, and sublimation (gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch5-1-food-love). The concept extends the Vol. I defenses catalogued in bourgeois-defenses-against-eros from managing sexual knowledge to rerouting desire itself.

Sublimation, complicated

Gay says Freud "naturalized" sublimation but did not fathom it. Sublimation is often accompanied by projection, and its "economic" picture (loss to sexuality as gain to culture) does not mean stricter abstinence yields better work; Freud himself said total celibacy makes "well-mannered weaklings." Gay distinguishes those who merely swapped sensuality for censoriousness from those who enlarged their satisfactions, and treats neuroses as the "shadow side" of the stratagems, not proof of a unique hypocrisy (gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch5-1-food-love).

The arenas

  • Music. The "only sensual pleasure without vice" was democratized through home music education, orchestras and conservatories. Wagner's cult let cultivated bourgeois "indulge their erotic dreams" while believing the experience elevated, and the audience's arousal ended "not by incest, or a love death, but by supper." Elizabet von Herzogenberg's diatribe against Bayreuth's "sultry sensuality with holy and solemn gestures" shows that some bourgeois could name what was happening (gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch5-1-food-love).
  • Food. Oral and genital gratification meet in the language of love, but eating is overdetermined, and the great bourgeois banquet was often a substitute for sex rather than an incitement to it (gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch5-2-budget-displacements).
  • Nature, gardens and water. Love of nature was the most successful stratagem of indirection, letting the bourgeois "both escape the implications of sensuality and intensify its delights." Children were schooled into it by anthropomorphic verse and birds-and-flowers sex education, genteel women by gardening, and bodies by the water cure. mabel-loomis-todd's journals show sensuality flowing across a "menu of pleasures" and persisting "in its stratagems" even through Austin Dickinson's dying (gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch5-2-budget-displacements).
  • Religion ("glandular Christianity"). Religion's centrality made it "an inexhaustible resource" for indirect, permissible erotic expression. The century generalized the saints' erotic piety to ordinary people and began to study it (gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch5-3-glandular-christianity-part-1). Coventry Patmore read "God is Love" as effectively "Love is God," and Gay judges this less a displacement than a way of enjoying this world by invoking the other (gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch5-3-glandular-christianity-part-1). Charles Kingsley's faith was "an erotic pantheism restrained by the Thirty-nine Articles" (gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch5-3-glandular-christianity-part-2; see charles-kingsley). Earlier, the Roes had justified their erotic letters in religious terms (gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch2-2-desire-holy-pure). Homosexual literary coteries of the 1890s also fused sexuality with Anglo-Catholic piety (gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch4-2-return-repressed).
  • Modernity. Religion supplied ancient objects, and the nineteenth century added new ones. In Zola's Au Bonheur des dames shopping is rendered as seduction and devouring. The railroad serves as punishing "social superego," lover's accelerator and outright erotic object, anchored by Freud's remark on the erotic charge of rhythmic motion. "Concrete, time-bound external forces leave their imprint on universal psychological mechanisms" (gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch5-4-sexualizing-modernity).
  • Art's doctrine of distance. Allegory, myth and history let the nude and staged sensuality claim elevated aims, from Wagner's stage to Geefs's The Lion in Love (gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch5-1-food-love, gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-epilogue-2-lion-love-last-thoughts).

Two-way traffic

Displacement in Gay's account does not only hide desire. Nature is both a defense against erotic pleasure and a source of more of it (gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch5-2-budget-displacements). Religious displacement is "a dubious blessing": it tightened repression for some but licensed forbidden longings by lending them religious dignity (gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch5-3-glandular-christianity-part-1). Kingsley and Patmore tried to "spiritualize the erotic" and "only succeeded in eroticizing spirituality" (gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch5-3-glandular-christianity-part-2). Railroad, music, nature and God together "expand—and at the same time ... police" middle-class sensual love, enriching it and containing its tensions without resolving them (gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch5-4-sexualizing-modernity).

World Machines resonances (hedged)

The notes read the stratagems as a class-wide emotional infrastructure: poetry, manuals, periodicals, sex education, theology, concert halls and churches jointly route desire through acceptable channels, often as institutions that are control apparatus and licensed outlet at once (gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch5-2-budget-displacements, gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch5-3-glandular-christianity-part-1; compare infrastructure-of-bourgeois-inwardness). The shift from disguising the railway in inherited genres to a counter-phobic mastery (Turner to Monet's Gare Saint-Lazare) is a small, dated case of a culture absorbing new infrastructure, which rhymes with history-machine. Gay tells it in psychological, not structural, terms (gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch5-4-sexualizing-modernity). Any rhyme with modernity-machine legibility in Zola's volume-selling store is the note's gloss, not Gay's claim (gay-bourgeois-experience-v2-ch5-4-sexualizing-modernity).
