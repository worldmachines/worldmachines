The Convenient Other

Commons · Entities · updated 26 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Peter Gay's name for the collective enemy onto which a group projects its own unacceptable aggression, an ancient alibi that the nineteenth century armored with race science and that recurs in colonial atrocity, duelling fraternities, satire, punishment, and the narcissism of small differences of 1914.

The convenient Other is the second of the three alibis-for-aggression in peter-gay's The Cultivation of Hatred (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3). It is a collective enemy whose supposed vices license the hostility of those who define themselves against it. Gay treats it as ancient and endlessly renewable. It appears in the Hebrews' covenant, in Greek self-flattery, and in Pericles's funeral oration: “the more one loved one's own, the more one was entitled to hate the Other.” The nineteenth century's distinctive contribution was to armor this old drama with the borrowed authority of science (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p1-convenient-other-race-theory).

The mechanism: projection

Gay's explanatory key is Freud's mid-1890s theory of projection. People defend against unacceptable wishes by expelling them “onto the convenient Other.” Gay's formula for the sequence is “I am bad / I am not bad / You are bad.” Because the mechanism is largely unconscious, it works: it denies one's own belligerence and licenses aggression against whoever now carries it. The Dreyfus Affair opens the argument. The split between friend and enemy is “immensely serviceable” because it “solidifies the bracing sense of one's merits” while “assuaging the secret fear of one's imperfections.” Chekhov diagnosed the same mechanism in the same affair before Freud published. Gay treats this as evidence that projection is a real social pattern and not only his own retrospective theory (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p1-convenient-other-race-theory). The targets are interchangeable. William James's list of historical victims includes Jews, Albigensians, Quakers, Mormons, and Armenians (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-front-introduction). Gay's own list adds Freemasons, immigrant laborers, and capitalists. The mechanism cares less about who the Other is than that there be one.

William Graham Sumner supplied the group-level version: “two codes of morals … one for comrades inside and the other for strangers outside.” Cohesion inside is manufactured by hostility outside, and religion intensifies this ethnocentrism rather than restraining it. Gay notes that “Freud would say it not much better” (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p1-convenient-other-colonial-violence).

Race: classification curdled into ranking

Linné, Buffon, and Blumenbach still did Enlightenment taxonomy: “Race theorizing had not yet become racism.” Gay treats the later slide into Gobineau, Broca, and Knox as a specific, explicable degradation. Broca's craniometrists kept data that embarrassed their thesis and complicated their criteria rather than abandon the ranking, which made their measurement regime self-immunizing. Over the century the tone moved from triumphalist to anxious racism, from a crowned superior race to a forecast of apocalyptic “race war.” Gay reads that shift as a symptom of mounting unease about modern civilization (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p1-convenient-other-race-theory).

In the colonies the racial alibi was portable. It licensed Eyre in Jamaica, Carl Peters in East Africa, Leopold's Congo, and von Trotha's campaign against the Herero, which German voters ratified at the polls in 1907. Even Eyre's prosecutors shared his racial contempt. A century of skeptics, from Bagehot to Boas, dismantled the science without dislodging racism. Gay's diagnosis is that racism “provided pleasure,” and people do not easily surrender what pleases them. Jim Crow turns out to be a late-century invention, not ancient custom (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p1-convenient-other-colonial-violence).

The Other across the volume

  • Exclusion and imitation. After 1871, bigoted fraternities declared Jews satisfaktionsunfähig, unworthy of a duel. Jewish students, Herzl among them, sought the Mensur precisely to disprove the stereotype of cowardice, so exclusion and imitation ran on the same rails (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-front-mensur-2-politics-adolescence).
  • Statesmen's enemies. Bismarck's “natural appetite for combat” required targets such as Jews, the Center Party, and the Social Democrats. Dividing the world into allies and enemies “provided him with an alibi for his pugnacity” (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p3-modern-caesars-bismarck). Roosevelt's “race suicide” panic is the convenient Other lived as domestic ideology, with inconsistencies Gay keeps in view (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p1-tr-extremist-of-the-center). Jahn's gymnastics “denounced Jews and Frenchmen with equal venom” (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p6-sport-referee-olympics).
  • Humor punching down. Busch drew the hook-nosed Schmulchen Schiefelbeiner and gloated over besieged Paris despite close Jewish friendships (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p5-laughing-cruel-sage). Punch's apelike Irish and Puck's jibes at Jewish immigrants were targets that “exposed publishers and their artists to no risk.” Such humorists were “not physicians to their society, but its panders” (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p5-physicians-to-society). Denied his true target, Daumier turned on women seeking divorce rights. The victim becomes executioner because aggression chooses “easy, defenseless targets” (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p5-victim-as-executioner).
  • Criminals, women, and the racialized rapist. Paul Näcke's “virtual animals” extends the logic to the criminal (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p2-bourgeois-conscience-capital-punishment). Feminists became a convenient object for men's displaced anxiety about dependence on women (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p4-domesticity-alibis-power-paradox). In the American South, rape was defined as “almost by definition” committed by Black men on white women (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p2-domestic-violence-rape-suicide).
  • The near neighbor, 1914. German hatred fell hardest on Britain, the nation “they had most admired.” Gay reads this through Freud's “narcissism of small differences”: likeness, not difference, licenses the sharpest hostility (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-epilogue-august-4-1914).

World Machines resonances (hedged)

  • Craniometry and race classification rhyme with legibility-as-machine-core: the same drive to make a population readable, measurable, and orderable. The notes stress that here it is “legibility in the service of an alibi rather than of administration,” serving domination and the psychological wage of aggression rather than state capacity (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p1-convenient-other-race-theory, gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p1-convenient-other-colonial-violence).
  • Rival Anglo-Saxon and Celtic pedigrees show a constructed national character doing the alibi-work race does. This loosely rhymes with nationalism; the two registers blur constantly in Gay's examples (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p1-convenient-other-race-theory).
  • The fin-de-siècle turn to anxious racism loosely echoes the “morbid symptoms” of a gramsci-gap. The resonance lies in the psychological shape of the anxiety, not in the timescale, since Gay describes one civilization losing confidence rather than a transition between machines (gay-bourgeois-experience-v3-p1-convenient-other-race-theory).
