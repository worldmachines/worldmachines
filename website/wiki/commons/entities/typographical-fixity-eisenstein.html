Typographical Fixity (Eisenstein)

Commons · Entities · updated 27 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Eisenstein's master feature of print culture: preservation through multiplied copies rather than durable material, which arrested and reversed scribal drift and made revolutions, heresies, observations and authorship cumulative and irreversible, including the paradoxical “permanent Renaissance”.

For elizabeth-eisenstein, preservation is the most consequential of print's effects. “Typographical fixity is a basic prerequisite for the rapid advancement of learning” (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch03-s4-data-collection). The suffix marks the page as her usage. It concerns texts, images and data becoming fixed and checkable, not fixity in general.

Quantity, not material

Scribal transmission could not reliably preserve anything. A manuscript was “fairly impermanent unless it was stored away and not used”, exposed to “moisture and vermin, theft and fire”, and each copy drifted from its exemplar. The old rule held: “Much is preserved when little is written; little is preserved when much is written.” Print broke the rule on a less durable substrate, paper rather than parchment. Enough dispersed copies survived even when any one perished. Jefferson's letter to Wythe states the logic: preservation through multiplication succeeded where lock-and-key had failed (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch03-s5-preservative-powers).

Arrest, then reversal, of drift

Fixity did more than freeze. Across successive editions, printers such as Estienne and Ortelius “were also able to improve on themselves”, converting the “immemorial drift” of corruption into feedback (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch03-s4-data-collection). The direction was not automatic. Herbals from the 1480s to 1526 grew steadily more distorted under “ignorant printers driving to make quick profits”; only under “technically proficient masters” could fresh observations be duplicated without blurring (same note). Corrupt maps and star positions were at first merely duplicated. Once corrected they could be “fixed permanently”, so that “an age-old process of corruption was being decisively arrested and was eventually reversed” (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch08-conclusion-1). Printed maps made the user-to-mapmaker feedback that Kenneth Boulding identified achievable at all (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch07-s1-great-book-of-nature).

Even error became productive. The first printed edition of a classical text, however “riddled with errors”, froze one textus receptus that generations could argue over passage by passage (eisenstein-printing-revolution-afterword-3). “Erasmus or Bellarmine could issue errata; Jerome or Alcuin could not” (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch03-s2-standardization).

What fixity made permanent

  • Revivals. Once Greek fonts were cut, knowledge of Greek never withered again in the West (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch03-s5-preservative-powers). Panofsky was right that the Italian Renaissance was “total and permanent” where the Carolingian and twelfth-century revivals were “limited and transitory”. Fixity is the causal term his formula lacks. Even Gothic type proved as durable as Roman, because what endures is being “impressed by type and not by a human hand” (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch05-permanent-renaissance-1). Findings “permanently secured by being registered in print” turned intermittent recovery into an “unending series of discoveries”. It was not the Constantinople refugees (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch05-permanent-renaissance-2).
  • Dissent. Lutheranism was “from the first the child of the printed book”. Fixity converted protest into a permanent schism where Wycliffite and Waldensian heresies had been transitory. The same mechanism let liturgy “fossilize” into a uniform mold for four centuries (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch06-christendom-disrupted-1).
  • Law and language. Printed statutes and charters made custom auditable (the 1533 “Tabula” counting Magna Carta's reconfirmations). The vernaculars that got primers and Bibles sedimented into national literary languages (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch03-s5-preservative-powers).
  • Events and names. A nova became “Tycho's star”, pinned to one man's name for a century (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch07-s2-copernican-revolution). Attaching a permanent name to a permanent text made authorship, priority disputes, patents, plagiarism and copyright possible (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch03-s5-preservative-powers).
  • Progress itself. “Permanence introduced a new form of progressive change. The preservation of the old, in brief, was a prerequisite for a tradition of the new” (same note).

Cumulative and irreversible as a criterion

Eisenstein uses fixity to decide which shifts count as revolutions. The twelfth-century move from monastic scriptoria to lay stationers was real but reversible, since scriptoria enjoyed a “last golden age” before Gutenberg. The fifteenth-century shift was “cumulative and irreversible” (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch01-unacknowledged-revolution).

The permanent Renaissance, and fixity's costs

She proposes separating the Renaissance as a cultural movement from “the ensemble of changes which were ushered in by print”. Conflating them yields the “paradoxical hybrid” of a permanent rebirth, “a contradiction in terms”, since “only dead ones can be embalmed and indefinitely preserved” (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch05-permanent-renaissance-3). The embalming image recurs. When Cujas fully restored and indexed the Digest, the more internally consistent the text became, the less it mattered to living jurisprudence (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch03-s3-reorganizing-texts). Preservation also accumulates an ever-larger “burden of the past”, an “overpopulation of Parnassus” (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch04-s2-men-of-letters). Scribal-era habits outlived their conditions. Under hand copying “the ancientest must needs be the right” was realism, because texts really did degrade (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch05-permanent-renaissance-3). Rosicrucians and Freemasons went on claiming that Descartes and Newton had merely rediscovered ancient secrets (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch03-s5-preservative-powers).

World Machines resonances, with their hedges

  • modernity-machine. Several notes read fixity as the mechanism by which the machine's apparatus accumulates rather than recurring episodically (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch03-s5-preservative-powers, eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch05-permanent-renaissance-3). Eisenstein makes a historian's claim about texts, statutes and tables, not about state-scale measurement.
  • legibility-as-precondition-not-cause. The herbal and map cases show fixity's direction depending on who runs the press, and Copernicus had wide access without critical rigor. The infrastructure enables but does not guarantee (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch03-s4-data-collection, eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch07-s2-copernican-revolution).
  • Legibility. Making custom auditable and stellar events citable is a local instance of what WMP names at civilizational scale (eisenstein-printing-revolution-ch03-s5-preservative-powers).
