The Succession of Dominant Centres (Braudel)

Commons · Entities · updated 24 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Braudel's rule that a world-economy has one dominant city at a time and that cities take turns (Venice, Antwerp, Genoa, Amsterdam, London, then New York). Centres rise through position rather than effort, age through a turn to finance that he calls a 'sign of autumn', and withdraw rather than collapse. The hand-off shows first at the periphery and in crises.

Source: braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3. This is the second of the three rules of a braudel-world-economy: every world-economy has one dominant city at a time, and the cities take turns. The sequence Venice, Antwerp, Genoa, Amsterdam, London structures the narrative chapters of fernand-braudel's third volume.

The sequence

  • Before Venice. Amalfi rises first and then loses "her place in the mainstream of history" while staying a presence in the Mediterranean (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-italian-cities-champagne-fairs). Europe's first world-economy was bipolar, with Bruges as the northern capital and Italy the southern pole (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-first-world-economy-bruges-hansa). The Champagne fairs sat "midway between the two poles of attraction" as a rotating "continuous market." They were an exception to the one-city rule (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-italian-cities-champagne-fairs). Bruges and Lübeck were "Weltmärkte, world-markets — but not quite world-cities" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-first-world-economy-bruges-hansa).
  • Venice. Venice won the War of Chioggia (1378–81) through home waters and an early hold on the Levant route, not capitalist superiority. Braudel says Genoa was "far more modern than Venice" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-venice).
  • Antwerp. Lisbon triumphed in pepper but "remained the captive of a certain world-economy into which the city was already integrated," while the continent's centre of gravity moved north. The crown passed to Antwerp, which "woke up one morning to find itself there" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-portugal-and-antwerp).
  • Genoa, 1557–1627. A "handful of banker-financiers" ran the Habsburg payments system, "not by her ships" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-age-of-the-genoese).
  • Amsterdam. Amsterdam was "the last city-empire" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-united-provinces-at-home).
  • London. London held the centre with "the backing of the entire English economy" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-united-provinces-at-home). Its supremacy was glimpsed at Utrecht (1713), visible by 1763 and achieved by the Treaty of Versailles (1783) (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-england-island-and-pound).
  • New York. Braudel dates the hand-off from London to around 1929 (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-world-economies-ground-rules, braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-decline-of-amsterdam).

The bipolar structure ended around 1600. Amsterdam's rise was "not a mere transfer of weight from Antwerp to Holland" but gave Europe a single centre of gravity for the first time (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-first-world-economy-bruges-hansa). The four secular turning-points of 1350, 1650 and 1817 mark the shift from Venice's Mediterranean system to Amsterdam and then to England. Braudel leaves open whether 1973–4 is another such turning-point (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-divisions-of-time).

Not every centre held the full arsenal

The first three centres each held only part of "the complete arsenal of economic domination." Venice had trade and finance but little industry. Antwerp had no shipping and no politics of its own. Genoa staked everything on banking and was "no more than an interlude." Amsterdam and, still more, London combined shipping, industry, credit and a national territorial base (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-world-economies-ground-rules). This is also the move from the city-centred economy to the territorial one. City-states outsourced their primary sector almost entirely. Territorial states first had to discipline agriculture and build a tax bureaucracy, which is why England "turned out in the end to be an infinitely superior rival to little Holland" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-elements-and-compounds). France held the fairs, Paris and Europe's leading university, yet the routes that mattered "encircled France at a distance" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-italian-cities-champagne-fairs). Her very size kept her from ever becoming the centre (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-france-paris-and-lyon).

How a centre rises

  • Position, not effort. Braudel's verdict on Portugal is deliberately anti-heroic: "foreign merchants followed the successful venture and took it over once it had taken place rather than initiating it" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-portugal-and-antwerp). Antwerp's three booms were each lit by an outside fuse: Portuguese pepper with German silver, then Spanish-American silver, then industry (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-portugal-and-antwerp).
  • Engineered dependency. The Fondaco dei Tedeschi forced German merchants to trade only in Venice and on Venice's terms (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-venice). Dutch commission merchants advanced credit so that they could dictate terms (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-amsterdam-entrepot-and-credit).
  • A home base. Dutch primacy rested on intensive agriculture, extreme urbanization, the herring fisheries and the fluyt. Toleration was compulsory rather than sentimental: "it is in any case hard to imagine the centre of a world-economy as anything but tolerant" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-united-provinces-at-home).
  • Serial antagonism. England expelled or dispossessed each foreign interest in turn: the Italian bankers, the Hansa's Stalhof, the Royal Exchange founded against Antwerp, the Navigation Act against Holland. Braudel sums it up as "today Antwerp, tomorrow Amsterdam, the next day Paris." A stable pound and a punctually serviced national debt were the weapons (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-england-island-and-pound, braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch04-london-great-britain-national-debt).

How a centre ages

  • The sign of autumn. An entrepôt's information advantage erodes as news travels faster, and its surplus capital turns from trade to pure finance. Braudel's authority for this mechanism is Kindleberger. Genoa did it in the fifteenth century and London would do it later. Braudel calls this turn a structural regularity, not moral decline. Every capitalist centre that reaches financial expansion has thereby "announced its maturity." Amsterdam's lending was more purely rentier than London's, because no industrial development stood behind it (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-amsterdam-entrepot-and-credit).
  • Fated by success. Dominant capitalism is "fated by its very success to come up against the threshold of financial activities or rather acrobatics," from the Champagne fairs through Genoa to Amsterdam (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-decline-of-amsterdam). Braudel historicizes Hilferding's finance capitalism in the same way: it "did not wait for the triumph of the City of London to come into being" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch06-finance-conjuncture-living-standards). Venice was "trapped by the logic of her own success," which Braudel compares to twentieth-century Britain (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-venice).
  • Withdrawal, not decadence. Amsterdam "gave way to London, as Venice did to Antwerp," yet stayed "one of the high altars of world capitalism" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-decline-of-amsterdam). Its regents became rentiers and adopted French fashion (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-dutch-state-taxes-business). The Genoese did not fall with the Spanish bankruptcy of 1627. They had already begun a "radical and wholescale redistribution" of their capital (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-age-of-the-genoese).

The hand-off

  • The eclipse of a centre "nearly always shows up first at the periphery," in the string of colonial losses that mark its unraveling (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-world-economies-ground-rules).
  • The city that "is becoming or has become the centre" is the first to show the system's shocks and the first to recover. London's origin of the 1772–3 crisis shows leadership had already passed there, and Braudel reads Black Thursday in 1929 the same way for New York (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-decline-of-amsterdam).
  • Political and economic fortune can come apart. England was "defeated politically" at Versailles in 1783 yet "emerged the economic victor" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-decline-of-amsterdam).
  • Capital crosses before crowns do. After 1730, Dutch capital flowed into English state loans, and in 1782–3 "the English card was a joker — a winning card which suddenly lost the game" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-away-from-amsterdam).
  • Contemporaries sometimes forecast the next hand-off. In the 1760s Accarias de Serionne warned that "New England is more to be feared than Old England." In 1801 a French diplomat described England's position relative to the United States as Holland's relative to England a century earlier (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-colonies-against-europe).

Outside Europe

The same logic runs in other world-economies. The Ottoman centre moved from Bursa to Aleppo and Alexandria, then Smyrna, then Istanbul (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-turkish-empire). In the Far East, Malacca (from 1403/1409) gave way to Batavia (1619), which lost primacy to Calcutta by around 1760 (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch05-india-decline-malacca-new-centres).

World Machines resonances

  • world-machines. The notes read the succession as a rhyme "in shape (centre succession, real turbulence at the hinge) rather than in mechanism or scale" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-world-economies-ground-rules). Centres turn over on "a scale of decades to a couple of centuries," not the thousand-year Dawn–Day–Dusk arc (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-italian-cities-champagne-fairs). The rise–dominance–senescence pattern repeating from host city to host city is "nested inside the larger cycle rather than constituting it" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-amsterdam-entrepot-and-credit). And where WMP expects a machine to cede ground to a successor, Braudel's capitalism outlives every centre, concentrating in crises rather than dying (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-conclusion-past-and-present).
  • gramsci-gap. A hand-off is framed as a shift in economic "equilibrium" rather than an ideological interregnum (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch01-world-economies-ground-rules). The Hansa's decline is gap-shaped, but Braudel treats it as "straightforward economic supersession" (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch02-first-world-economy-bruges-hansa).
  • decay-sequence. Amsterdam's formal prestige outliving its functional dominance loosely rhymes with the Functional-to-Ornamental drift, though that concept is built for institutions, not a city's trading position (braudel-civilization-capitalism-v3-ch03-decline-of-amsterdam).
