Symphilosophie (Wulf)

Commons · Entities · updated 28 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Extends Symphilosophie with Andrea Wulf's narrative of the Jena Set's collaborative practice, from Goethe and Schiller's complementary methods and the concealed authorship of the Xenien to the Athenaeum's 'giant symphonising', and records its recurrent hiding of women's labour and its curdling into grievance.

Extends Symphilosophie with what Andrea Wulf's wulf-magnificent-rebels shows of the practice. The word arrives late in her narrative; the practice is built up through a sequence of collaborative forms, each of which the reading notes mark as a precursor or variant rather than the thing itself.

A sequence of collaborative forms

  • A complementary pair. Goethe moved from the particular to the general, Schiller from the general to the particular; Goethe later said the competition between object and subject sealed their connection (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch01-happy-event-2).
  • Social thinking. Schiller said his ideas 'develop faster and better in conversation' with Wilhelm von Humboldt; with Goethe they formed 'a three-leaf clover of critical thought' (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch03-finest-minds-2).
  • Thinking in public. Fichte said he generated his system 'with the listeners, and before their eyes' (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch02-priest-of-truth-2).
  • Concealed authorship. Goethe and Schiller mixed the Xenien's lines 'so much that no one could tell any more who had written what', which Wulf says let them 'aim sharper and hit harder' (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch04-intellectual-friction-2).
  • Infinite reflection between friends. Novalis and Friedrich Schlegel turned Fichte's closed self-reflection into an endless back-and-forth (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch05-novalis-in-love).
  • Giant symphonising. Friedrich's phrase for the Athenaeum, their 'communal work of art'. Wulf says the friends were the first to make the deliberate fragment a genre, and gives practical reasons: fragments are quick, need little research, can be written together over a meal, and radical lines buried among milder ones might escape Prussian censors (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch09-sublime-impertinence-1). 'Symphilosophise' names two minds as divided halves (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch09-sublime-impertinence-2).
  • Conversation as holiday. In Dresden the friends argued through the galleries, an 'ur-assembly of humanity' in Friedrich's phrase, and Caroline's 'Paintings. A Conversation' is the written trace (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch10-symphilosophy). Letters themselves counted as symphilosophising (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch13-divorce-women-sex).
  • One roof. In Leutragasse in November 1799 the friends agreed they never had to agree, since nothing was duller than 'flat uniformity of opinion'; Novalis: 'I produce best in dialogue' (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch15-new-confederation). They treated revision as a virtue; Novalis feared their works might be 'already withered' on publication (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch14-schlegel-clique).

Who got the credit

The collective forms repeatedly hid women's work. Caroline edited, reviewed and translated under August Wilhelm's name (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch04-intellectual-friction-2, wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch06-splendid-circle-2) and was the Athenaeum's de facto editor without publishing under her own (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch09-sublime-impertinence-1); Dorothea's translations appeared under Friedrich's name (wulf-magnificent-rebels-epilogue-after-jena-1). Wulf hedges the reasons (fees, wariness of attention) (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch06-splendid-circle-2). The collective also curated its dead: Schlegel and Tieck edited Novalis by chopping fragments and suppressing his political and scientific work, which Wulf argues built a legend (wulf-magnificent-rebels-epilogue-after-jena-2).

When the practice turns

The same intensity that generated ideas generated grievance: 'Gone were the days of symphilosophising' (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch16-republic-of-despots-1), and in Dorothea's letters shared enterprise gave way to rivalry (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch16-republic-of-despots-2). The costs were visible earlier too: Friedrich's demands strained Caroline and August Wilhelm's marriage (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch09-sublime-impertinence-1).

Hedges

Goethe's late claim that his 'life's work is that of a collective' resonates with the ideal and with collaborative-creativity, but the note stresses he meant borrowing from and absorbing the world, not organised group authorship (wulf-magnificent-rebels-epilogue-after-jena-2). The notes read the fragment's fit for many-hand production as only a half-rhyme with history-machine, since the Athenaeum was deliberate and authored (wulf-magnificent-rebels-ch09-sublime-impertinence-1). For the scene that housed the practice see jena-set-wulf.
