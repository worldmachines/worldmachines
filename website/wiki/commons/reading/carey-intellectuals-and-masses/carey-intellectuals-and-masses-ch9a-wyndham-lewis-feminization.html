Wyndham Lewis and the Feminization of the West

Commons · The Intellectuals and the Masses: Pride and Prejudice among the Literary Intelligentsia · updated 22 August 2026 · source on GitHub
Carey traces Wyndham Lewis's lifelong misogyny to a personal psychology of bodily disgust and resentment, shows how he generalized it into a theory that democracy, mass culture, and even relativity and stream-of-consciousness fiction were symptoms of a 'feminization' of the West, and follows that logic through to his apologetics for Mussolini and Hitler and his racism and antisemitism.

This unit examines Wyndham Lewis as the paradigm case of an intellectual whose contempt for women, the masses, and modern mass culture form a single, self-reinforcing system. Carey moves from Lewis's biography (abandoned children, a hidden wife, boastful promiscuity) through his Nietzsche-derived 'masculine' aesthetic, into his elaborate thesis that democracy and modernity are a 'feminization' of Western values (elaborated at length in Time and Western Man, 1928), and finally to the payoff: Lewis's championship of Mussolini and Hitler as the aesthetic-political solution to a world he saw as dissolving into feminine flux, mass conformity, and racial 'melting-pot' degeneration.

Themes

Misogyny as biographical wound, generalized into doctrine. Carey opens with Lewis's father's desertion, his string of mistresses, illegitimate children he 'disposed of,' and a wife, Anne Hoskyns, kept literally hidden from friends. Carey reads Lewis's adult 'masculine' intellectual stance — contempt for women and children as a badge of artistic seriousness — as compensation for this history, compounded by what Carey calls a Nietzsche-derived revulsion at his own 'bodily functions,' which Lewis projected outward as disgust at 'soft,' 'fluid,' feminine textures.

The feminization-of-the-West thesis. Lewis's signature move is to fuse his anti-woman and anti-democratic prejudices into one claim: that mass democracy has produced 'an increasingly feminine world' in which 'the natural feminine hostility to the intellect' now dominates. Homosexuality, Bloomsbury, department-store shoppers, Pater's influence on Eliot — all are recruited as symptoms of the same rot. This is not incidental color; Carey shows it as the generative engine of Lewis's entire intellectual system, including his god (who exists solely to endorse Lewis's own taste in art).

Time versus space as gendered metaphysics. Time and Western Man recasts Bergson, Einstein, Proust, and Joyce as apostles of a 'feminine' time-cult of flux, durée, and 'merging,' against which Lewis poses a 'masculine' ideal of static, bounded, classical space — things 'standing apart,' 'the air circulating freely.' Carey treats this as a sensory prejudice dressed up as philosophy, and links it directly to Lewis's aesthetic conservatism: art's values are 'timeless' and 'static,' incapable of reinterpretation — a metaphysics of stasis-as-virtue that recurs across his hostility to photography, cinema, jazz, and stream-of-consciousness ('snapshotting'). This static/flux polarity is a striking instance of the tension between fixed, legible order and irreducible proliferation that runs through world-machines historiography — Lewis coding the fixed, classical, hierarchical pole as 'masculine' truth and the proliferating, multiplying, non-standard pole as feminine contamination, a near-literal aesthetic version of the friction between legibility-as-machine-core and divergence-machine logics, though Lewis of course intends it as pure metaphysics rather than institutional description.

Contempt for mass technology and mobility. Lewis's hatred of photography, advertising, cinema, radio, tourism, and popular education is presented as continuous with his gender theory — all are 'vulgar and feminine,' mechanisms that destroy individuality and produce 'hysterical imbecility' in a docile mass. Carey highlights Lewis's genuinely prescient sketch of home video as an object of ridicule, and his frank argument that even Tolstoy should be kept from the masses lest it incite them.

The robot/beast contradiction and the eugenic fantasy of a ruling caste. The Art of Being Ruled oscillates between picturing mass man as mindless automaton and as violent beast — Carey notes this is a common, self-contradicting move among the intellectuals surveyed in the book. Lewis's 'solution' is an inbred intellectual caste, entered by examination, potentially producing a 'new species of superior being' through selective interbreeding — biological elitism as the logical endpoint of his aesthetic hierarchy.

Fascism as aesthetic solution, and its racist extension. Carey argues Lewis's attraction to Mussolini and Hitler was fundamentally aesthetic — an appeal to 'rigid,' 'disciplined,' clean-lined masculine order over democratic 'humbug' and feminine flux — and traces how this shades into willful misrepresentation of Nazism (storm-troopers as a 'picked police force,' Hitler as a 'man of peace') and into explicit racism and antisemitism in Paleface and Count Your Dead, where whiteness is 'aristocratic,' blackness 'proletarian,' and Jews are blamed for provoking their own persecution.

Notable passages

The passage on the 'two strands' of Lewis's psyche is the hinge of the whole unit: it is where Carey shows Lewis explicitly welding anti-woman prejudice to anti-democratic prejudice into the single feminization thesis. The Time and Western Man passage demonstrates how thoroughly that thesis colonizes even his engagement with Einstein and Bergson, reducing physics and philosophy to gendered sensory metaphor. The home-video prediction shows Carey's method of using Lewis's own prose — its lower-middle-class ventriloquism ('Yes, do lets!') — to expose class contempt riding alongside the philosophical objection. The 'robot/beast' passage captures the definitional incoherence Carey diagnoses in intellectuals' image of the masses. The extended passage on Jews shows the antisemitism is not incidental but structurally identical to the anti-mass, anti-feminine logic already established. And the storm-troopers passage is the clearest evidence of Lewis's aesthetic-fascist apologetics, where 'pillars of the law' language directly recycles the 'masculine,' 'marmoreal' vocabulary built up across the whole chapter.
