Sowers of Discord and Falsifiers: Malebolge's Ninth Chasm to the Giants' Well (Inf. XXVIII–XXXI)

Commons · The Divine Comedy · updated 20 July 2026 · source on GitHub
Dante and Virgil cross the eighth and ninth chasms of Malebolge, where sowers of discord (Mohammed, Bertrand de Born) are eternally hacked apart and falsifiers of metal, person, coin, and word (Griffolino, Capocchio, Gianni Schicchi, Myrrha, Master Adam, Sinon) rot with disease, before descending past the giants Nimrod, Ephialtes, and Antaeus into the pit of treachery.

This unit carries Dante and Virgil through the end of the ninth Malebolge chasm — the sowers of scandal and schism, mutilated and re-wounded in an endless circuit — into the tenth chasm of the falsifiers (alchemists, impersonators, counterfeiters, and liars, all afflicted with plague-like disease), and finally to the rim of the pit where the giants Nimrod, Ephialtes, and Antaeus stand sentinel; Antaeus lowers the two pilgrims down toward Cocytus, the frozen floor reserved for traitors.

Themes

Contrapasso as literalized rupture. The schismatics of Canto XXVIII enact their sin as bodily fact: Mohammed is "cleft" from chin to fundament, Bertrand de Born carries his own severed head "lantern-wise," his brain "parted" from its trunk because in life he "parted" a father from a son. Dante states the logic outright through Bertrand's mouth — division sown among the living becomes division inflicted on the damned body, healed only so a demon's sword can reopen it on each circuit of the ditch.

Falsification as corruption of substance. Canto XXIX–XXX groups four kinds of falsifiers — alchemists who counterfeited metal (Griffolino, Capocchio), impersonators who counterfeited identity (Gianni Schicchi, Myrrha), a counterfeiter of coin (Master Adam of Brescia), and liars/false witnesses (Sinon, Potiphar's wife) — under a single punishing logic: scabbed, swollen, feverish bodies that mirror the adulteration they practiced on the world's materials. The text frames this explicitly as the work of "All-searching Justice," a kind of cosmic auditor keeping "her dread record" of forgery — the sin of corrupting a trustworthy sign (metal, testament, coin, word) is punished by corrupting the sign of the self, the body.

The pettiness of hell and the pilgrim's complicity. The slapstick brawl between Sinon and Master Adam — each accusing the other of the worse lie — briefly turns the pit into farce, and Dante's own rapt attention draws a sharp rebuke from Virgil: lingering over such spectacle is "a joy for vulgar minds." This is a rare moment of authorial self-correction, flagging the voyeuristic pull of atrocity-viewing that the poem otherwise indulges throughout Malebolge.

Personal and political stakes. Amid the political traitors and Italian factionalists (Curio, Mosca dei Lamberti, the frivolous Sienese, the Tuscan alchemists), Dante meets his own kinsman Geri del Bello, whose unavenged murder passes him in accusing silence — grounding the canto's abstract themes of schism and vendetta in Dante's own family history.

Babel and the collapse of shared language. Nimrod, chained among the giants, shouts pure nonsense ("Raphel bai ameth sabi almi"), and Virgil explains that Nimrod's "ill counsel" is why "no more / One tongue prevails" — the confusion of tongues rendered as a giant literally beyond communication, guarding the descent into the circle of betrayal, where trust and shared meaning have collapsed entirely. This reads as a striking premodern figure for the failure of a coordinating, legible common language — worth flagging against legibility-as-machine-core for readers tracking how infrastructures of shared meaning break down at civilizational hinge-points.

Notable passages

Mohammed's self-display ("How is Mohammed mangled!") states the chasm's contrapasso doctrine directly: those who "did sow / Scandal and schism" are correspondingly "rent." Bertrand de Born's closing lines make the logic explicit as legal-sounding retribution ("Thus the law / Of retribution fiercely works in me"), the poem's clearest statement of the mechanism governing the whole ninth chasm. The falsifiers' introduction ("the minister of the most mighty Lord, / All-searching Justice") frames the tenth chasm as a ledger of forgery being audited eternally. The Sinon–Adamo quarrel and Virgil's rebuke ("Such wrangling is a joy for vulgar minds") supply the unit's moment of moral self-awareness. Nimrod's gibberish and Virgil's gloss on the confusion of tongues mark the pit's edge as a place where language itself has failed. The unit closes with Antaeus stooping to lift Virgil and Dante and set them down at the threshold of Cocytus, "that Lucifer with Judas low ingulfs" — the physical and moral floor of the whole descent now in view.
