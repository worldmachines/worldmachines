1493 (Charles C. Mann)

Commons · Summaries · updated 28 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Charles C. Mann's 1493 (2011) extends Alfred Crosby's Columbian Exchange into an account of how Colón's voyage created a single ecological and economic world, the Homogenocene, tracing silver, crops, microbes and people across the Atlantic and Pacific and keeping globalization's gains and costs on one unsettled ledger.

Author: charles-c-mann. 1493: Uncovering the New World Columbus Created (Knopf, 2011).

The book by its own account

Mann starts in a college greenhouse of heirloom tomatoes and follows the question of why an American crop is bred worldwide to Alfred Crosby's Ecological Imperialism. He adopts Crosby's frame, the Columbian Exchange, as his organizing idea (mann-1493-prologue). His thesis is that Colón's voyage did not discover a New World but created one. From the collision of two old worlds, or three counting Africa, a single ecological system emerged 'almost instantly, in biological terms', and by the nineteenth century a single ecological and economic system (mann-1493-prologue). Mann names the resulting era with a term some biologists use, the Homogenocene: once-distinct ecosystems blended into a uniform mix (mann-1493-ch01-to-the-lighthouse). He declines the free-trade versus anti-globalization polarity, suspects both sides are partly right, and argues that four-century-old events 'set a template' for today's disputes (mann-1493-prologue). See columbian-exchange-mann and homogenocene-mann.

Structure

  • Opening. The garden, Crosby and a preview of four sections (mann-1493-prologue). Mann then visits the ruins of La Isabela, Colón's failed colony on Hispaniola, where he places the start of globalization and the biological reknitting of Pangaea (mann-1493-ch01-seams-of-pangaea), and the Columbus Lighthouse in Santo Domingo, where he deflates Colón (wrong about the earth's size, lucky) and introduces the Homogenocene (mann-1493-ch01-to-the-lighthouse). A neglected Manila monument to Legazpi and Urdaneta dates the economic counterpart, the silk-for-silver link of 1571-72 (mann-1493-ch01-shiploads-of-silver). An imagined flight round the world in 1642 ties silver, crops, disease and climate into one turbulent system (mann-1493-ch01-reversals-of-fortune).
  • The Atlantic. Jamestown as the unintended English opening of the Exchange: Rolfe's tobacco, possibly earthworms, and a Powhatan landscape the English could not read (mann-1493-ch02-lowly-creatures-strange-land); the Virginia Company as a joint-stock risk pool with appalling mortality (mann-1493-ch02-risk-pool); tobacco and its ecological package undoing Tsenacomoco (mann-1493-ch02-english-flies). Then malaria and yellow fever: seasoning and extractive states, the turn from servants to African slaves, and the mosquito at Yorktown (mann-1493-ch03-extractive-states-seasoning, mann-1493-ch03-about-face, mann-1493-ch03-no-distempers-villa-plasmodia, mann-1493-ch03-yellow-jack-war-mosquitoes).
  • The Pacific. The Ming sea ban and the wokou (mann-1493-ch04-extra-effort-merchants-pirates), China's failed currencies and hunger for silver (mann-1493-ch04-out-of-money), Potosí and the monetary shock (mann-1493-ch04-treasure-of-the-world), the Yuegang-Manila junk trade and the Parián (mann-1493-ch04-wooden-noses), and Manila's massacres and the two roles of trade (mann-1493-ch04-magic-mountain). Then the ecological feedback in China: American crops and the shack people (mann-1493-ch05-hidden-passengers), Hong Liangji and highland collapse (mann-1493-ch05-malthusian-interlude-mountains), and the Loess Plateau today (mann-1493-ch05-unlearning-from-dazhai).
  • Europe in the World. The potato and the agro-industrial complex (mann-1493-ch06-potato-wars-sea-of-genes), guano (mann-1493-ch06-guano-age), the blight and the Irish famine (mann-1493-ch06-modern-famine-lazy-beds), the Colorado potato beetle (mann-1493-ch06-war-upon-the-beetles); then rubber as a fundamental industrial material (mann-1493-ch07-no-birds-grease-chemistry), the Amazon boom (mann-1493-ch07-bather-in-the-bubbly) and Wickham's seeds in Asia (mann-1493-ch07-wickham-end-of-world).
  • The human exchange. Juan Garrido opens it (mann-1493-ch08-johnny-good-looking); sugar and the Atlantic islands (mann-1493-ch08-bad-beginnings); encomienda, the New Laws and Africans across Spanish America (mann-1493-ch08-new-world-born); the casta system (mann-1493-ch08-family-values); Asians in Mexico City (mann-1493-ch08-shook-up-city). A chapter on maroons runs from Calabar (mann-1493-ch09-in-calabar) through Panama (mann-1493-ch09-in-the-isthmus), Palmares (mann-1493-ch09-africans-in-charge) and negotiated freedom (mann-1493-ch09-capitulations) to Amazonian quilombos (mann-1493-ch09-shake-it-ox-dona-rosario).
  • Coda. The Philippines: introduced species, Ifugao terraces that are 'a monument to the galleon trade', and Mann's own 'fractured cerebration' (mann-1493-ch10-fractured-cerebration-staircases); Bulalacao, externalities and the garden (mann-1493-ch10-on-the-boat).
  • Appendices. A note on naming people without anachronism (mann-1493-appendix-a-fighting-words), and Zaytun as a 'first pass at globalization' that explains why Fujian became the silver hub (mann-1493-appendix-b-globalization-in-beta).

Throughlines

  • Processes nobody controlled or understood. Crosby's Exchange was 'neither fully controlled nor understood by its participants' (mann-1493-prologue). Mann's cases run from fire ants on Hispaniola (mann-1493-ch01-seams-of-pangaea) and earthworms in Virginia (mann-1493-ch02-lowly-creatures-strange-land) to a blight carried on a guano ship (mann-1493-ch06-modern-famine-lazy-beds). He keeps the evidential status of each link visible.
  • Ecology shaping institutions. Malaria and yellow fever, Mann argues, strengthened the economic case for African slavery without causing it, and produced absentee 'extractive states' where Europeans could not survive (mann-1493-ch03-no-distempers-villa-plasmodia, mann-1493-ch03-yellow-jack-war-mosquitoes, mann-1493-ch08-bad-beginnings). See disease-regimes-and-extractive-states-mann.
  • Silver as the wire of one system. Potosí's supply met Ming demand at Manila, with European turmoil, Chinese fiscal trouble, and a contested historiography over which end drove the system (mann-1493-ch01-reversals-of-fortune, mann-1493-ch04-treasure-of-the-world, mann-1493-ch04-out-of-money). See silk-for-silver-trade-mann.
  • Uniformity as fragility. Clonal potatoes, level fields, uniform pest-friendly farms and clonal rubber recur as the vulnerable side of the Homogenocene (mann-1493-ch06-potato-wars-sea-of-genes, mann-1493-ch06-war-upon-the-beetles, mann-1493-ch07-wickham-end-of-world). See agro-industrial-complex-mann.
  • Agency from below. Africans and Indians are agents, not only victims. On Mann's reading Africans controlled the supply side of the slave trade, and maroon communities often won negotiated freedom (mann-1493-ch09-in-calabar, mann-1493-ch09-africans-in-charge, mann-1493-ch09-capitulations). See maroon-societies-mann.
  • An unsettled ledger. Mann's net judgments are rare and hedged. He partly endorses Warren Dean's view that the benefits of moving species outweigh the harms, conceding that 'the balance can be closer than free-exchange advocates tend to admit' (mann-1493-ch07-wickham-end-of-world). He ends on externalities (gains diffuse, costs intense and local) and on the garden as a place of owned change (mann-1493-ch10-on-the-boat).

Where it meets the World Machines frame

Mann is a journalist-historian, not a machine theorist, and the notes flag every resonance as partial.

  • Legibility. Many notes read Mann's cases as illegible processes escaping control, or as control projects that bred ungovernable flows: the Ming sea ban, Spain's Manila cartel, the casta system (mann-1493-ch04-extra-effort-merchants-pirates, mann-1493-ch04-wooden-noses, mann-1493-ch08-family-values). See Legibility, legibility-as-machine-core and the synthesis control-breeds-its-own-counterflows-mann. Mann never uses that vocabulary.
  • Convergence and divergence. The Homogenocene is a convergence story and so a counterpoint to the divergence-machine. Mann also stresses transculturation and local remaking, and poses 'unity or division' as an open question (mann-1493-prologue, mann-1493-ch10-on-the-boat).
  • Dating. Mann's hinge dates (1493, 1571-72) are a journalist's markers, not a claim about the modernity-machine's 1200-1600 construction window (mann-1493-ch01-shiploads-of-silver). His Zaytun appendix places a 'first pass at globalization' in the fourteenth century (mann-1493-appendix-b-globalization-in-beta).
  • Distributed assembly. Outcomes assembled from crop, incentive, livestock, insect and joint-stock finance with no one intending them loosely rhyme with the history-machine (mann-1493-ch02-english-flies), though Mann's mechanisms are ecological rather than institutional.
