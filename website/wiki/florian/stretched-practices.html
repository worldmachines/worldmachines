An empty practice slot pulls old practices into performing work they cannot do

Florian · updated 14 September 2026 · source on GitHub
An empty practice slot does not sit quietly — it exerts pressure on the practices next to it, so personal time management gets stretched over a regime it cannot reach, and its failure there gets read as personal insufficiency rather than as a category error.

The idea

A vacant slot in a culture's repertoire does not stay politely empty. It exerts pressure, and the nearest available practice gets drawn into it — stretched over work it was never built for, because something has to be there and nothing else is on offer.

For Micro Time, the nearest available practice is personal time management, and the stretch is visible everywhere. People meet machine-speed systems with the tools of allocation and self-discipline: better routines, tighter boundaries, notification settings, focus modes, airplane mode, a more considered relationship to the feed. These are not silly responses. They are the best repertoire anyone currently has. But they operate at the human-facing control surface, and the consequential timing logic has moved below it. By the time a prompt is visible, the sensing, the interpretation, the comparison and the selection of an intervention window have already happened. Managing your response to the prompt is real work; it is not work on the thing that decided when the prompt would arrive.

The mechanism that makes this worse than mere ineffectiveness is what the mismatch does to attribution. A practice carries an implicit promise: do this well and the regime becomes navigable. When the practice cannot reach the regime, that promise inverts. The failure does not read as wrong tool, because the tool is the culturally sanctioned one and other people appear to be managing. It reads as insufficient discipline. The person concludes they are bad at something, rather than that they are doing something that does not connect. Systemic pressure gets translated into a personal optimization problem, and the translation is invisible precisely because the practice looks appropriate.

This is a crenellation. In England between 1194 and 1589 roughly five hundred and fifty licences to crenellate were granted, and almost none of the resulting battlements were built to survive a siege — they were a defensive vocabulary applied to buildings that would never be defended. A crenellated manor is architecturally a castle and functionally nothing like one. Personal time management applied to Micro Time has the same shape: a full, elaborate, historically genuine repertoire of temporal control, performing temporal agency at exactly the surface where temporal agency no longer lives. Read it without the history and it looks like evidence of control. Read it with the history and it becomes evidence of the opposite.

None of which makes personal time management obsolete or oppressive in itself. Against Capitalist Time it works, and it can be genuinely emancipatory — protecting concentration, holding commitments, making room for care. The claim is narrower: applied one regime down, it performs rather than functions.

Where it comes from

From "Time Moves Down the Stack" (Protocol Institute Symposium, 2026), and specifically from relocating the question my own 2014 industrial design thesis was built around. That thesis held that individuals and societies shape the temporal structures around them, so we should design them deliberately, and it worked at the human-facing control surface: clocks, devices, habits, schedules, interfaces. I still think the claim is right. I no longer think the control surface is where it applies. The licence-to-crenellate material comes from Charles Coulson's reading of the licences as passports to respectability rather than military permissions.

How settled this is

That personal time management underperforms against algorithmic and infrastructural systems is widely observed and not original to me. The crenellation reading of why — that it is a live practice performing at a dead surface rather than a weak practice — is mine, and it inherits every weakness of the diagnostic it borrows from. It also has a specific vulnerability the architectural version does not: I am the person who spent a thesis inside this practice, so reading it as ornamental is a move I have an intellectual interest in making.

The falsifier is clean enough. If disciplined personal time management reliably changes outcomes that are actually decided below the interface — not the experience of them, the outcomes — then the practice reaches further than this note claims and the stretch is not a stretch.

Connections

  • It takes connoisseurship for ornamental baggage to identify dead frameworks — the diagnostic this note applies; the first time I have run it on a practice rather than on an institution or a framework, which is either a real extension or an overreach
  • Micro Time arrived with infrastructure and no practice — the vacancy that generates the pressure described here
  • Frameworks can turn performative as they become more sophisticated — the same drift one level up: what that note claims about frameworks, this note claims about repertoires
  • The vacant slot after Marxism — Sean's account of a persisting shape in the political imagination exerting reoccupation pressure on weak candidates; structurally the identical claim, applied to political rather than temporal practice, and the better-developed version of the mechanism
  • The instrument becomes the self — Sean on what happens once a personal instrument crosses from tool to identity, which is where a stretched practice ends up when it stops working and does not stop being used

Open

Does the stretch ever produce the missing practice, or only delay it? A repertoire used hard against a regime it cannot reach might mutate into one that can — which would make stretching a stage rather than a failure. I have no case either way.
