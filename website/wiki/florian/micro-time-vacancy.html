Micro Time arrived with infrastructure and no practice

Florian · updated 14 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Machines now sense, infer and act below the threshold of human perception, and that regime arrived with full infrastructure and no human practice at all — the technical conditions hardened before any repertoire for inhabiting them was invented, and the gap is structural rather than accidental.

The idea

Micro Time is the condition in which machines sense, compare, infer, route, synchronise and act at resolutions below direct human perception and reaction. It is not a synonym for speed. Most microsecond-scale computation is mundane and politically inert. Micro Time becomes significant when machine-speed operations shape humanly meaningful possibilities before human deliberation can enter the loop at all.

The regime is fully built. Oscillators, timestamps, buffers, packet schedules, synchronisation protocols, latency budgets and ordering rules are deployed everywhere, and the relation they establish is new: the earlier regimes asked people to interpret a cycle, synchronise to a standard, or optimise an allocation, while this one senses you, infers from you, and can act before you. What has not arrived is any repertoire for inhabiting that. There is no Micro Time equivalent of ritualization, punctuality or personal time management — nothing socially learnable, nothing you can be taught, nothing you can be recognisably good at.

The asymmetry is the actual claim, and it is structural rather than a matter of timing. Infrastructure only has to be deployed; it propagates by procurement, by standards adoption, by whatever the next hardware generation happens to include. A practice has to be invented, transmitted, made common, and survive the person who taught it. Those are wildly different diffusion mechanisms operating on wildly different clocks, so the technical conditions can harden into place while interpretation, norms and agency remain immature. That is not a lag that patience fixes. It is what you should expect whenever the deployment of a temporal arrangement is cheap and the cultivation of the matching repertoire is expensive.

The vacancy is not absolute, and overstating it would be the easy error. Relevant practices already exist in scattered form — engineering, labour organising, standards work, market design, accessibility, human-computer interaction, security research, and the ordinary improvisation of people working around systems that will not accommodate them. What is missing is a recognised synthesis and a broadly shared cultural repertoire: something that can be named, taught and pointed at, rather than reinvented locally every time by whoever happens to notice the problem.

Where it comes from

From "Time Moves Down the Stack" (Protocol Institute Symposium, 2026). The condition is named against the three regimes and practices set out in the companion note. The tangible case in the talk is eye tracking in virtual reality — infrared cameras estimating gaze, foveated rendering reorganising the scene around where you look, gaze becoming an input before any click, and attention patterns becoming a behavioural inference stream. The same temporal structure, not the same ethics, appears in grid protection, high-frequency trading, congestion control, autonomous vehicle loops and active noise cancellation.

How settled this is

That machines operate at these resolutions is not in dispute, and neither is the claim that some of those operations matter politically. The contested part is the negative half: that no practice exists. An absence is hard to establish and easy to assert, and I am claiming one from inside a field where I would not necessarily see a practice forming in a domain I do not work in.

The stronger version — technical conditions systematically outrun cultural repertoire because deployment is cheaper than transmission — is mine and only lightly tested. It would weaken considerably if someone showed a historical case where a temporal regime and its practice arrived together, or where the practice arrived first. I have not gone looking hard for one, which is a gap in my own evidence rather than a sign there isn't one.

Open

Whether Micro Time is a fourth regime or the same regime the other three have always been, finally operating at a resolution that makes its structure obvious. I have written it as genuinely new, but a good argument that it is only clock time with better instruments would not obviously be wrong, and would cost this whole cluster its footing.
