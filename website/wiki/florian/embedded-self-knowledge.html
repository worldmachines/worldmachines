Divergent self knowledge requires embedded, embodied, non-reproducible inquiry

Florian · updated 14 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Humboldts field method models a workable mode of self-knowledge under divergence — embed in the matter, learn through the body in the territory, and mark gaps instead of forcing closure — offered as a direction rather than a doctrine.

The idea

Alexander von Humboldt's field studies model a mode of self-knowledge suited to a divergent age, built from three moves rather than one doctrine. First: embed in the matter itself rather than speculate about it from a distance — go to the territory instead of theorizing about the territory from an armchair. Second: learn through the body, in the actual place, in ways that can't be repeated or standardized — the knowledge is tied to the specific conditions it was produced under, not extractable into a general procedure. Third: shift registers to mark a gap honestly, rather than force a false closure when the inquiry runs out — say plainly what isn't known instead of papering over it.

This is offered as a direction, not a doctrine, and that distinction matters more than it might look. A doctrine would claim to be the correct method for self-knowledge under divergence, and claiming that is exactly the kind of confident, closed framework this whole cluster is suspicious of. A direction just says: here's where Humboldt's practice points, try moving that way, don't expect it to resolve into a formula.

This note functions as the constructive counterpart to a companion claim, elsewhere in this cluster, that the lab-science toolkit for self-knowledge — a stable baseline, isolable variables, an observer distinct from the system observed — stops fitting once those three preconditions withdraw under divergence. If the old toolkit doesn't fit anymore, what does? This note's answer: something closer to what Humboldt actually did in the field, not a new lab protocol.

That answer is held in deliberate, unresolved tension with a separate worry in this cluster: that announcing "the new method" for self-knowledge under divergence might itself be another shitty castle — a sophisticated-looking framework doing the same performative work the old ones did. The tension isn't resolved here. It's held as a branch, on purpose, rather than argued away.

Where it comes from

From Wrestling With Eels (§3), where the framing is described as "the epistemology of divergence" — my own coinage, not a term from the Humboldt literature. The Humboldt sourcing behind it is mostly secondary: footnotes 1 through 4 draw on Andrea Wulf, Douglas Helferich, and Andrew Zimmerman Jones's writing on Humboldt (through Finkelstein's editorial work), plus Emil du Bois-Reymond's Reden, volume 2, for period context.

How settled this is

The three moves themselves — embed, embody, mark gaps — are a genuine extraction from documented Humboldt practice, and I trust that extraction. What's unsettled, deliberately, is everything downstream of it: whether this actually works as self-knowledge under present conditions rather than just under Humboldt's nineteenth-century ones, and whether proposing it at all survives the performative-framework worry this cluster keeps raising against itself. I'd call this a genuine hypothesis, held with real uncertainty, not a settled method.

Open

Would learning about oneself under divergence actually work better through the opposite approach — distanced, abstract, lab-style experiment? And separately: does embedded, embodied, non-reproducible inquiry actually produce useful self-knowledge, or does it just get you stuck in localized rabbit holes with no way to check your own conclusions against anything external?
