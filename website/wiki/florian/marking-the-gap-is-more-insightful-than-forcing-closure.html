Marking the gap is more insightful than forcing closure

Florian · updated 3 June 2026 · source on GitHub

Claim

When a theory can't reach closure, marking the gap honestly — registering what is not known, including by shifting registers — is a more productive epistemic move than forcing artificial closure. It points at what might come later instead of faking completeness. (Humboldt inserting the Lebenskraft poem and letting it "stand for the gap.")

Body

Split out from Divergent self knowledge requires embedded, embodied, non-reproducible inquiry — separately falsifiable from the embed/embody elements.

Candidate answer to the open question on Frameworks can turn performative as they become more sophisticated (honest handling of the advantageous-vs-true gap). LIVE TENSION — do not reconcile — with that same probe: gap-marking can become performative humility that never commits.

Situation

What would weaken this / change my mind

  • examples where intellectual inquiry would significantly benefit from plastering over gaps in knowledge, from hiding inconsistencies with made up placeholders.
  • A „fake it till you make it“ approach in theory building, successfully deployed to deliver something that is not performative, but actually interesting and useful to others
