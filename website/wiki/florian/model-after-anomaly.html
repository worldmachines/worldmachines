Alive models are built downstream of observation, not from first principles alone

Florian · updated 14 September 2026 · source on GitHub
Working mental models get built downstream of a specific observed anomaly, not deduced upstream from context-free first principles — the model is the minimal machinery that explains what was actually seen, not a theorem derived from an axiom.

The idea

Mental models get assembled quickly, and the ones assembled without a specific observation behind them tend to turn into dead infrastructure the moment they're shared — they look like insight but don't actually grip anything. To build a model that can really interact with reality, it has to be tethered to living data drawn from observation. It can't be deduced from context-free first principles alone, however clean the deduction looks.

The clean example is the Gervais Principle, Venkatesh Rao's model of organizational behavior sorting people into Sociopaths, Clueless, and Losers. The three-tier structure wasn't deduced from a premise like "organizations are meritocracies" and then checked against reality afterward. It emerged as the minimal machinery needed to explain a pattern that already felt structurally true to a lot of people — the model is downstream of the anomaly it explains, not upstream of some abstract axiom about how organizations ought to work. That ordering is the whole claim: model after anomaly, not anomaly predicted by model.

This matters because the two directions produce genuinely different objects even when the resulting model looks the same on paper. A model built upstream from first principles is only as good as the principles, and principles that feel self-evident are exactly the ones most likely to be quietly wrong in a way nothing will catch, because there's no anomaly keeping it honest. A model built downstream from a real anomaly has a built-in check: it has to actually explain the specific thing that provoked it, or it doesn't survive contact with the person who noticed the anomaly in the first place.

The likely exception, and the reason this isn't an absolute rule: axiomatic, first-principles reasoning still produces real, useful models in domains with genuinely stable underlying structure — mathematics, parts of physics. The claim is narrower under divergence specifically: as domains stop holding still, the space where pure first-principles derivation still produces something that actually grips reality (rather than "Muskian weed chatter" — confident-sounding first-principles talk that doesn't survive contact with anything) keeps shrinking.

Where it comes from

Developed from a von Neumann line — "models, not truth" — encountered secondhand through a Rao-adjacent source relaying a 2011 Quora answer; that attribution is unverified and should be checked against a primary source before it's quoted anywhere as von Neumann's own words. The Gervais Principle material is Rao's own, and is primary. A related line, "theory as filter," from Rao's Tempo, needs its exact wording checked before quoting.

How settled this is

The Gervais Principle case is well-documented and I trust it as a real instance of the downstream-of-observation pattern. The von Neumann attribution is the weakest link in the note — secondhand, bot-relayed, unverified — and I'd treat the note's argument as resting on the Gervais case alone until that's fixed. The broader generalization (this is how alive models get built, generally) is mine, offered with the explicit domain-stability caveat above rather than as an unqualified rule.

Connections

  • Divergent self knowledge requires embedded, embodied, non-reproducible inquiry — closest relative: Humboldt's field method is this same downstream-of-observation logic applied to self-knowledge specifically
  • Frameworks can turn performative as they become more sophisticated — live tension, not agreement: "the justification is that it works" is one short step from the deferral structure that note is suspicious of. Held as a branch, not reconciled

Open

What does a genuinely useful first-principles-derived model — not Muskian weed chatter, an actual working filter on reality — look like under present, divergent conditions? I'd expect these to get rarer as domains stop holding still, but rarer isn't the same as absent, and I don't have a clean example either way yet.
